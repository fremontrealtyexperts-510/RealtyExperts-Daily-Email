Rates Ease 8 BPS To 6.67% After Three-Day Climb; ARMs Surge To 10% Of Mortgages (Highest Since Oct 2025); House Strips Forced-Sale Provision From Housing Bill
Three threads to carry into client conversations today.
First, rates eased back. The 30-year fixed dropped 8 bps to 6.67%, the first downtick after climbing every session this week (Monday 6.68, Tuesday 6.75, Wednesday 6.75, Thursday 6.67). The 15-year fell 3 bps to 6.22%. The 10-year was flat at 4.57% session-to-session, but it is still 9 bps below Monday's 4.66% reading, which is what finally gave mortgage rates the room to come down. The MBS-Treasury spread narrative from Tuesday (widening on volatility) has begun to unwind. The practical read for clients: rates were not stuck at 6.75%. They moved 8 bps in a session in their direction. The buy decision should not be waiting for cuts; it should be running the math at the rate available today.
Second, the ARM share is the leading-indicator worth flagging. Adjustable rate mortgages made up 10% of all mortgages last week, the highest share since October 2025, per Market Briefs. The 30-year fixed had reached a seven-week high before today's relief. ARMs offer lower starting rates and lower starting payments, which is why buyers reached for them when fixed hit 6.75% earlier in the week. But the payments reset later, often with no cap, when the locked period ends. For any buyer agent walking a client through financing this week, the conversation is: if the affordability gap was big enough that an ARM looked attractive at 6.75% fixed, the math probably still works on the fixed at 6.67% today, without the reset risk later. The 8-bp drop is the window.
Third, the housing-policy update. The Senate version of a new housing bill would have forced investors with 350+ units to sell homes they built within seven years. The House just cut that provision, and the bill now heads back to the Senate for a final vote. For Bay Area build-to-rent and institutional landlord positions, this is the policy datapoint to track. The legislative intent to break up institutional housing portfolios is still in motion, but the specific seven-year forced-sale mechanic just got pulled. We will know the final shape after the Senate vote.

Total active inventory across the 15-city board is 2,903, up 10 from yesterday. 185 new listings on the board this morning, down 25 from yesterday's 210 and the lowest mid-week reading we have seen. 256 closed sales for the rolling 7-day window, down 4 from 260. The composition is unmistakable: only Danville (+11), Castro Valley (+3), Newark (+1), San Ramon (+1), and Dublin (+1) added supply. Everywhere else gave back. Pleasanton (-11), Oakland (-9), Fremont (-8), and Hayward (-6) were the biggest pullbacks. The Tri-Valley supply pullback that started yesterday extended; Danville broke the pattern and added significant supply against the trend.
For listing agents: Danville is the playbook to study today. Sellers there added 11 new listings into a market where four of the surrounding cities pulled back. The 8-bp rate drop today is the cover story that justifies the timing, list now, while the rate narrative is "rates came back down" rather than the "rates climbed every day" tape from Mon-Wed. Pleasanton, Oakland, Fremont, and Hayward sellers who held back today gave the active buyers a thinner board on which to compete, which raises pressure on Danville's newly-listed sellers. The seller advantage is sharpest in cities where supply moved against today's pullback trend.
For buyer agents: 185 new listings is the thinnest mid-week board of the month. The 30-year just dropped 8 bps. ARMs jumped to 10% of all mortgages because affordability was stretched at the higher rate, but today's 6.67% fixed is the same buyer pool's better deal without the reset risk. If you have a client on the fence who was leaning ARM on Tuesday or Wednesday, today is the day to re-run the fixed-rate math. The board is thin, the rate moved their way, and the IPO and risk-on tape on Wall Street is signaling that the next leg of capital allocation is into longer-dated risk, which is generally bullish for housing demand in the back half of the year.

The 10-year settled at 4.57% Wednesday, flat session-to-session but still 9 basis points below Monday's 4.66% reading. That move is what gave mortgage rates room to ease today (the 30-year dropped 8 bps to 6.67%). The bond market is signaling the macro tape is settling. WTI Crude, by contrast, is moving the other way intraday: $101.17 (+2.96%) after closing under $100 yesterday on Iran-deal optimism. Citi still sees Brent reaching $120 by the end of this year. Gold at $4,549 (+0.31%). Rice futures separately just hit their highest level since August 2025, jumping over 16% in the last month as fertilizer prices linked to Iran tensions climb and El Nino drier conditions hit Southeast Asia. Translation: the disinflation read on bonds is fragile, energy and food are still wild cards.
The political fiscal pitch worth carrying: Jeff Bezos called for zero federal income tax on the bottom 50% of U.S. earners. That group paid only 3% of total U.S. income tax in 2023, while the top 1% pays around 40%. Whether or not it ever happens, the framing matters because consumer-affordability narratives are starting to shape policy proposals across both sides of the aisle.
The structural consumer story sits in the chart above. U.S. quarterly EV sales fell from a ~440K peak in Q3 2025 to roughly ~210K in Q1 2026, after the federal EV tax credit expired. The MB data point that goes with it: 72.1% of new EV buyers in April still traded in a gas car, up from 67.1% in January. So even as the headline sales pace cooled, the buyers who DID buy are still moving away from gas, in part because gas prices are up about 44% year-over-year after February's Iran disruption. This is the same pattern showing up everywhere right now: the buyer pool is smaller, but the conviction inside that pool is structural, not opportunistic. Carry that lens into the mortgage conversation below.

Crypto held bid Wednesday with the risk-on tape: BTC $77,960 (+0.63%), ETH $2,143 (+0.75%), XRP $1.35 (+1.38%). The headline today is a treasury-side consolidation rather than a price move: Tether bought SoftBank's full stake in Twenty One Capital (XXI +2.23%), taking control of one of the largest public Bitcoin treasuries, about 10,500 BTC. Twenty One Capital went public in December 2025 with 43,500 BTC and is being positioned by Tether as the core of a proposed three-way merger with Bitcoin finance company Strike and Bitcoin miner Elektron Energy. If that lands, the publicly traded BTC-treasury vehicle ends up significantly larger and more vertically integrated.
The regulatory story to track: Japan's financial regulators are proposing to cut the top crypto tax rate from 55% to a flat 20%, matching the stock-market rate, and would let traders carry losses forward for three years. About 105 tokens, including Bitcoin and Ethereum, would qualify. Adoption is unlikely before 2028, but it is the kind of structural rate-cut that historically pulls a meaningful slug of institutional money into the asset class. The Japan tax cut is the one to bookmark.
