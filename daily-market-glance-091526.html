Mortgage News Daily has the 30-year at 7.17% and the 15-year at 6.70%, Monday's readings and the highest of the past year. Tuesday's numbers post around 1 PM PT.
The bond market is why. The 10-Year touched 5.01% during trading Monday, its first trip to 5% since October 2023, and closed at 4.97%, its highest close since then. It was trading just above 5% again this morning, and mortgage rates follow the 10-Year.
The Fed decides Wednesday at 11 AM PT. CME FedWatch puts the odds of a quarter-point hike at 92.3%, up from 48.4% a month ago. A hike would lift the Fed's range to 3.75% to 4.00%, its first increase since 2023. The Fed does not set mortgage rates, but it sets the tone for the bonds that do.

Stocks slipped Monday, all three down half a percent or less, and they are lower again Tuesday morning as the Fed's two day meeting begins.
Banks took the hit. Bank of America fell about 5% after CEO Brian Moynihan said third quarter trading revenue should be roughly flat with last year's $5.4 billion and investment banking fees could land at $1.6 billion to $1.8 billion, down more than 10% and below the roughly $2 billion analysts expected.
Today's first chart is AI's power bill. Moody's says U.S. data centers need 45 gigawatts of new power plants by 2030, costing $110 billion. More than 30 of those gigawatts will be natural gas, most of the rest solar and battery storage, and nuclear restarts no more than 5%. Moody's estimates the buildout adds $25 billion to $30 billion a year to U.S. electricity costs, with data centers paying up to $15 billion of it directly. The graphic making the rounds splits out about 13 gigawatts of solar; Moody's gives no such figure, so our version does not either.

The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve close, gold and silver are the COMEX front month at Monday's 1:00 PM PT print measured against Friday's same time print, and Brent is the ICE front month settlement. Tuesday so far: at 8:07 AM PT gold was $4,322.30, silver $63.69, and Brent was trading near $103.14, a live tick, not a settlement.
This week is about the Fed, and the Fed is reacting to inflation. Consumer prices rose 3.4% over the past year in August. Core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 0.3% in the month, faster than July's 0.2%, and that is what pushed the hike odds up.
Today's economy charts come from a video walking through the CPI report and Wednesday's decision, and we checked every number in them against BLS and Treasury data. The long view holds: inflation is far below the 9.1% peak of June 2022, but the headline turned back up this year, from 2.4% in January to 4.2% in May and 3.4% now, while core, at 2.4%, is the lowest since March 2021.
The category chart gets two labels wrong. Its "diesel fuel, up 52%" is fuel oil, the home heating oil index; diesel sits in other motor fuel, up 44%. Its "transportation, up 2.4%" is transportation services; transportation overall, gasoline included, is up 6.2%. The rest checks out: gasoline up 27.4%, energy 16.3%, clothing 3.6%, shelter 3.0%, food 2.7% and new vehicles 0.6%.
Short term rates have already moved. The 3-month Treasury bill closed Monday at 4.11%, above the top of the Fed's 3.50% to 3.75% range and up from about 3.7% in the first five months of the year. That is the market pricing in the hike, and it means the Treasury now pays about 4.1% to roll over its bills.
Oil and metals: Brent settled at $105.68 Monday, up 1.0%, and was near $103.14 Tuesday morning. At Monday's 1:00 PM PT print gold fell 1.6% to $4,340.00 and silver 2.2% to $63.76.
A note on the newsletter's numbers: its stocks and mortgage rates check out. It had the 10-Year at 4.99% against Treasury's 4.97%, gold down 1.68% at $4,334 where the same time prints show a 1.56% drop to $4,340.00, and Brent at $106.22, up 1.54%, where the settlement was $105.68, up 1.02%. Silver was left out again.
