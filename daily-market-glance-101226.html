Consumer mood is near a record low, and buyers point to the same two things home shoppers feel: high prices and borrowing costs.

Consumers are in a sour mood. The University of Michigan's sentiment index slipped to a preliminary 46.3 in October from 48.1. If it holds, that is the second lowest reading since the survey began in 1952, behind only May's 44.8. Before this year, the low was 50.0 in June 2022.
The survey's director said buying conditions for big ticket items "plummeted amid high prices and borrowing costs," and year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.7% from 4.6%. Home buyers know the feeling: Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed is 7.48% and the 15-year 7.15%. Those are Friday's readings, since the bond market is closed for Columbus Day.
Locally, a cautious buyer shows up as patience. Across our five cities, 156 of the 611 homes on the market have been listed 60 days or longer, up from 148 on Sunday. The five city ledger reads 686, up from 684. Fremont rose to 241 and Milpitas to 101, Newark slipped to 90, and Hayward (192) and Union City (62) held. The board wide table eased to 2,661 from 2,662.
What this means for you: a gloomy survey is not a market crash, but it does thin the crowd. Buyers who are ready can find sellers more willing to talk, especially on homes that have sat 60 days or more. Sellers should price for the buyer who is actually showing up, not the one from last spring.
• Board wide: 2,661 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 158 coming soon and 222 brand new.

France now pays more to borrow than Italy. France's 10-year yield closed Friday at 4.86% against Italy's 4.60%, per CNBC data. Thursday's gap of 0.28 points was the widest in data back to 1999. At the start of 2024, Italy paid 1.12 points more than France.
France first edged above Italy in October 2025 and has stayed above since early August. Its 10-year is now the highest since 2002, and its gap over Germany (3.49%) is 1.37 points. Spain pays 4.11%. The bond market, it seems, has stopped grading on reputation.
For American borrowers the takeaway is that rates are high everywhere. The U.S. 10-Year closed Friday at 5.24%, above all four, and that is the yield mortgage rates follow.
