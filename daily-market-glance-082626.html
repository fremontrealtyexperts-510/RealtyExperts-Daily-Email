The 30 year fixed is 6.75% and the 15 year is 6.32%, each up a single basis point today, per Mortgage News Daily.
The 15 year flag comes down, and you should know why:
• What we reported: for three sessions we carried the 15 year at 6.62% and warned that it did not add up, because it left only 16 basis points between the 15 year and the 30 year where the normal gap is closer to 70.
• What happened: Mortgage News Daily has revised its own history. Its board now shows the 15 year at 6.27%, 6.30%, 6.31%, 6.32%, 6.31% and 6.32% across the last six sessions. The 31 basis point jump we flagged on Friday has been erased from the record.
• What this means: today's 6.32% is not a 30 basis point collapse in the 15 year. It is the correction of a bad print, and the 15 year has in truth been drifting quietly between 6.27% and 6.32% all week. The gap to the 30 year is back to a normal 43 basis points. The flag was right, and you can quote the 15 year again.
Two affordability stories worth carrying into a client conversation:
• Cash is losing its grip: all cash purchases fell to 31.4% of sales from 32.3% a year ago. Every point that leaves the cash column is a point where a financed buyer competes on more even footing. If you have buyers who spent last year losing to cash, the odds have moved slightly toward them.
• Renting is cheaper than buying in seven metros, and none of them is here: the first chart below is the newsletter's ranking. Austin leads it at $1,054 a month and Sacramento is the only California market on the list. Read the method before repeating it. It compares an average apartment rent against a median mortgage payment on a house, so it measures monthly cash out the door, not the same roof, and it leaves out principal paid down and any appreciation. The study is also dated July 24, so it is a month old despite arriving in this morning's inbox.
• So I ran the same question on our own market, and the answer is not close: the second chart is Fremont, Milpitas, Union City, Newark and Hayward, priced off Zillow's typical home value and typical rent for July, with a payment of 20% down on a 30 year fixed at 6.75%, principal and interest only.
Fremont's gap is $4,280 a month. That is roughly four times Austin's, and even Hayward, the smallest of our five at $1,680, beats the metro the newsletter ranked first. To keep it honest I ran Austin through my own math too and got $1,006 against their published $1,054, so the method holds. And every figure here is the conservative one, because property taxes, insurance and any HOA sit on top of every payment shown.
One caution to carry: on this like for like basis Sacramento's gap works out to about $429, not the $1,042 in the newsletter. Their figure is inflated by pricing an apartment rent against a house sized mortgage. Fremont's lead survives either way.
A second cross check, using our own MLS rather than Zillow: the median list price of today's 240 active Fremont listings is $1,260,912, which at the same terms is $6,543 of principal and interest and a $3,185 gap. So call Fremont's true range $3,200 to $4,300 a month depending on whether you price the typical home or today's median listing.
What to do with it: this is not an argument against owning, and do not let it be used as one. It is the arithmetic behind why your buyers hesitate, and the honest answer is that in this market buying is a bet on equity and staying put, not on beating rent from month one. A buyer who plans to move in three years is better off renting. A buyer who plans to stay ten is not. Saying that out loud is what earns the listing later.

A quiet, broad advance. All three indexes finished higher, with the NASDAQ doing most of the work at +0.66% while the S&P 500 and the Dow each added about a third of a percent. Nothing in the session suggested a change of trend, and the S&P is up 2.5% for the month so far.
Under the surface the moves were company specific rather than macro. Ten year yield and Brent are Tuesday's August 25 settlements. Gold and silver are Fortune spot prints at 8:55 AM and 7:15 AM Eastern today.
The ten year eased to 4.64%, six basis points below Monday. Worth flagging: this morning's newsletter printed that same 4.64% level but called the day's change 0.00%. Treasury's own daily curve shows Monday at 4.70%, so the yield fell, it did not sit still. The direction matters more than the number here, because a falling ten year is what eventually pulls mortgage rates down.
Brent settled at $88.58, down 3.89% on the day. The newsletter reported $86.23; that figure does not match the front month contract's settlement, which is the number we quote. Oil giving back nearly 4% while Iran and Oman negotiate a shared safe corridor through the Strait of Hormuz is the market pricing a little less risk into every barrel.
Gold slipped $15 to $4,615 and silver added 38 cents to $68.55. Do not let the small daily moves hide the month: gold is up 14.6% in August and silver 19.2%. That is the story the chart below tells.
