Mortgage rates rose for a second day. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year reached 7.58% on Tuesday, up 0.08 and the highest since November 2023, and the 15-year is 7.20%, the highest since October 2023. The 30-year began September at 6.89%; on our five city median of $1,079,500 with 20% down, that climb adds about $404 a month.
Tuesday's S&P Cotality Case-Shiller report is today's first chart. U.S. home prices rose 1.9% in July from a year earlier while consumer prices rose 3.4%, so prices fell after inflation for a 14th straight month. Chicago led at 6.9% and Seattle was last, down 1.6%. The version making the rounds showed six metros and none of ours, so we drew all 19.
The San Francisco metro index, which includes Alameda County, rose 3.5%, fifth of the 19 and ahead of San Diego at 1.6% and Los Angeles at 1.2%. It is one of only five metros where prices kept up with inflation. One caution: July's reading averages sales that closed May through July, when the 30-year averaged 6.63%. It is a postcard from a cheaper summer.
Owners do have a cushion. Cotality puts the average California borrower's home equity at $623,000 as of the second quarter, second only to Hawaii and about double the national $310,000. Locally, the five city ledger slipped to 691 from 703 on Tuesday and the board wide table fell 27 to 2,700.
What this means for you: the same median home costs a buyer about $404 a month more than it did on September 1, so price cuts and seller credits carry more weight; 158 of the ledger's 606 homes on the market have been listed 60 days or longer. Sellers have the equity, but a July price index will not set an October price. Price for 7.58% money.
• Board wide: 2,700 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 174 coming soon and 184 brand new.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Live ledger: 691 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City. 606 are on the market and 85 are coming soon.
• Fremont: 249 live, 192 active, 32 coming soon, 24 brand new and 1 back on market. The median on market list price is $1,199,990 at $764 a square foot, and the typical active listing has been out 29 days.
• The rest of the ledger: Hayward 179, Milpitas 110, Newark 89, Union City 64.
• Across all five cities the median is $1,079,500 at $671 a square foot. 285 homes are priced under a million, and 158 have been sitting 60 days or longer.
The San Francisco metro, which includes Alameda County, rose 3.5% in the year to July, fifth of the 19 metros reported and well ahead of the 1.9% national gain.

Levels are Tuesday, September 29 at the closing bell. Wednesday is live: at 8:08 AM PT the S&P was 7,710.31 (+0.51%), the Dow 51,384.88 (+0.07%) and the Nasdaq 27,043.48 (+0.92%).
Stocks slipped for a second day Tuesday after two soft readings on the economy. The Conference Board's consumer confidence index fell 6.7 points to 81.9 in September, and job openings dropped to 7.1 million in August from 7.3 million in July. Wednesday, the last session of the third quarter, is higher so far.
Closer to home, Netflix is having a rough year. The Los Gatos company's shares are down 25% in 2026, even after Tuesday's 1.55% gain, and HSBC and Wells Fargo both cut their ratings this month on signs that subscribers are watching less. YouTube took 14.2% of U.S. TV viewing in July against 7.8% for Netflix, per Bloomberg and The Wrap. The worry is not subscribers leaving; it is subscribers staying and watching YouTube.

All four are Tuesday, September 29: the Treasury daily yield curve close, the COMEX December gold and silver settlements, and the ICE November Brent settlement. Wednesday is live: at 8:08 AM PT the 10-Year was near 5.28%, gold $4,199.60 (+0.48%), silver $60.80 (-0.57%) and Brent $103.80 (+1.18%).
The 10-Year Treasury closed at 5.26% Tuesday, matching its June 12, 2007 close; the last higher one was in May 2002. The newsletter printed 5.23% and unchanged, while Treasury's own curve shows a gain of two basis points. It is not only here: Britain sold 10-year bonds Tuesday at 5.383%, its highest auction yield since 1999, Bloomberg reports.
Consumers see it too. In the Conference Board's September survey, 68.4% expect interest rates to be higher in a year, expected inflation rose to 6.1%, and plans to buy homes slipped. December gold settled up 0.27% and silver down 0.92%.
Brent's November contract settled down 2.56% at $102.59. A heads up for tomorrow: that contract expires today and December takes over, trading about $5 lower ($99.04 against $103.80 this morning). Tomorrow's lower number on this card will be the calendar, not a crash.
