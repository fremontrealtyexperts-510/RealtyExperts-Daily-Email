Mortgage rates are still 6.89% on the 30-year and 6.49% on the 15-year. Those are Friday's close and they have not moved since, because Mortgage News Daily does not publish on a federal holiday and Monday was Labor Day. The next real reading lands this afternoon.
There is a headline making the rounds that deserves a careful look, because it points straight at your mortgage rate. The dollar's share of global central bank reserves has fallen from 64% in 2015 to 56% in 2025. The worry writes itself: if the world stops parking money in dollars, it stops buying Treasuries, yields rise, and mortgage rates follow.
• The chart below is the actual research, from the New York Fed. Two things in it cut against the scary version. Roughly equal numbers of countries RAISED their dollar holdings as lowered them, and the entire aggregate decline traces to a handful of large holders, chiefly China and Russia, with Mexico and Morocco joining later. Among countries with complete data only Brazil and Hong Kong meaningfully cut.
• What that means in plain terms: this is a few big players rebalancing, not a world walking away from the dollar. It is not the thing that decides your rate this year. The August jobs report and the Fed's September meeting are.

If your home is in that group, the issue is almost never the market. It is the price.
Plotted on a true time axis, the recent decline is the steepest of the decade. But the same New York Fed research finds roughly equal numbers of countries raised as lowered their dollar holdings.

All four are Friday, September 4, the last completed session before the Labor Day close. The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve, which published nothing on the holiday. Gold and silver are the COMEX front month at Friday's 4:00 PM ET print, measured against Thursday's same time print. Brent is the ICE front month settlement.
China's central bank bought 650,000 troy ounces of gold in August, its biggest month in nearly three years and its 22nd consecutive month of buying, a streak that started in November 2024. Reserves are now at a record 76.73 million ounces, about 2,387 metric tons.
The chart below tells a better story than the raw headline does. China nearly stopped early this year, adding just 40,000 ounces in January and 30,000 in February. Then it bought more every single month after that, ending at 650,000 in August. That is more than a twenty fold acceleration inside eight months, and it is the sharpest signal in the data.
Why it matters beyond the metals desk: a central bank that buys gold every month for nearly two years is diversifying away from something, and gold near $4,477 an ounce reflects a lot of buyers reaching the same conclusion. It is also the same story as the dollar reserve chart in the Real Estate section above, seen from the other side.
Elsewhere, copper hit an all time high at $14,533 a ton in London, up about 47% from a year ago, as traders rushed metal into the United States ahead of possible tariffs. Copper sits in wiring, panels and plumbing, so that feeds directly into construction and renovation budgets.

One newsletter claim needs correcting outright. Market Briefs reported that Bitcoin "went up 22.4% during the week ending September 4" and called it the best week since March 2024. Bitcoin closed that week at $79,824 against $78,246 the Thursday before, which is a gain of 2.0%, not 22.4%. The decimal appears to have wandered. The related claim that Bitcoin briefly crossed $82,000 is true: it touched $82,262 on September 2.
The real news is a security incident. Hackers took about $320 million from the Liquid Network, a Bitcoin sidechain several exchanges use to move coins, by exploiting how the network mints its wrapped version of Bitcoin. The network paused transactions in response. If you hold coins on an exchange that routes through it, expect slower transfers.
