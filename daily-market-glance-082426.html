The 15 Year Anomaly Did Not Correct: Mortgage News Daily Prints 6.62% While Freddie Mac Says 5.95%; Fremont Opens The Week With 248 Active Listings
30-YEAR FIXED
6.78%
15-YEAR FIXED
6.62%
The 30 year fixed is 6.78% this morning, up 1 basis point, per Mortgage News Daily. That number cross checks cleanly. The Mortgage Bankers Association has the 30 year at 6.77% and Freddie Mac's weekly survey has it at 6.65%, both in a believable relationship to a daily index.
The 15 year does not cross check, and this is the second session in a row we are flagging it:
• What Mortgage News Daily shows: 6.62%, up 1 basis point from Friday's 6.61%. Friday's print was a 31 basis point jump in a single session while the 30 year moved 1 basis point, and it has not reversed.
• Why that is hard to believe: it leaves only 16 basis points between the 30 year and the 15 year on their own board. Freddie Mac's survey, published last Thursday, has the 15 year at 5.95%, and the Mortgage Bankers Association has it at 6.08%. Both of those put the normal gap near 70 basis points.
• What to do with it: we publish Mortgage News Daily because it is our source of record and it is a daily index rather than a weekly survey. Do not quote 6.62% to a client without a live lender quote in hand. If it is a feed problem it will correct, and if it is real your lender confirms it in a minute.
Two housing stories in this morning's newsletter are worth an agent's attention:
• Builders cannot staff the jobs: The skilled trades are short roughly 300,000 workers, and data center construction is now pulling crews off home building. Less new supply means the resale inventory we count every morning keeps carrying more of the demand.
• Young buyers are picking the market over a mortgage: Gen Z retirement contributions are up 65% year over year, and the reason given is that owning feels out of reach. That is a first time buyer pipeline problem. It is the conversation to have with the 25 to 32 year olds in your database now, not in three years.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)

• Fremont: 248 active across all property types, made up of 35 townhomes, 87 condos and 126 detached homes, plus 102 in contract, 43 coming soon and 21 new today.
• Five city live ledger: 703 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City.

• Fremont detail: 102 Fremont listings are in contract against 248 active, so roughly three in ten of the city's current supply is already spoken for. That is a seller's market by any normal reading, and it has not loosened while rates sat near 6.8%.

Ten year yield is a mid session quote at 10:00 AM Pacific against Friday's 4.74% Treasury close. Gold and silver are Fortune spot prints at about 9:10 AM Eastern today. Brent is the August 21 settlement, the last one the front month futures contract has completed.
Start with the oil story, because the newsletter's version of it does not survive a look at the data. It reported that U.S. refinery output has been falling. The Energy Information Administration's weekly numbers say the opposite. Refiners ran 17.40 million barrels a day in the week ending August 14, the highest week of this year, above the 17.21 million of the same week last year and well clear of the 16.59 million five year average. Utilization is averaging 93.1% so far in 2026 against 90.0% last year. The Canadian maintenance the newsletter describes is real and worth watching, because Midwest refineries take roughly 70% of their crude from Canada, but 300,000 barrels a day of it has not shown up in national runs yet.
A note on the chart. We could not recreate the world refining graphic that came with the newsletter. It credits Energy Aspects, a private consultancy whose global series is not published anywhere we can check, and its levels sit well above the range normally quoted for global refinery throughput. Rather than pass along numbers we cannot verify, the chart below is the U.S. picture built from the EIA's own weekly series.
Brent settled at $94.39 on Friday, up 0.65%. The newsletter had that one exactly right, which is worth saying on the days it happens.
Gold ran to $4,674 an ounce this morning, up 1.90% from Friday's $4,587. Silver eased to $69.18, down 0.62%. One caution about the gold source: Fortune's article says today's price is a $604 gain from Friday, which would put Friday at $4,070. Fortune's own Friday article said $4,587, which is the number we published. The $87 move is right, the comparison line in their story is not.
The ten year Treasury is quoted at 4.69% mid session, five basis points below Friday's 4.74% close. The newsletter printed 4.73% and called it a three basis point rise. Treasury's own daily curve has Friday at 4.74%, up five. Its ten year has now missed the official close on most sessions this month, so treat that line as a rough guide and pull the curve when the number matters.
Ray Dalio's debt warning is the other headline. Federal spending is running about 40% ahead of revenue, and his fix of spending cuts, higher taxes and lower rates all at once is not a package that passes before a crisis. The part that reaches housing is the last item. Mortgage pricing tracks the long end of the curve, and a debt scare pushes that end the wrong way.
