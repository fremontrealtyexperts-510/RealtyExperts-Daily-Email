REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.33% +0.03  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.98% +0.02

A new order goes after mortgage insurance. An executive order signed Oct. 22 directs the Federal Housing Finance Agency, which oversees Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, to pilot canceling a borrower's mortgage insurance once the home's current market value shows enough equity. It also expands the agency's alternative to lender's title insurance on refinances, and tells USDA and VA to cut rates on the home loans they make directly.

What that could mean here. Put 10% down on our five city median of $1,079,000 and you borrow $971,100, a high balance conforming loan in our area. You reach 20% equity once the home is worth about $1,213,900, a 12.5% rise before any principal is paid down. Today, getting that insurance dropped on a higher value usually means asking your servicer and paying for an appraisal, and the order asks FHFA to cut those costs. Nothing changes until FHFA writes the rules.

Rates and the Fed. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average stands at 7.33% and the 15-year at 6.98% from Friday, with Monday's reading due later today. The Fed meets Tuesday and Wednesday.

Neighbors versus data centers. S&P Global Ratings counts 146 U.S. data center projects delayed or canceled, about three quarters of them tied to local pushback, and local governments in 40 states have imposed bans. Energy costs, water, air pollution and noise top the complaints, which will sound familiar to anyone who has sat through a planning commission meeting.

What it means for you. If you are buying with less than 20% down, ask your lender how the mortgage insurance comes off, because under the pilot your home's value, not just your payments, could get you there. If you own and are weighing a refinance, ask whether the title waiver could apply to your loan. If you are selling, today's buyer is still pricing a 7.33% payment, so price for that from day one.
