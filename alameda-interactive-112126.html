REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.33% -0.12  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.89% -0.10

Rents turn up nationally, and faster here. Realtor.com's October rental report shows the median asking rent for 0 to 2 bedroom units in the 50 largest metros at $1,692, up 0.1% from a year ago, the first annual increase since June 2023. Zillow's rent index has Milpitas up 9.4%, Union City 7.1%, Fremont 6.8%, Newark 5.5% and Hayward 3.9%, against 2.9% nationally on the same measure.

More homes, fewer signed contracts. In realtor.com's latest weekly data, homes for sale are up 8.3% from a year ago, new listings are down 10.3%, and the median list price is down 1.2%, the 43rd straight week below last year. Here, supply is up 3% from this date last year across the 14 cities in both of our exports (1,710 from 1,659), while homes in contract are down 29% (567 from 803).

New homes are giving ground. Realtor.com finds builders holding their prices nationally while existing home prices slip. Here it runs the other way: the board's median new listing (built 2025 or 2026) asks $1,249,000, down from $1,382,750 for the newest homes a year ago, while resale rose to $899,694 from $825,000. Part of that is mix, since many new homes are townhomes.

Rates eased with the selloff. Friday's stock slide pulled mortgage rates down with Treasury yields. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average fell 0.12 to 7.33%, with the 15-year at 6.89%, and Freddie Mac's weekly survey on Thursday was 7.28%.

What it means for you. If you rent, local rents are rising faster than the national headline, so price out a purchase before your lease renews. If you are buying, rates just eased and fewer people are signing contracts, so there is room to negotiate.

Today's count. Board wide: 2,262 listings across the 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, down from 2,313 Thursday, with 125 coming soon and 102 brand new. The five city ledger carries 565 live listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, 501 on the market and 64 coming soon, down from 589.

Fremont holds 194 live listings: 159 active, 24 coming soon and 10 brand new, with a median on market list price of $1,242,321 at $769 a square foot and a typical listing out 40 days; with 20% down at 7.33%, that median runs about $6,834 a month in principal and interest. Hayward carries 161, Milpitas 88, Newark 69 and Union City 53. Across all five cities the median is $1,000,000 at $667 a square foot, 250 homes are priced under a million, and 179 have been sitting 60 days or longer.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 6,910.39 -3.70%  |  Dow: 48,147.33 -1.93%  |  Nasdaq: 22,617.73 -5.28%

The worst week since 2020. Friday's close: the S&P 500 fell 3.70%, the Dow 1.93% and the Nasdaq 5.28%, after Thursday's drop of about 2% across the board. For the week the Nasdaq lost 10.0% and the Dow 8.0%, both their worst weeks since March 2020, and the S&P 500 lost 8.6%, its worst since April 2025.

In correction territory. The S&P 500 is now 11.6% below its October 6 record and the Dow 11.4% below its August 5 record, both in correction territory, and the Nasdaq is 18.1% off its high and below where it started the year. The VIX fear gauge closed at 35.89, its highest since April 2025.

Chips led the fall. Santa Clara's Nvidia dropped 14.01% to $125.53 on reports that SEC staff are examining its accounting, which neither the SEC nor Nvidia has confirmed; it lost 22.9% on the week. AMD fell 13.25% and Broadcom 12.19%. Anthropic, which priced at $83, closed its first day Thursday at $97.17 and fell 8.50% Friday to $88.91, still above the offer price.
