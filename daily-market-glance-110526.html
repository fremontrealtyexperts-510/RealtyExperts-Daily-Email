PROP. 37 UPDATE

Prop. 37 is still projected to pass.

The count grew overnight and the margin held. The Secretary of State's semi-official tally as of 7:20 PM PT Wednesday has Prop. 37, the loan program for middle income buyers of newly built homes, at 61.2% Yes (4,783,474 votes) to 38.8% No (3,036,774), about 268,000 more ballots than Wednesday morning. Alameda County is at 67.5% Yes.
Mail ballots postmarked by Election Day still count if they arrive within seven days, and the state certifies the result by Dec. 11.

Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.22% this morning, unchanged, and the 15-year is 6.86%, up 0.01. Rates kept easing after our Wednesday report went out: the 30-year finished Wednesday at 7.22%, not the 7.25% we printed that morning. At 7.22%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,299,444 median with 20% down would pay about $7,070 a month in principal and interest, $57 less than at Tuesday's 7.30%.
Zillow said the same thing from the other side of the table. Its residential revenue, mostly fees from real estate agents, fell 4% from a year ago to $419 million, which it blamed on higher than expected mortgage rates slowing sales, while rentals grew 26%. Most of its $158 million loss was a $165 million settlement of a shareholder lawsuit over Zillow Offers, its old home buying business. The stock rose 9.43% Wednesday before the report and is down 9.0% this morning.
The board wide table is 2,443, from 2,438 Wednesday, with 149 coming soon and 129 brand new. The five city ledger is 634, from 642: Fremont (214) fell four, Milpitas (97) three, Hayward (181) and Union City (60) one each, and Newark (82) added one.
What this means for you: stocks are falling and the 10-Year is easing this morning, and that tends to reach lender rate sheets within a day. If you are close to locking, ask your lender to watch it with you rather than guess. Sellers, 171 homes in our five cities have been listed 60 days or longer; the market is rewarding the right price, not the hopeful one.
