All four are Thursday, September 10. The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve close, gold and silver are the COMEX front month at Thursday's 1:00 PM PT print measured against Wednesday's same time print, and Brent is the ICE front month settlement. Friday so far: at 8:52 AM PT gold was $4,413.70, silver $65.02, and Brent was trading near $104.74, a live tick, not a settlement.
Wholesale prices are the story. The Producer Price Index rose 0.4% in August and 5.4% over the past year. Energy drove the month: final demand energy rose 4.2%, diesel jumped 24.1%, and BLS says energy accounted for over three fourths of the rise in goods prices.
Today's chart is the second one making the rounds, and its numbers are right. The headline, energy did the heavy lifting, is true of August. It is less true of the year: take out food, energy and trade and producer prices are still up 4.7%, and services are up 4.5%, both more than twice the Fed's 2% goal. Our version adds services and the month's detail so both halves show. The Fed's goal is for consumer prices, so read it as a yardstick.
That is why markets moved toward a hike, not a cut. The Fed meets next Wednesday, and CME FedWatch puts the odds of a quarter-point increase at 85.6%, up from 48.4% on August 11. The 10-Year jumped to 4.95%, its highest close since at least January 2024.
Oil kept climbing. Brent settled at $107.63 on Thursday, up 6.3% and its highest settlement since May 19, as Iran said it was ready for a wider conflict after the U.S. destroyed five of its tankers. It eased to about $104.74 Friday morning. Metals went the other way: at Thursday's 1:00 PM PT print gold fell 2.0% to $4,358.50 and silver 5.6% to $64.09, and both were bouncing Friday morning.
A note on the newsletter's numbers: its stock, rate and PPI figures check out, including diesel's 24% jump. It had the 10-Year at 4.96% against Treasury's 4.95%, gold down 2.29% where the same time prints show 1.99%, and Brent at $108.63, up 7.28%, against the $107.63 settlement, up 6.34%. Silver was omitted again.
Energy led August, rising 4.2% in the month with diesel up 24.1%. Over the year, producer prices excluding food, energy and trade are still up 4.7%, more than twice the Fed's 2% goal.
