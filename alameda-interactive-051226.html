Rates Pop 10 BPs On Iran And ECB Hawks; April Existing Sales Stall At +0.2%; Blackstone Funds 50K Builder Homes

Three real-estate stories worth weaving together for client conversations this week. First, mortgage rates jumped sharply overnight. The 30-year sits at 6.52% (+10 bps) today, the 15-year at 6.03% (+4 bps). Yesterday’s rates were 6.42% and 5.99%. A 10-basis-point jump is the largest one-day move in weeks. The driver is global: Iran-driven oil at $102 plus the ECB now signaling rate HIKES equals a sudden repricing of duration risk worldwide. For a $1.2M Fremont purchase at 20% down, the monthly payment difference between 6.42% and 6.52% is roughly $63 a month. Not huge in absolute terms, but a real qualification shift for buyers near the DTI line.

Second, April existing home sales barely moved (+0.2%) versus consensus of +3%. The median sale price hit a fresh April record at $417,700. The catch: first-time buyers held just 33% of sales. The price ceiling plus the new rate jump is going to tighten the first-time buyer pool further. Sellers pricing for “the buyer who’s been qualified for months” need to be ready to negotiate.

Third, Blackstone is launching a new lender to fund 50,000 new homes a year. The firm’s real-estate debt arm will lend directly to builders. The U.S. is now building fewer homes annually than in 1960, despite a population that has nearly doubled. The new-construction segment was already pricing aggressively (last week we covered builder discounts taking new-home prices to a five-year low). Blackstone funding the pipeline is a structural shift, not a cyclical one.

WTI Adds Another +3.96% To $101.95; ECB Now Expected To HIKE Twice; Gas Splits Restaurant Industry

The 10-Year stepped up to 4.41% (+0.04%), gold ticked higher to $4,745 (+0.31%), and WTI ripped another +3.96% to $101.95, the second straight session of major energy moves. Brent at $104.63 (+3.30%). The Iran war premium is now built into the curve.

The big macro shift is across the Atlantic. European economists now expect the ECB to HIKE rates twice this year, reversing the prior one-cut outlook. Iran-driven oil prices pushed euro-area inflation forecasts to 2.7% for 2026. For U.S. investors, higher rates in Europe means higher rates for longer at home, which is exactly the tape mortgages are now reading.

Gas at $4.50 is splitting the restaurant industry in two. Fast food (Burger King, McDonald’s MCD -0.42%) saw sales rise last quarter; casual dining is collapsing. Applebee’s parent Dine Brands (DIN +2.43%) says diners pull back once gas tops $3.50, and 43% of drivers have already cut back on dining out. Trump is reportedly considering temporarily suspending the federal gas tax, a discretionary-budget release valve if it lands.

BTC Holds $81K; Crypto Fund Inflows Hit $857M (7x Prior Week); Circle Drops First Public-Co ICO For $222M

Bitcoin held the $81,000 line at $81,190 (-1.21%), ETH at $2,345 (-1.00%), XRP at $1.45 (-1.27%). The story is in flows and regulation, not price.

Crypto fund inflows hit $857M last week, more than seven times the prior week, with Bitcoin scooping $706M of that. The catalyst was a Senate compromise on the CLARITY Act and stablecoin rules, the first real clarity on U.S. crypto regulation in years. The institutional money that has been waiting for a regulatory floor now has one.

Separately, Circle (CRCL +15.91%) raised $222M in the first ICO by a U.S. public company, a new template that other public companies will watch closely. The line between traditional public equity issuance and token sales just got blurry in a structural way.
