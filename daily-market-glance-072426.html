The daily 30-year fixed ticked up to 6.85%, its highest in a year, and the 15-year rose to 6.29%. Oil crossing $100 is not helping the inflation picture that drives these rates.
There is a supply story worth watching. More than 300,000 vacant lots are sitting on Zillow right now, and experts call that good news. Those empty lots could become the homes America is short on. The catch is that most are in rural areas where land is cheap but water, roads, and power lines are missing, so turning them into housing is a long project, not a quick fix.
Also notable: Florida's economy just grew big enough to beat most countries, with GDP hitting $1.8 trillion, now larger than Australia or Mexico, after twenty years of cutting taxes and courting new residents. For investors that means opportunity, but also higher costs in the popular spots. Sound familiar to anyone who has watched the Bay Area price curve.

Stocks fell for a second straight day. The NASDAQ dropped 2.15% to 25,137, the S&P 500 gave back 1.21% to 7,408, and the DOW slipped 0.97% to 51,711. Investors are bracing for the Middle East conflict to drag on, and oil posted its biggest jump in more than two months.
One bright spot: Intel just posted its best quarterly growth in over a decade. Revenue hit $16.1 billion, a 25% jump that blew past every estimate, and shares rose 11% after hours. The company says AI demand is fueling a chip boom, and it locked up 10 long-term agreements for its AI memory chips, going head to head with Micron and SanDisk.
AI money kept flowing even as the market wobbled. Investors poured into AI focused ETFs, partly for a tax reason: many traditional mutual funds are down yet still throwing off taxable distributions, while the AI ETFs are gaining and taxed differently. And in the department of things we did not have on the bingo card, Nvidia is sending a GPU to the moon aboard a 2026 rover, the first ever to run on the lunar surface.

Gold and silver are live spot quotes taken the morning of July 24 (change versus the same time a day earlier); the 10-Year, Brent, stocks, and crypto are as of the July 23 close. Metals can move fast intraday, so treat these as a morning snapshot.
The layoff list keeps growing. Disney announced another round of job cuts, including at least 116 Pixar staff, even after Toy Story 5 and Inside Out 2 shattered box office records. That is the company's third round this year. Zoom out and 2026 has been a heavy year for announced cuts across some of the biggest names in business (chart below), led by Volkswagen at 50,000, with UPS and Oracle at 30,000 each, Amazon at 16,000, and Dell at 11,000.
Shoppers are feeling it too. Albertsons made less money last quarter and pointed straight at customers buying less as high food and gas prices eat into household budgets. The grocer cut its guidance for the rest of the year.
Oil crossed a line. Brent crude broke $100 a barrel for the first time since May as the U.S. and Iran conflict drags on, and the Senate voted down a resolution to end military action. Higher crude works its way into everything from gas to groceries. The 10-Year Treasury held at 4.70%.
The metals bounced back. Gold edged up 0.29% to $4,058 and silver jumped about 2.0% to $58.98, recovering some of yesterday's pullback as investors looked for cover.
Why a REALTOR® cares about layoffs: job security is the quiet variable behind every pre-approval. A buyer who feels shaky about their employer waits, and a household that just lost an income becomes a seller on a timeline. Watching where the cuts land tells you which move-up buyers are about to pause.

Crypto was quiet. Bitcoin was essentially flat at $65,073, Ether eased 0.20% to $1,873, and XRP slipped 0.16% to $1.11.
The interesting news was defensive. Nine big crypto and finance firms, including Strategy and BlackRock, are teaming up to protect Bitcoin from future quantum computers, pledging $15 million over three years for quantum resistant cryptography research. The worry is that a quantum machine could one day crack the blockchain and expose trillions in crypto.
Meanwhile the exchange that pioneered 100x Bitcoin leverage is closing for good. BitMEX let traders borrow against their cash at eye watering multiples, and it lost the race to legal trouble and newer rivals like Binance and Hyperliquid. Turns out 100 to 1 leverage was not a durable business model.
