Rates backed off their 52 week highs after a soft jobs number, the first daily decline in a week

Both rates fell today. The 30 year is at 6.88%, down three basis points, and the 15 year at 6.48%, down two, per Mortgage News Daily. That is the first daily decline in a week.
Read it in context, because the level still is not friendly. Wednesday's close set fresh 52 week highs at 6.91% and 6.50%, and MND's own headline yesterday afternoon was that rates were approaching 7%. Today gives back three basis points of that. The cause is the soft ADP number in the Economy section: weak hiring means a better chance of Fed cuts, money moves into bonds, yields fall, mortgages follow.
Do not over read one day. This is a three basis point move off a one year high, driven by a private payroll survey. The government's own jobs report lands Friday. If it comes in near the 53,000 consensus, today holds. If it surprises upward, this gets given back quickly.
What it costs, concretely: on a $450,000 purchase with 20% down, the payment is about $2,366 a month, roughly $7 below Wednesday's peak and about $209 above February.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Board wide: 2,642 listings across the 15 city board, counting everything active alongside everything in contract, with 196 coming soon and 154 brand new today. That is 7 fewer than yesterday.
• Fremont: 231 active across all property types, made up of 32 townhomes, 78 condos and 121 detached homes, duplexes and half plexes, plus 100 in contract, 38 coming soon and 16 new today.
• Five city live ledger: 686 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, down 9 from yesterday. Fremont 260, Hayward 184, Milpitas 114, Newark 75, Union City 53.
• The read: this is the quietest day for new listings in a while. Sixteen new in Fremont against 26 yesterday, and 154 board wide against 192. Meanwhile Fremont's in contract count rose from 98 to 100. Fewer homes arriving, slightly more going under contract. That tightens things at the margin.
What to tell a client this week: rates just came off a one year high because of one soft jobs survey, and Friday's government report will either confirm it or erase it. If you are floating, this is a conversation to have with your lender today, not Monday. If you are listing, the new supply arriving this week is thin, which means less competition for the next two weeks than the last two.

A green day across all three indexes, and the two stories worth your time are both about what a number actually means.
Broadcom told investors its AI revenue should roughly double twice, to $115 billion in fiscal 2027 and $230 billion in fiscal 2028, after guiding fiscal 2026 up to $58 billion. Last quarter's AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7 billion, up 221% from a year ago and 56% of everything the company sold. Management said demand already exceeds the 2027 target and that supply, not orders, is the limit. The stock fell 0.66% anyway.
Uber is cutting 10% of its roughly 34,000 staff and 20% of its management layers, saying too many handoffs are slowing decisions. Investors approved. It is also one more tech employer shrinking in a week when the labor data went soft, which is the thread running through today's Economy section.
The first chart below is the Broadcom roadmap with the forecasts drawn as forecasts. The second is Palo Alto Networks, which is a lesson in how a true headline can still point the wrong way.
One bar is a result and three are company targets, so the forecasts are hatched. The FY2028 AI target alone is about 3.6 times Broadcom's entire FY2025 revenue of $64 billion.

Ten year yield and Brent are Wednesday's September 2 settlements. Gold and silver are live spot quotes at about 11:15 AM Pacific on September 3, with the change measured from Wednesday's settlement.
One number set the tone for everything else today. ADP said private employers added just 38,000 jobs in August, against roughly 47,000 expected. Hiring came from schools and hospitals, restaurants and hotels, and construction. Factories and business services shed workers.
Everything on this line is the market repricing rate cuts off that miss. Gold is up 3.9% and silver up 4.7% this morning, both large single day moves. The ten year Treasury settled Wednesday at 4.79%, unchanged from Tuesday, but the live yield has dropped to about 4.75% since the ADP print landed. Mortgage rates eased with it, which is the Real Estate section below.
Two corrections against the source before publishing.
• Ten year Treasury: the newsletter printed 4.77%. Treasury's own daily yield curve has Wednesday closing at 4.79%, the same as Tuesday. Their direction, flat, was right. Their level was not.
• Brent: the newsletter printed $95.50 and called it a 0.14% decline. ICE front month futures settled Wednesday at $95.63, up 1.04%. Wrong level and wrong direction, which is the third day running Brent has come in wrong from that source.
• Silver was left off again, as it is most days. It is on the card.
The government's own payroll report lands Friday, with consensus near 53,000 jobs and 4.1% unemployment. That is the number that will actually move mortgage rates next.
The chart below is the ADP year, rebuilt, because the version going around this morning was not telling the truth about three of its eight months.
Hiring has cooled for four straight months, from 122,000 in May to 38,000 in August. January's 11,000 was still the weakest month of the year, so 2026 is a hump rather than a steady slide.
Source: ADP National Employment Report via FRED, current vintage. The graphic circulating this morning carried ADP's original February, April and June prints of 63K, 109K and 98K, since revised to 66K, 105K and 95K.
