Mortgage rates start the week where Friday left them. Mortgage News Daily has the 30-year at 7.43% and the 15-year at 7.10% as of September 25, with Monday's reading due this afternoon. The 10-Year is not helping: it was near 5.26% at 7:54 AM PT, above Thursday's 5.18% close, which was already the highest since 2007.
The newsletter's housing story is a California one. Kenneth Klein, a law professor at California Western School of Law, obtained state Department of Insurance data on 74,070 fire claims on owner-occupied homes from 2018 to 2023. Of the homes destroyed, 71.3% were underinsured, by about 19% on average.
Wildfire made it worse but did not create it: catastrophe losses came up short 78% of the time, ordinary house fires 68%. The weak link was the insurer's own rebuild estimate at the point of sale, which ran below the final loss 75% to 95% of the time. The newsletter wrote that only about 90% of homes have insurance; in the study, 90% is the share of policies that carried extended or guaranteed replacement cost.
Locally the weekend was quiet. Fremont's 257 live listings did not change between Sunday's and Monday's MLS exports, and the five city ledger stands at 717, up from 714 on Friday.
What this means for you: ask your insurer for a fresh replacement cost estimate rather than trusting the one written with the policy, and check whether extended replacement cost is on it. Buyers should line up quotes before contingencies come off; sellers who have a recent estimate can hand it to buyers as one less worry.

The newsletter stamped its gold and 10-Year prints 4:00 PM Eastern on Sunday, when neither market trades; they were overnight quotes.
Fun is getting pricier, and people are paying anyway. Sales at sporting goods, hobby, music and book stores rose 10.7% in the year to August, against 6.0% for all retail, per the Census Bureau, and Bank of America says hobby spending on its cards rose 7.9%. Airline fares are up 23.4%, not the 26.5% making the rounds, which was June's reading.
Closer to home, recreation prices in the San Francisco, Oakland and Hayward area rose 7.5% over the year to August, more than twice the area's 3.4% overall inflation and nearly three times the national 2.7%. Funflation, it turns out, has a Bay Area premium.
Hobby stores and hobby card spending are growing faster than retail overall, and Bay Area recreation prices rose 7.5%, nearly three times the national rate. Airfares are August's 23.4%.
