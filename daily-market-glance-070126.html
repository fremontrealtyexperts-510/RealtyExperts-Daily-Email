Mortgage Rates Hold At 6.54% As Home Prices Set Another Record On Near-Flat Growth; Digital Realty Buys Out Blackstone's Data Center Stake For $3.5B; The East Bay Board Eases To 2,805 Active On 177 New And 206 Closed
The 30-year fixed held at 6.54% and the 15-year stayed at 6.12%. Rates have gone quiet, and so has the resale market, with the lock-in effect still keeping owners in place until the gap to their existing rates narrows.
U.S. home prices set another record high in May, but annual growth was just 0.7%, the ninth straight month under 1%. High rates and thin supply have the market in a standoff: prices are not falling, but they are barely rising either, which leaves buyers and sellers staring at each other with no clear direction.
On the commercial side, Digital Realty (DLR -5.79%) agreed to buy Blackstone's stake in three Virginia data centers for $3.5 billion. The market flinched because the deal leans on $2.3 billion in new stock, diluting existing shareholders, a reminder that even the hottest corner of real estate has a price.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,805 active (down 96 from yesterday), with 177 new listings against 206 closings. Oakland again led the board with 36 new on 744 active; Fremont added 24 new on 339 active.

Wall Street just closed the books on its best quarter in six years. The S&P 500 climbed 0.79% to 7,499, the NASDAQ jumped 1.52% to 26,213, and the DOW added 0.26% to 52,319 (figures as of the June 30th close). Over the past three months the S&P added roughly $8 trillion in value.
Chips did the heavy lifting. Semiconductor stocks gained about $2 trillion in combined value in the second quarter, their best three months ever, led by names like AMD (+7.68%) and Intel (+6.01%), though the group cooled 7.9% in the final week as investors questioned whether AI spending can keep pace with the buildout. Nike (-1.04%) topped earnings estimates, but a one-time $986 million tariff refund did most of the work after the Supreme Court struck down the original tariff plan.
