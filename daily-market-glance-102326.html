Mortgage rates eased for the first time since August, after September's climb cut pending home sales 7.3%.
30-YEAR FIXED

Rates finally blinked. Freddie Mac's weekly 30-year average fell to 7.32% Thursday from 7.40%, its first weekly decline since Aug. 20. Mortgage News Daily's daily average dropped to 7.30% Thursday afternoon after the bond rally, below the 7.36% morning reading in yesterday's report, and sits at 7.33% today, with the 15-year at 6.98%.
The newsletter's chart had rates still climbing at 7.40%; that was the Oct. 8 and Oct. 15 reading. For the year, Freddie's average has run from 6.15% at the end of 2025 to a 5.98% low in late February and the 7.40% peak this month. A year ago it was 6.19%.
Buyers felt the climb in September. Pending home sales, contracts signed but not yet closed, fell 7.3% from August and 11.3% from a year earlier, NAR said Tuesday, and the West fell the most. NAR's Lawrence Yun said "mortgage rates could be topping out."
The biggest landlords feel it too. Blackstone's distributable earnings fell 9.2% in the third quarter, and its opportunistic real estate funds lost 3.2% of their value. The stock still rose 8.38% Thursday; Market Briefs had it falling 8.38%.
Locally, inventory ticked up. The board wide table rose to 2,636 from 2,631, with 132 coming soon and 174 brand new. The five city ledger rose to 684 from 679: Fremont to 235 from 231, Newark to 92 from 89 and Union City to 68 from 65, while Hayward slipped to 194 from 197 and Milpitas to 95 from 97.
What this means for you: the rate climb has already cost real money. On our five city median of $1,080,000 with 20% down, principal and interest runs about $5,935 a month at Freddie's 7.32%, against $5,169 at February's 5.98%, or $766 a month more. Every quarter point moves it by about $146. If you are buying, a week of easing is a good time to get a fresh quote; if you are selling, price for today's payment, not February's.

Levels are Thursday, October 22: the 10-Year is Treasury's close, gold and silver are COMEX December settlements, and Brent is the ICE December settlement.
Young workers are losing the best paid entry jobs. In Britain, payroll jobs held by 18 to 24 year olds in information and communication, the government's tech category, fell 30.0% from December 2022 to December 2025, and finance jobs fell 9.7%, according to HMRC's payroll tables, the data behind a Boston Consulting Group analysis Bloomberg reported Thursday. All jobs for that age group were flat, down 0.2%.
The Bay Area is sharper. In Alameda, Contra Costa, San Francisco, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties, information sector jobs held by 19 to 24 year olds fell 44.6% from fall 2022 to fall 2025, to 7,353 from 13,281, Census Bureau data show. Finance jobs for that age group fell 16.1%, while all jobs for young workers slipped just 2.6%. Most of the drop came in 2023 and 2024. For housing, these are the jobs that turn young renters into first-time buyers, so a thinner pipeline now can mean fewer of them later.
Bonds held their rally. The 10-Year closed Thursday at 4.96%, down 0.17 point and its lowest close since Sept. 22, and traded near 4.99% at 10:39 AM PT. Brent settled up 4.00% at $113.17 and traded near $116 this morning, its highest since late April, while gold and silver fell for a second day.
A correction: Market Briefs had the 10-Year up 0.01 point Thursday; Treasury shows it down 0.17 to 4.96%. It also had gold down 0.72% at $4,031 and Brent up 0.56%; the settlements were gold down 2.16% at $4,060.40 and Brent up 4.00%. Oil and bonds rallied together for a second day, which is how you know nobody agrees on what comes next.
In Britain, tech jobs for 18 to 24 year olds fell 30% in three years while young people's jobs overall held flat.
Source: HM Revenue and Customs payrolled employments by industry and age, December 2022 to December 2025, the data behind the Boston Consulting Group analysis Bloomberg reported.
