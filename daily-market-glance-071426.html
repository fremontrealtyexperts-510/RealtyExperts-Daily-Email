Mortgage Rates Tick Up To 6.75% As Realtor.com Trims Its 2026 Price Outlook To Just 1.2%; The East Bay Board Eases To 2,759 Active On 235 New And 191 Closed
The 30-year fixed ticked up to 6.75% and the 15-year to 6.21%, a touch higher on the week. Rates back near 6.75% keep the lock-in effect firmly in place, so most owners stay put and resale supply stays tight.
The forecast headline: Realtor.com trimmed its outlook and now expects U.S. home prices to rise just 1.2% over the next year. With high mortgage rates still squeezing buyers, sellers are dialing back expectations, which can hand buyers more room to negotiate even as it slows equity growth for current owners. It fits the national picture of a market cooling at the edges without breaking.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,759 active, down from 2,814, on 235 new listings and 191 closings. Oakland again led by a wide margin with 65 new on 719 active, followed by Fremont at 30 and Danville at 29.

Stocks pulled back to start the week, with all three major indexes in the red. The NASDAQ led the decline, off 1.55% to 25,873, the S&P 500 slipped 0.79% to 7,515, and the DOW eased 0.26% to 52,498.
The week's marquee event is earnings. Traders are loading up on options in Netflix (NFLX +0.63%) ahead of its report later this week, hoping the streamer bounces back after a recent yearly low, and the big banks begin reporting today.
AI spending kept rolling despite the jitters. Intel (INTC -6.12%) plans to spend $5.6 billion on a new chip plant in Ireland, and Meta (META -1.86%) is building a roughly $50 billion data center in Louisiana. In fintech, Robinhood (HOOD -1.88%) is issuing $400 million in bonds backed by card payments so it can grow its credit card business without tapping its own cash, with Wells Fargo (WFC +0.59%) and Barclays (BCS -0.69%) helping issue the cards.

Stocks and the 10-Year as of the July 13th close; gold and silver are spot as of the morning of July 14th; Brent as of July 13th (Market Briefs, Trading Economics, Yahoo Finance).
Americans are leaning harder on plastic. More households are putting everyday costs, even groceries, on credit cards and falling behind. The share of borrowers missing a minimum payment climbed to 8.7% in 2025 from 7.1% in 2023, as food prices sit about 32% higher than five years ago while real wages have slipped. Total card balances are now at a record $1.27 trillion (see today's chart). Since consumer spending drives most of the economy, a stretched cardholder is a warning light worth watching.
Geopolitics stayed front and center. President Trump wants to charge a toll on every ship using the Strait of Hormuz, about $30 million per supertanker, roughly a 20% tax on the cargo's value, as the U.S. and Iran ceasefire frays and a blockade looms. Roughly a fifth of the world's oil moves through that strait.
In the markets, the 10-year Treasury held at 4.61%. Gold rebounded about 1.5% to $4,085 after the latest inflation reading, while silver fell 1.4% to about $58.00, near a seven-month low as Middle East tensions and growth worries weighed on the more industrial metal. Brent crude firmed 0.92% to $84.07.
Total credit card balances have climbed to a record $1.27 trillion as more households lean on plastic and fall behind on payments.

Crypto was mixed but quiet, with Bitcoin up 0.10% to $62,327, Ether up 0.23% to $1,776, and XRP down 0.20% to $1.06.
The attention-grabber was a role reversal. Strategy (MSTR -2.68%), long the loudest corporate Bitcoin buyer, sold about $466 million of its own stock this week and bought no Bitcoin, lifting its cash reserve to roughly $3 billion to cover dividends and interest on its preferred shares. Its recent selling has rattled the rest of the market.
On the policy front, President Trump is pushing the Senate to pass the CLARITY Act, a bill that would set clearer rules and oversight for crypto. Its path forward is less certain after the loss of one of its key backers.
