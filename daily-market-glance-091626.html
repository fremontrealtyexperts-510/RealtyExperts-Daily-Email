🔔 The Fed just raised rates:
At 11 AM PT the Federal Reserve raised its target range by a quarter point, to 3.75% to 4.00%. It is the Fed's first increase since July 2023. The vote was 12 to 0, and the new rate takes effect Thursday.
We flagged this one. Our September 11 edition put the odds of a hike at 85.6%, Monday's at about 85%, and Tuesday's at 92.3%, and markets had it at 92.7% this morning. The Fed's statement says inflation "remains elevated" and that the move supports a timelier return to its 2% goal.
What comes next: the Fed's new projections put its rate at 4.1% at the end of this year and again at the end of next year, up from 3.8% and 3.6% in June. In plain terms, the typical Fed official now expects one more quarter point increase this year and no net change in 2027.
What it means for mortgages: the Fed does not set mortgage rates, and this hike was widely expected, so much of it was already built into rates. Mortgage News Daily had the 30-year at 7.19% and the 15-year at 6.81% this morning, before the announcement, just under Tuesday's 7.22% and 6.82%, the highest readings of the past year. The 10-Year closed Tuesday at 5.00%, its first close at 5% or higher since July 2007, and was near 4.96% after the decision.
• The local math: at Fremont's median list price of $1,250,000, a 20% down loan is $1,000,000, under Alameda County's $1,249,125 high-balance conforming limit. At 7.19%, principal and interest run about $6,781 a month, and high-balance loans often price a little above that.
• Every eighth of a point on that loan is about $85 a month.

The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve close, gold and silver are the COMEX front month at Tuesday's 1:00 PM PT print measured against Monday's same time print, and Brent is the ICE front month settlement. Wednesday so far: at 11:26 AM PT gold was $4,358.30, silver $64.31, and Brent was trading near $105.57, a live tick, not a settlement.
Today's two economy charts are about budgets, one for the Pentagon and one for families.
The Congressional Budget Office estimates the conflict with Iran added about $38.1 billion to Defense Department costs from February 28 through August 1. More than half, $21.6 billion, is the cost of replacing munitions, led by $13.1 billion for missile defense interceptors. Extra flying hours added $10.4 billion and higher fuel prices $2.7 billion. CBO says each additional month would cost $2 billion to $3 billion, depending on intensity. The graphic going around shows five of the seven categories and labels the total as what "the U.S. spent." CBO's figure covers only the Pentagon's added costs, and our chart shows all seven categories.
The second chart is for parents. Trump Accounts, the new IRS savings accounts for children, can take up to $5,000 a year, and 2026 money has to be in by December 31. Employers can put in up to $2,500 per employee, but that counts inside the $5,000, not on top of it as the newsletter said. U.S. citizen children born 2025 through 2028 can also get a one time $1,000 Treasury deposit that does not count toward the cap. Putting in more than the limit triggers a 6% yearly tax on the extra.
Oil and metals: Brent settled at $108.75 Tuesday, up 2.9%, and was near $105.57 late this morning. At Tuesday's 1:00 PM PT print, gold slipped 0.15% to $4,333.40 and silver rose 0.66% to $64.18. After the Fed decision gold was $4,358.30 and silver $64.31 at 11:26 AM PT, both above Tuesday's prints but off this morning's highs.
