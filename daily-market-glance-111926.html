Pending home sales hit a record low nationally; here, homes in contract are down 29% from a year ago.

Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.45% this morning, down 0.01 from Wednesday, with the 15-year at 6.99%. Freddie Mac's weekly survey, out this morning, rose to 7.28% from 7.11%, its first increase since October 8 (the newsletter's 7.35% does not match Freddie's release). On today's $1,258,995 Fremont median with 20% down, that is about $7,008 a month in principal and interest.
Pending home sales fell 4.7% in October and 16.7% from a year ago, NAR reported, a second straight month at a record low. The West fell hardest, down 20.3% on the year. NAR's Lawrence Yun said October "could mark the low point in contract signings for this housing cycle."
Our own count points the same way, and more sharply (chart). On this date a year ago, 803 homes were in contract across the 14 cities in both of our exports; today it is 570, down 29%, while homes for sale rose 3% to 1,755. That is 3.1 homes for sale for every one in contract, up from 2.1. Fremont's contracts fell from 89 to 56, Hayward's from 97 to 59 and Newark's from 35 to 24.
Thursday's export shows 2,313 board wide, from 2,350 Wednesday, with 131 coming soon and 105 brand new. The five city ledger is 589, from 594: Fremont (197) fell three, Hayward (170) two and Milpitas (94) one, while Newark (72) added one and Union City (56) held.
What this means for you: buyers, with three homes on the market for every signed contract, you have time to compare and room to negotiate. Sellers, fewer buyers are signing, so price to today's comps rather than last spring's.
