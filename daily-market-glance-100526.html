Friday's jobs report was soft, and mortgage rates rose anyway. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed ended the week at 7.57% and the 15-year at 7.19%, and the 10-Year Treasury is higher again this morning, near 5.33%. A weak jobs report usually helps rates; this one did not get the memo.
Here, flat hiring is not new. The Oakland, Fremont and Berkeley metro division added only about 500 jobs from December to August, while the San Jose area added 14,700 (see the chart in Economy).
Locally, the five city ledger held at 664 for a third straight day, though the mix shifted: Union City rose to 60 and Fremont slipped to 238. The board wide table edged up to 2,688 from 2,687.
What this means for you: at 7.57%, the five city median list price of $1,070,000 with 20% down runs about $6,026 a month in principal and interest. Slower hiring can thin the buyer pool, so sellers should price for the buyers who are here today. Buyers with steady income have more leverage; ask for seller credits toward a rate buydown.

Levels are Friday, October 2 at the closing bell. Monday at 8:16 AM PT: S&P 500 +0.40%, Dow -0.22%, Nasdaq +0.70%.
Ford has had a rough ride. The stock closed Friday at $12.10, down 7.8% for 2026 while the S&P 500 is up 12.8%, and 31% below its May 29 high of $17.44. That peak was a battery storage rally: shares jumped 13% on May 13 after a bullish Morgan Stanley call on Ford Energy, and on May 18 Ford agreed to supply EDF with up to 20 GWh of grid storage starting in 2028.
September let the air out: a recall of 223,472 F-150s over fuel tank straps, a supplier problem that disrupted F-150 production at month end, and third quarter U.S. sales down 6.6%. Ford says the sales drop reflects the planned end of the Escape and Corsair, and that the supplier issue fits inside its $10 billion to $11 billion profit guidance. Ford is unchanged this morning at $12.10.
Ford has given back its whole May battery storage rally and closed Friday 7.8% below where it started the year, while the S&P 500 is up 12.8%.
