REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.27% unchanged  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.92% unchanged

Nearly half the budget now goes to the roof and the refrigerator. New federal data show housing and food took 46.9% of the average American household's spending in 2025, the most in 15 years. Housing did nearly all of that work: it rose 5.8% to $27,790 a year, and shelter alone rose 8.0%, while total spending rose 4.3%.

It runs higher here. The latest metro data, for 2023 and 2024, put the San Francisco metro, which includes Alameda County, at $42,517 a year on housing, 36.2% of spending, and 48.6% with food. A Fremont buyer at today's $1,346,900 median with 20% down would pay about $7,365 a month in principal and interest at 7.27%, about double the metro average before taxes and insurance.

Rates held, for now. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is unchanged at 7.27% and the 15-year at 6.92%. Oil's plunge pulled the 10-Year to about 4.88% this morning from 4.94%, and if that lasts, mortgage rates should follow. It is one morning, and nothing is signed.

One headline to read twice. Census said residential construction spending fell 5.5% in September. All of that was home improvements, which had jumped in August. Spending on new single family homes was flat at a $403 billion annual pace.

What it means for you. Budget the whole payment, not the list price. If you are buying, run principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any HOA against take home pay before you start touring; 274 of the 583 homes on the market in our five cities are priced under a million. If you are selling, the buyer across the table is doing that same math, so price to the payment.

Today's count. Board wide: 2,482 listings across the 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, down from 2,554, with 144 coming soon and 138 brand new. The five city ledger carries 650 live listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, 583 on the market and 67 coming soon, down from 665. Part of that looks like the calendar: the count has dipped at each of the last six month turns.

Fremont holds 220 live listings: 169 active, 30 coming soon and 21 brand new, with a median on market list price of $1,346,900 at $824 a square foot and a typical listing out 38 days. Hayward carries 183 (from 188), Milpitas 99 (from 101), Newark 84 (from 90) and Union City 64 (from 63). Across all five cities the median is $1,088,000 at $671 a square foot, 274 homes are priced under a million, and 179 have been sitting 60 days or longer.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,580.07 -1.82%  |  Dow: 51,770.31 -1.09%  |  Nasdaq: 25,437.00 -2.86%

Stocks are higher as oil falls. The numbers above are Friday's closes. As of 10:20 AM PT Monday the Dow is up 1.06%, the S&P 500 up 0.42% and the Nasdaq up 0.37%. October was rough on tech: the Nasdaq lost 5.3%, its worst month since March 2025, while the Dow gained 1.7%. This week brings the midterms and the jobs report.

Big Oil's big quarter. Exxon earned $15.4 billion, up from $7.5 billion a year ago, and Chevron earned $9.9 billion, up from $3.5 billion, with refining doing the heavy lifting. Today's oil drop is the other side of that trade: Exxon is down 3.5% and Chevron 3.1% this morning.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 4.94% -0.04  |  Gold: $3,898.90 -3.29%  |  Silver: $49.28 -5.93%  |  Brent Crude: $113.43 +2.37%

Oil is the story. Brent is down about 9% this morning after Iran signaled it will try dialogue and its foreign minister said a proposal in Qatar could reopen the Strait of Hormuz in as little as two days if Iran's conditions are met. Nothing is signed. Cheaper oil would ease the inflation that has kept mortgage rates above 7%, which is why the 10-Year fell to about 4.88%. The numbers above are Friday's closes. Brent's moved to the January contract after December expired at $117.64, a roll and not a drop.

I bonds got a raise. Treasury's new rate is 5.51%, up from 4.26%, the highest since the window that ended in April 2023, for bonds bought through April 30. The 1.40% fixed part is the highest since November 2006 and stays for the life of the bond. The limits matter: $10,000 a year per person, no cashing in for 12 months, and you lose the last three months of interest if you cash in before five years.

CRYPTOCURRENCY

Bitcoin: $77,132 -0.99%  |  Ethereum: $2,256.01 -0.22%  |  XRP: $1.24 -0.43%

Stocks got the oil memo; crypto did not. Bitcoin is down 0.99% over 24 hours, Ether down 0.22% and XRP down 0.43%, live from CoinGecko at 10:20 AM PT. Binance reopened withdrawals Sunday and says it has restored backing for about 90% of the roughly $6.3 billion taken last week; the rest is in less liquid tokens it is still buying. That is backing, not recovery. The FBI has attributed the theft to North Korea.
