US 10-Year and Gold as of 4:00 PM ET Tuesday, May 26th. WTI Crude reflects today's intraday move via investing.com.
The 10-year is at 4.47%, off another 4 bps from yesterday and now 17 bps below where it sat a week ago. The bond rally has legs.
WTI cracked. Crude sits at $89.94, down nearly $4 from yesterday's $93.74 close. The low of the day was $87.80, the deepest sub-$90 print since early May. The catalyst is global supply rerouting, not a demand collapse, and the chart below tells the cleanest version of that story.
Japan's oil imports from the Middle East fell 67% YoY in April, the lowest level since 1979. Total Japan oil imports dropped from roughly 2.8 million barrels per day in January to about 1.7 million now. The substitution flow is going to the U.S.: shipments to Japan are running four times last year's pace. Translation: when one of the world's largest oil buyers reroutes its supply chain away from the Strait of Hormuz, the price impact shows up in WTI, not Brent.
Gold held steady at $4,501 despite the dollar staging a small rebound. The NFCC's quarterly consumer stress reading hit 6.7 out of 10, up sharply from the post-pandemic low of 3.5 in 2021. Consumer spending is two-thirds of the economy, so when stress is this elevated, the bond market eventually agrees with what gold is already saying.

Crypto slipped slightly across the board: BTC down 0.30%, ETH down 0.16%, XRP down 0.08%. A quiet day, but two governance stories worth flagging.
The U.K. just sanctioned HTX, the crypto exchange suspected of channeling more than $1.5 billion back to the Kremlin. U.K.-registered crypto firms are now legally required to enforce these sanctions on their platforms, which adds compliance cost across every major exchange operating in Europe. Sanction enforcement is the new audit, and the audit is not optional.
Separately, Polymarket disputes are being decided by nine anonymous whale wallets. Sixty percent of those "third-party" voters are linked back to Polymarket trading accounts. Decentralized governance has always had this problem; this is just the first time the structure has been documented this cleanly.
