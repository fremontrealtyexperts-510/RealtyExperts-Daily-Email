Reverse mortgage cash is forecast to nearly triple by 2035, and the U.S. is the biggest market for it.

More retirees are expected to turn their homes into cash. A new EY survey for EPPARG, a European industry group, puts the equity released each year through reverse mortgages and home reversions across 14 countries at about $19.8 billion today, rising to $58.3 billion by 2035. The U.S. is the largest single market: about $8.6 billion a year now, forecast at $24.1 billion.
Two fixes to the newsletter version. The survey reports in euros (17.7 billion and 52.1 billion), which Market Briefs printed as dollars, and it covers reverse mortgages, not the cash-out refinances most owners use. For now the forecast is ahead of the borrowers: FHA reverse mortgage endorsements fell 14% to 24,182 in fiscal 2026, and HUD's San Francisco field office logged 784, down from 964.
Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed eased to 7.46% Tuesday and the 15-year to 7.14%. Locally, the five city ledger reads 683, down from 686. Fremont (241), Milpitas (101), Newark (90) and Union City (62) held while Hayward slipped to 189 from 192. Of the 606 homes on the market, 161 have been listed 60 days or longer, up from 156. The board wide table eased to 2,652 from 2,661.
What this means for you: owners 62 and older sitting on Bay Area equity have more ways to tap it without selling, but a reverse mortgage carries real costs, and HUD requires a counseling session before one closes. Buyers get a slightly lower rate today, and 161 homes sitting 60 days or longer leave room to negotiate.
• Board wide: 2,652 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 163 coming soon and 207 brand new.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)

• Fremont: 241 live, 199 active, 29 coming soon and 13 brand new. The median on market list price is $1,262,444 at $789 a square foot, and the typical active listing has been out 34 days.
• The rest of the ledger: Hayward 189, Milpitas 101, Newark 90, Union City 62.

The U.S. is the largest market in EY's survey: about $8.6 billion a year now, forecast at $24.1 billion by 2035.

The 10-Year is Friday's Treasury close, since the bond market was shut Monday for Columbus Day. Tuesday's COMEX settlements: gold $4,177.30 (down 0.32%), silver $59.53 (down 1.63%). At 10:47 AM PT: 10-Year 5.22%, Brent $105.72 (down 0.87%).
Inflation likely peaked in May at 4.2%, but it is in no hurry to leave. Consumer prices ran 3.4% above a year earlier in both July and August, and Kalshi traders and the FactSet consensus expect 3.6% for September when the Labor Department reports Wednesday at 5:30 AM PT. That would be a step up from August, though still under the peak. This morning Kalshi priced 3.6% and 3.7% at close to even odds.
Bay Area prices run on their own clock. The government measures the San Francisco area every other month, and it was up 3.4% in the year to August, matching the nation, after 3.8% in April and June. Sticky inflation is what keeps the 10-Year near 5.2%, and mortgage rates follow it.
A correction: Market Briefs showed the 10-Year at 5.19% for Monday, but the bond market was closed; Treasury's latest close is Friday's 5.24%. It also had gold up and Brent down Monday, when the settlements show gold down 0.61% and Brent up 1.84%.
A 3.6% September would be a step up from August's 3.4%, still under May's 4.2% peak. The government reports Wednesday.
