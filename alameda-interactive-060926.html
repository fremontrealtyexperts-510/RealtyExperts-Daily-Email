REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 6.68%  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.20%

The 30-year edged up to 6.68% and the 15-year to 6.20% — both just a hair above yesterday. The reason it was not more is the 10-year Treasury, which held flat at 4.55%; mortgage rates take their cue from that 10-year, so when it sits still, they sit still. The real swing factor lands tomorrow with the May CPI report. Working in buyers’ favor, oil slid hard as the Middle East de-escalated and OPEC moved to pump more — if that holds, it eases the inflation pressure that has kept rates elevated.

Local board: 2,886 active (down 26 from Monday), 252 new listings, 224 closed sales. New supply eased slightly out of Monday while closings actually rose — buyers kept clearing inventory even as fewer fresh homes hit the market, which is why the board edged lower.

National: Co-buying is on the rise. The share of buyers purchasing a home with someone other than a spouse climbed from 25% in 2021 to 30% in 2025, and nearly 60% of renters say they’d buy with a friend, per a Rocket Mortgage survey. The math is simple — one income rarely clears today’s Bay Area prices, but two or three can. New platforms are even matching strangers who want in — a trend worth raising with first-time buyers who feel priced out solo.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,405  |  DOW: 50,786  |  NASDAQ: 25,929

Stocks steadied on Monday after Friday’s sharp sell-off, but the bounce was uneven. The NASDAQ led with a 0.86% gain as beaten-down chip and tech names found buyers, the S&P 500 edged up 0.30%, and the DOW slipped 0.16% — a split that reads as the AI trade stabilizing rather than roaring back.

The headline event was Apple’s (AAPL -1.89%) developer conference, where it unveiled a smarter Siri that reads on-screen context and a new in-house AI model, with Google helping build the features and the assistant running on Nvidia (NVDA +1.73%) hardware. It was also Tim Cook’s last conference as CEO — John Ternus takes over in September. Elsewhere, Yum Brands (YUM -2.32%) is reportedly in talks to sell Pizza Hut for as much as $4 billion, and OpenAI filed to go public, joining Anthropic and SpaceX in a class of names that could each be worth over $1 trillion.

ECONOMY

US 10-Year: 4.55%  |  Gold: $4,345  |  Brent Crude: $93.42

The 10-year Treasury held steady at 4.55%, leaving the rate backdrop quiet for a change — but oil stole the spotlight. Both Israel and Iran said they are halting strikes for now, and OPEC announced it will raise output by 188,000 barrels a day in July. Brent crude eased to $93.42 on the news. Lower oil tends to filter through to gas prices, grocery bills, and headline inflation, so a sustained pullback would be welcome relief.

The next big test lands tomorrow: the May CPI report. Closer to home, a New York Fed survey shows household money worries just hit their highest level since 2022 — the share who feel “much worse” off than a year ago jumped to 13.3%, even as the one-year inflation outlook slipped to 3.5%. Gold eased slightly to $4,345.

CRYPTOCURRENCY

BTC: $63,136  |  ETH: $1,682  |  XRP: $1.17

Crypto was quiet, drifting modestly lower across the board. The notable move came from Strategy (MSTR +5.61%) — the company formerly known as MicroStrategy — which scooped up another 1,550 Bitcoin for $10 million, roughly 50 times what it had just sold a week earlier in its first sale since 2022. It also built its cash pile to $1 billion to cover dividends; it is still down about $10 billion on its Bitcoin position and still buying. Meanwhile, money is pouring into a new fund tracking the HYPE token — close to $150 million since May — even as BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust fell about 16% in a week.
