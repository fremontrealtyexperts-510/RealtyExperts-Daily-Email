REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.42% +0.12  |  15-Year Fixed: 7.00% +0.08

Rates climbed as bonds reopened. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average rose to 7.42% Thursday, up 0.12 from Tuesday, and the 15-year to 7.00%, up 0.08, as bond trading resumed after Veterans Day and yields climbed. At 7.42%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,288,500 median with 20% down would pay about $7,151 a month in principal and interest, $84 more than at Tuesday's 7.30%.

Home sales hit a 16 year low. Existing home sales fell 5.5% in October to a 3.75 million annual pace, the lowest since August 2010, the National Association of Realtors said Thursday. In the West, sales fell 9.7% from September and 14.5% from a year ago, while the median price held at $624,300.

Condo buyers hold the cards, less so here. Redfin counted 123% more condo sellers than buyers nationwide in September, the widest gap in its records back to 2013, led by San Antonio at 459%. The Bay Area is far tighter: 69% in the Oakland metro, which covers Alameda and Contra Costa counties, 65% around San Jose and a balanced 6% in San Francisco. The Bay East board agrees: 481 condos are on the market across the 15 city table against 98 in contract, about five for every one, while houses run about 2.4 to one.

What it means for you. A 0.12 jump adds about $84 a month on the median Fremont home, so if you are ready to lock, talk with your lender about locking now or floating. Condo sellers, with five on the market for every one in contract, price to the building next door, not to last spring. Condo buyers, you have room to negotiate, so ask for the HOA budget and reserve study before you write.

Today's count. Board wide: 2,412 listings across the 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, up from 2,399, with 147 coming soon and 129 brand new. The five city ledger carries 629 live listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, 557 on the market and 72 coming soon, up from 628.

Fremont holds 219 live listings: 169 active, 35 coming soon, 14 brand new and 1 back on the market, with a median on market list price of $1,288,500 at $785 a square foot and a typical listing out 36 days. Hayward carries 181, Milpitas 94, Newark 74 and Union City 61. Across all five cities the median is $1,020,000 at $665 a square foot, 277 homes are priced under a million, and 184 have been sitting 60 days or longer.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,619.38 +1.31%  |  Dow: 52,486.49 +0.49%  |  Nasdaq: 25,466.19 +1.60%

Nvidia's founder steps down. Nvidia's board accepted the resignation of Jensen Huang as chief executive Wednesday, effective immediately, and named chief financial officer Colette Kress interim CEO while it searches for a successor, the Santa Clara company said in an SEC filing. Board member Harvey Jones becomes chair. As of 11:02 AM PT Nvidia is down 5.41%, after trading more than 10% lower earlier, while rivals AMD and Broadcom are up 4.56% and 5.31%, and the Nasdaq is off 0.36%. Kress keeps the finance job too, so for now two of Nvidia's top offices share one desk.

CoreWeave's revenue keeps climbing. CoreWeave, which rents out AI computing power, reported third quarter revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168% from a year earlier and $1.09 billion more than the second quarter, in its quarterly filing Thursday. Its raised 2026 forecast of $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion implies $4.69 billion to $4.99 billion in the fourth quarter. The stock is down 7.70% today after jumping 24.7% Wednesday, and rival Nebius is down 17.86% after its own outlook disappointed. The numbers above are Wednesday's closes, when chip stocks rebounded.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 4.98% +0.10  |  Gold: $4,000.50 -0.74%  |  Silver: $52.00 +1.52%  |  Brent Crude: $92.76 +1.39%

Yields rose as bonds reopened. Bond trading resumed Thursday after the Veterans Day holiday, and the 10-Year was near 5.12% at 11:02 AM PT, up from Treasury's 4.98% close Tuesday and the highest since October 21. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said she favors raising rates in smaller, more frequent steps and does not see the need for a half point increase in December. First time jobless claims rose 9,000 to 200,000, above the 195,000 economists polled by Dow Jones expected.

Gold fell below $3,900. Gold settled Thursday at $3,885.30, down 2.88%, and silver at $50.07, down 3.72%, after Wednesday's settlements of $4,000.50 and $52.00. Brent settled Wednesday at $92.76, up 1.39%, and is trading near $95.35. At Thursday's settle, an ounce of gold buys about five square feet of a median priced Fremont listing. The numbers above are Wednesday's settlements and Treasury's Tuesday close.
