REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 6.80%  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.33%

The daily 30-year fixed eased to 6.80% and the 15-year to 6.33%, both down a hair as oil pulled back. Small relief, but relief.

The housing market is not just slow, it is getting smaller. Residential investment fell 8% in the first quarter of 2026, squeezed by mortgage rates above 6.5%, climbing property taxes, and a new housing law pushing some investors to sell.

Builders are responding the only way that works: they cut the median new-home price 2.7% to $398,300 in June, on top of the rate buy-downs and cash credits already on the table. When a builder needs to move inventory, the buyer gets leverage.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,411 ▲ 0.05%  |  DOW: 51,947 ▲ 0.46%  |  NASDAQ: 24,975 ▼ 0.64%

Oracle landed a big one. The company signed a roughly $7 billion, 10-year deal to build software for the Pentagon, which analysts read as steady revenue at a moment when Oracle is taking on debt to fund AI data centers. The stock has not been rewarded for any of it: shares fell 4.21% on the day and are down about 41% for the year (chart below), a long way off their June peak near $250.

Nvidia made a bigger one, locking in a contract with SK Hynix for the memory chips its AI processors need, a deal that could be worth $500 billion over several years. On the policy front, President Trump opened a trade investigation into the European Union over fines levied on U.S. tech giants, including a $1 billion antitrust penalty against Google.

ECONOMY

US 10-Year: 4.68% ▼ 0.02%  |  Gold: $4,077 ▲ 0.62%  |  Silver: $58.53 ▲ 0.95%  |  Brent: $96.78 ▼ 1.00%

AI has quietly become an everyday habit. Market Briefs asked readers how often they use it: about 63% daily and another 18% weekly, so roughly 8 in 10 use it at least once a week, while 13.9% still do not use it at all (chart below). Not everyone is thrilled, either. Librarians in Maine are running seminars on how to switch off unwanted AI features.

The money it created is showing up in strange places. AI wealth has minted a new wave of millionaires, and top auction houses pulled in around $6 billion in the first half of 2026, a record, as buyers chased rare fossils and expensive watches as stores of value.

Oil finally cooled. The U.S. paused airstrikes on Iran for a second night and Brent eased to $96.78, though crude is still up about 27% in two weeks with the Strait of Hormuz blocked. The metals kept climbing, with gold at $4,077 and silver at $58.53 ahead of this week’s Fed meeting.

Why a REALTOR® cares: tariffs now cover 99.4% of U.S. trade and small businesses are suing to overturn them. That fight runs straight through construction costs, which is what turns into the price of a new home.

CRYPTOCURRENCY

Crypto started the week firm, with XRP consolidating near $1.10 after lagging the other two. There is a new seller in town: the Smarter Web Company sold 177.9 Bitcoin for $11.68 million to clear an $11.7 million debt, choosing to part with coins rather than dilute existing shareholders. A reminder that a Bitcoin treasury is only a strategy until the loan comes due. And in the department of things nobody turned off, the Washington Nationals are still running ads for Terra, the crypto company that shut down in 2024.
