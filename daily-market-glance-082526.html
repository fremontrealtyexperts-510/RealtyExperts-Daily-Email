A Third Session With The 15 Year Stuck At 6.62% While Freddie Mac Says 5.95%; JPMorgan Puts $750 Billion Behind Housing And Fremont Holds 241 Active Listings

The 30 year fixed is 6.78% and the 15 year is 6.62%, both unchanged, per Mortgage News Daily. Their board has not posted a Tuesday print yet, so these are Monday's closing figures.
The 15 year flag stays up for a third session:
• Where the three boards sit: Mortgage News Daily has the 15 year at 6.62%, Freddie Mac's weekly survey has it at 5.95%, and the Mortgage Bankers Association has it at 6.08%. All three agree on the 30 year within 13 basis points.
• Why it still does not add up: 6.62% leaves only 16 basis points between the 15 year and the 30 year on the same board, where the normal gap is closer to 70. Nothing has moved since Friday's 31 basis point jump, so this is now a settled disagreement rather than a one day spike.
• What to do: quote the 30 year freely. Do not put the 15 year in front of a client without a live lender quote behind it.
Two housing stories landed together this morning, and they point in opposite directions:
• JPMorgan is committing $750 billion to housing: The pledge runs ten years and targets building or preserving 1 million homes. Read the incentive honestly. Mortgage lending is at a low and banks earn when people borrow, so the bank is buying itself a future pipeline. That does not make it bad news. Capital at that scale aimed at supply is precisely what this market has lacked.
• Small builders are being squeezed: Material costs rose 9.1% for builders with five or fewer starts against 1.8% for the large firms, because scale buys discounts. The builders most likely to put up a home on a single infill lot are the ones facing the worst cost inflation, which is part of why new supply keeps arriving as large tract product instead of scattered houses.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Board wide: 2,701 active, BOMK, PCH and new listings across the 15 city board, with 193 coming soon and 213 brand new today.
• Fremont: 241 active across all property types, made up of 35 townhomes, 83 condos and 123 detached homes, plus 102 in contract, 44 coming soon and 19 new today.
• Five city live ledger: 697 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, down 6 from yesterday.
• The pattern to notice: Fremont has held 102 listings in contract for two days running while its active count slipped from 248 to 241. Demand is steady and supply is thinning, which is the quiet version of a tightening market.
• Coming soon is the tell: 44 of Fremont's listings are staged and not yet live. That is the largest coming soon block on the board and it is where a prepared buyer gets a look before the portals light up.

A soft Monday under the surface. The S&P 500 closed at 7,653, down 0.28%, the NASDAQ fell 0.77% to 25,980, and only the Dow finished green, up 0.26% to 53,417. Every stock figure in this morning's newsletter reconciled against the tape, which is worth saying on the days it happens.
Nvidia is the name to watch this week. It bought Groq for $20 billion last year and says the first chips from that deal come online this year. Groq builds language processing units, the chips that answer questions rather than train models, so this is Nvidia buying its way into the part of AI that runs every day rather than the part that gets built once.
Elon Musk floated sending rockets to the moon late next year to set up data centers there, which is what prompted the chart below. Before anyone gets too excited about server farms in orbit, it is worth seeing where the ones on Earth actually sit.
The United States holds 5,427 of the world's data centers, roughly 46% of every one on Earth, and more than the next ten countries combined. Two notes on the newsletter's version of this chart: its headline said 12,000 while its own source says more than 11,700, and it drew Germany, the UK, China, Canada and France as identical blocks even though Germany has two thirds more than France. These bars are to scale.

Ten year yield and Brent are Monday's August 24 settlements. Gold and silver are Fortune spot prints at about 9:10 AM and 8:00 AM Eastern today.
Two corrections to this morning's newsletter, both in this section.
First, oil. The newsletter printed Brent at $92.31 and called it a 0.15% gain. Brent settled Monday at $92.17, down 2.35% from Friday's $94.39. The level is off by 14 cents and the direction is simply wrong. Oil had a hard day, not a quiet one.
Second, the ten year. The newsletter shows 4.70% and no change. Treasury's own daily curve has Monday at 4.70%, down four basis points from Friday's 4.74%. The level is right this time, the change is not.
Oil is the story to watch this week. Washington unveiled its largest ever financial penalties on Iran, and Tehran is threatening to close the Strait of Hormuz and seize ships. Roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil moves through that strait. The front month contract is trading near $87 as this goes out, though that session has not settled, so the market is currently reading sanctions as less demand rather than less supply. That is exactly the kind of position that reverses on a single headline, and mortgage pricing feels it through inflation expectations.
Metals gave back ground. Gold is $4,630 an ounce, down 0.94%, and silver is $68.17, down 1.45%. Both of Fortune's day over day comparisons reconcile exactly with the numbers we published yesterday, which is worth noting after their gold article contradicted its own prior edition on Monday.
The tariff on Canadian built vehicles is doubling to 50%, and the newsletter's line that Toyota and Honda build 76.5% of Canada's cars is exactly right. The chart below shows why that matters more than it first looks. If you are shopping a RAV4, a Civic or a CR-V this year, the sticker is more likely to move than it was last week.
One more from the Treasury desk. The government may tap its $950 billion cash balance to double bond buybacks to $4 billion per operation, which Secretary Bessent argues would calm a jumpy bond market without issuing new debt. Goldman Sachs and Citadel Securities both say it will not work. Watch it anyway, because a calmer long end is the shortest path to a lower mortgage rate.
Toyota and Honda assembled 938,000 of the 1,226,099 vehicles Canada built in 2025, which is the 76.5% the newsletter cited, and each of them individually out-built Ford, General Motors and Stellantis combined. So a tariff aimed at Canada lands hardest on two Japanese automakers. The Detroit figure is a residual, total minus the other two, and is labeled as one on the chart.
Sources: Global Automakers of Canada; Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada.

The rally has stalled rather than reversed. Bitcoin is at $79,384, essentially flat over 24 hours, with Ether at $2,478, down 0.76%, and XRP at $1.47, down 1.85%. The newsletter's numbers are a Monday 4:00 PM Eastern snapshot and run a little higher across the board, which is a timing difference and not an error.
Two items worth an agent's attention, both about money organizing itself. Strategy set up a separate $1.59 billion reserve called USD Cash, splitting its dividend and debt obligations away from its Bitcoin buying, and it has not bought Bitcoin since June. When the largest corporate holder starts ring fencing cash, it is managing risk rather than adding it.
Meanwhile the industry has committed close to $200 million to the midterms, and Stand With Crypto has endorsed 32 House incumbents who backed the Clarity Act. Crypto has stopped waiting for rules and started buying the people who write them.
