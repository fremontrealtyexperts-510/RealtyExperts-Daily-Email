REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 6.54%  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.12%

The 30-year fixed held at 6.54% and the 15-year stayed at 6.12%. Rates have gone quiet, and so has the resale market, with the lock-in effect still keeping owners in place until the gap to their existing rates narrows.

U.S. home prices set another record high in May, but annual growth was just 0.7%, the ninth straight month under 1%. High rates and thin supply have the market in a standoff: prices are not falling, but they are barely rising either.

Local board: 2,805 active (down 96 from yesterday), 177 new listings, 206 closed sales. Oakland again led with 36 new on 744 active; Fremont added 24 new on 339 active.

National: Digital Realty (DLR -5.79%), one of the largest owners of U.S. data centers, agreed to buy Blackstone's stake in three Virginia data centers for $3.5 billion. The REIT's shares fell on the news because the deal leans on $2.3 billion in new stock, diluting existing shareholders, a reminder that even the hottest corner of real estate has a price.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,499  |  DOW: 52,319  |  NASDAQ: 26,213

Wall Street closed the books on its best quarter in six years, with the S&P 500 up 0.79% to 7,499, the NASDAQ up 1.52% to 26,213, and the DOW up 0.26% to 52,319. Over the past three months the S&P added roughly $8 trillion in value. Chips did the heavy lifting: semiconductor stocks gained about $2 trillion in the second quarter, their best three months ever, led by AMD (+7.68%) and Intel (+6.01%), though the group cooled 7.9% in the final week on AI-spending worries. Nike (NKE -1.04%) beat earnings, but a one-time $986 million tariff refund did most of the work.

ECONOMY

US 10-Year: 4.46%  |  Gold: $3,989  |  Silver: $58.74  |  Brent: $73.29

Metals eased: gold slipped 1.23% to $3,989 and silver held near $58.74, while the 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to 4.46% and Brent crude added 0.47% to $73.29.

The Fourth of July cookout is the priciest on record, feeding ten for about $73.82 as the U.S. cattle herd sits at a 70-year low. Meanwhile President Trump is leaning on gas stations to cut prices to $2.50 a gallon against a national average near $3.86, arguing that crude has fallen but the savings have not reached the pump.

CRYPTOCURRENCY

BTC: $59,046  |  ETH: $1,589  |  XRP: $1.05

Crypto edged higher on the day but the trend is still lower, with Bitcoin up 0.89% to $59,046, Ether up 1.31% to $1,589, and XRP up 1.03% to $1.05. Even with the bounce, Bitcoin is down about 32% year to date. Strategy (MSTR -6.20%), the largest corporate Bitcoin holder, sold more than $1 billion of its stash this week, and the coin dipped toward $58,000. On the policy front, crypto firms have already poured $189 million into the 2026 midterms, more than they spent in all of 2024, largely to push the stalled Clarity Act.
