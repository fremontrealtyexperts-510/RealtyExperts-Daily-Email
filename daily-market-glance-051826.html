Rates Tick Up Into Monday At 6.65%, Industrial Production Surge Keeps Rate-Cut Path Closed, Gen Z Quietly Becomes 20% Of Buy Demand
Three threads to open the week with clients.
First, rates ticked up over the weekend. The 30-year is at 6.65%, up 3 bps from Friday's 6.62%. The 15-year is at 6.10%, up 1 bp. Modest moves, but the direction matters. Five days ago we were at 6.42% on the 30-year. We are 23 bps higher than that in a week, the largest weekly move we have tracked this year, and the rates have now ticked up again on the Monday open rather than mean-reverting. The bond market is not done repricing.
Second, the macro is no longer working in favor of the rate-cut narrative. April industrial production rose 0.7% (chart in the Economy section above), the strongest single-month reading in over a year, with motor vehicles and parts up 3.7%. The Philly Fed's forecaster panel now expects CPI to hit 6% this quarter, up from 2.7% in February. A resilient economy plus a rising inflation forecast is the exact macro mix that closes the rate-cut window. The CME Fed-cut odds for 2026 sat at 1.6% as of Friday. Nothing in this morning's data moves that number higher. Any client telling you they are waiting for cuts is making a bet against a 98.4% probability.
Third, the demographic story worth flagging on buyer calls this week: Gen Z is now responsible for almost 20% of U.S. mortgage buy requests, per LendingTree. The interesting wrinkle is where they are buying. Minneapolis, Birmingham, and Indianapolis are the biggest Gen Z destinations. Miami, San Francisco, and Las Vegas are showing essentially no Gen Z interest. The under-30 cohort is voting with its feet against coastal-and-large-city pricing. For Bay Area listings, the implication is that the pool of demand from that age bracket is structurally smaller than it would have been five years ago. The buyers coming to the table here remain dominated by older move-up and equity-rich households. Price your listings and shape your messaging accordingly.

April industrial production rose 0.7%; motor vehicles and parts up 3.7%, biggest single-month gain in over a year. Source: Federal Reserve via FRED.
The macro picture into Monday is that the economy is staying hot, not cooling. The 10-year edged up to 4.62%, gold pulled back 1.11% to $4,511 as risk-off bid faded, and WTI Crude is up another 1.25% to $102.28. Brent is at $111.78, a meaningful spread that reflects the Iran-war energy premium still feeding through.
The headline data point of the week: U.S. factories just posted their best month in over a year. Industrial production rose 0.7% in April, with motor vehicles and parts jumping 3.7%. The chart above is the FRED series, and the slope tells the story. Three years of essentially flat factory output, then a sharp upward break starting in late 2025 that has continued through Q1 2026. Oil shocks and rising bond yields have not slowed activity yet.
The other shoe drops in the inflation outlook. The Philly Fed's panel of forecasters now expects CPI to hit 6% this quarter, up from 2.7% in February, with the Iran-war oil premium being cited as the trigger. Whether the panel is right or wrong, what matters for rates is that consensus is moving the wrong direction. A resilient economy plus a rising-inflation outlook is the worst possible mix for the rate-cut thesis that 2025's bull run was built on.
For client conversations, the takeaway is the same one we have been carrying for two weeks: do not let any buyer or seller anchor on "rates will come down soon." The data is no longer cooperating with that narrative.

The story worth flagging for clients who follow crypto: Strategy (MSTR -5.11%), the largest corporate Bitcoin holder on earth with $63B in BTC, just filed to buy back $1.5B of its debt and explicitly listed Bitcoin sales as one of the funding options.
