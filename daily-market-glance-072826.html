The daily 30-year fixed slipped to 6.76% and the 15-year to 6.31%, helped along by oil's collapse. Three straight days of easing now.
The backyard is the new subdivision. Accessory dwelling units made up nearly 28% of all new home permits in the Bay Area in 2024, as California's housing push turned side yards into build sites. Plenty of owners put them up to rent out and offset a cost of living that keeps climbing. If you own a lot here, that is a real equity lever worth understanding.
The other half of the story is who is not buying. One in three young Americans now lives with their parents, priced out of living alone. That is a demand problem with a long tail: millions of would be first time buyers sitting out for years, which eventually shows up as fewer move-up sellers too.

Stocks were mixed again. The DOW rose 0.51% to 52,210, the S&P 500 was essentially flat at 7,413, and the NASDAQ eased 0.18% to 24,932.
Amazon wants to put your phone on a satellite. It is seeking approval to launch 5,105 satellites for a direct-to-phone network, no cell towers required, chasing SpaceX's Starlink, which already offers the service. If approved, Amazon would own the second largest fleet in orbit. The scale of that catch-up is the story: there are about 15,500 active satellites circling Earth today and Starlink alone accounts for roughly 10,200 of them, about two thirds (chart below).
Apple is having a moment. Traders placed $590 million in Apple options bets in a single day ahead of its July 30 earnings, and the company has overtaken Nvidia as the most valuable in the world at $4.9 trillion. Meanwhile a new Chinese memory chip maker, CXMT, jumped as much as 466% on its Shanghai debut as investors bet on it becoming China's answer in the global AI chip race.

Gold and silver are live spot quotes taken the morning of July 28 (change versus the same time a day earlier). The 10-Year and stocks are as of the July 27 close. Brent is the July 27 settlement, live sourced because the newsletter's printed figure did not reconcile with its own daily change.
Tariffs are the quiet cost in everything. The average statutory rate now sits around 10.2% across all goods, but it is wildly uneven by category (chart below). Steel and aluminum carry roughly 52.6%, about five times the all goods rate, while semiconductors run 10.3%, automobiles 9.4%, food 5.7%, and pharmaceuticals 4.7%.
The trade news moved both ways. China said the U.S. agreed to cap a new set of tariffs on Chinese goods at 20%, though the two sides are still fighting over AI. The U.S. accused China of stealing AI information and is preparing sanctions, and China says it will retaliate if they go through.
The Fed meets today and tomorrow and is expected to hold rates steady. Inflation is running at 3.5%, still well above the 2% target, with tariff costs and the U.S. and Iran conflict both feeding into the picture.
Oil fell off a cliff. Brent settled at $88.36, down about 8.7% in a single session, after the U.S. paused strikes on Iran and Tehran said it had halted retaliation. That is one of the largest one day drops in years. Gold slipped about 1.0% to $4,032 and silver fell 1.49% to $57.53 as a stronger dollar and Fed uncertainty weighed on both.
Why a REALTOR® cares: steel and aluminum at 52.6% is not an abstraction. It is framing connectors, ductwork, roofing, and appliances, and it lands in the price of every new build and major remodel your clients are quoted.
Steel and aluminum carry a statutory tariff rate near 52.6%, about five times the 10.2% average across all goods. Semiconductors sit at 10.3%, automobiles 9.4%, food 5.7%, and pharmaceuticals 4.7%.
