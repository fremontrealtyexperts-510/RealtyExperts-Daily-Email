Mortgage rates rose for a third straight day. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year reached 7.60% on Wednesday, up 0.02 and the highest in almost three years, and the 15-year is 7.22%, the highest since October 2023. On our five city median of $1,088,000 with 20% down, principal and interest come to $6,146 a month, $419 more than when September began at 6.89%.
Today's first chart is a new Urban Institute survey. One in five working-age renters (20.0%) paid rent late or short at some point in 2025, up from 16.5% in 2024 and the survey's first significant yearly change since it began in 2017. The jump came from middle income renters, 14.3% to 21.6%. The lower income rise the graphic going around shows (26.4% to 27.8%) is within the margin of error, so we drew it dashed.
The survey's income bands are national, so a family of three earning $53,300 to $106,600 counts as middle income, and the West was the one region where the share slipped. Our rents tell their own story. Zillow's August index puts the typical Fremont rent at $3,375, up 5.2% in a year, with Milpitas up 9.5% and Union City 6.3%, against 2.5% nationally. All five of our cities beat the national pace.
What this means for you: today's squeezed renters are tomorrow's buyers, but at 7.60% the median home costs about 1.8 times Fremont's typical rent before taxes and insurance. Rent growth is holding up for owners who lease; for buyers, the math rewards negotiating on price, and 147 of the ledger's 593 homes on the market have been listed 60 days or longer. Locally the five city ledger slipped to 675 from 691 and the board wide table fell 28 to 2,672.
• Board wide: 2,672 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 167 coming soon and 166 brand new.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)

• Fremont: 242 live, 189 active, 30 coming soon, 22 brand new and 1 back on market. The median on market list price is $1,242,633 at $771 a square foot, and the typical active listing has been out 30 days.

Middle income renters drove 2025's rise in late rent nationally. Here, rent rose faster than the national 2.5% in all five of our cities.

Thursday is live: at 7:50 AM PT the 10-Year was near 5.29%, gold $4,204.00 (+0.41%), silver $61.64 (+1.77%) and December Brent $100.79 (+2.82%).
The 10-Year Treasury closed at 5.29% Wednesday, up three basis points and its highest close since May 14, 2002. Higher U.S. yields are pulling money into the dollar: the euro is near $1.13 this morning, its lowest in more than a year.
Today's second chart is Europe, where inflation jumped in September. Spain's harmonised rate hit 5.0%, Italy's 4.1%, France's 3.4% and Germany's 3.3%, all up from August and all above the European Central Bank's 2% target, which it has raised rates twice this year to defend. For scale, consumer prices in the U.S. and the San Francisco area both rose 3.4% in the year to August. The euro area figure comes Friday.
December gold settled up 0.17% and silver down 0.96%. As flagged yesterday, the Brent card switches to December, the new front month: it settled at $98.03, up 1.94% from its own Tuesday settle of $96.16. The newsletter's $103.50 was November's last day before it expired.
All four of Europe's largest economies saw inflation rise in September, each well above the ECB's 2% target. U.S. and Bay Area prices are running at 3.4%.
