Rates Pop Another +10 BPs To 6.62% (+20 BPs Weekly); CME Fed-Cut Odds For 2026 Now 1.6%; Friday Listing Surge

Three threads to close the week with clients.

First, the rate march continued today. The 30-year jumped another 10 bps to 6.62%, the 15-year is at 6.09%. That’s +20 bps from Monday’s 6.42% in a single week, the largest weekly rate movement we have tracked this year. MND today is flagging "MBS prices significantly weaker" which means the move likely continues into Friday’s close. The Wednesday MBA Mortgage Applications survey shows applications stayed flat week-over-week, meaning buyers absorbed the rate jump and kept going. That’s the data point worth reading aloud to any fence-sitting client. The market is not waiting for the Fed.

Second, the "rates fall in 2026" thesis is mathematically dead now. CME puts Fed rate-cut odds for 2026 at 1.6%, down from the teens a week ago. Treasury Secretary Bessent is the only major voice still calling for disinflation. Any client telling you they are waiting for cuts is making a bet against a 98.4% probability. Frame the conversation that way and the urgency reframes itself.

Third, a headline anecdote worth bookmarking: a $3M Nantucket house is being given away for free on the condition the buyer relocates it within 180 days (moving cost $150K to $500K). Empty Nantucket lots start at $1.65M, so it’s still arguably a deal. The land-vs-structure economics on the coasts have completely inverted in some markets, and the Bay Area’s land scarcity is the same dynamic running in reverse.
