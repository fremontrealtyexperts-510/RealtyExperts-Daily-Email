REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.36% -0.04  |  15-Year Fixed: 7.00% -0.04

Rates eased again. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed average slipped to 7.36% today from 7.40% Wednesday, and the 15-year to 7.00% from 7.04%.

Buyers have not come back yet. Mortgage applications fell 5.3% in the week ending Oct. 16, the Mortgage Bankers Association said Wednesday, even though the 30-year contract rate in its survey dipped to 7.41% from 7.50%, the first decline in over two months. Purchase applications fell 7% and ran 18% below a year ago, and refinances fell 2%. MBA's Joel Kan said "higher rates are pricing buyers out of the market." About one application in ten was an adjustable rate loan, priced almost a full point below the 30-year fixed.

Today's bond market could change that math. The 10-Year Treasury yield, which mortgage rates follow, fell to about 4.96% at 11:18 AM PT from Wednesday's 5.13% close, its first time under 5% since late September, as investors sold technology stocks and bought bonds. If it holds, lenders have room to price lower in the days ahead.

What it means for you. Watch the 10-Year this week more than the headlines. On our five city median of $1,075,000 with 20% down, principal and interest runs about $5,931 a month at 7.36%, and every quarter point in rate moves that by about $146. If you are ready to buy, ask your lender about a float down, which lets a locked rate fall if the market improves before closing. If you are selling, remember that purchase applications are 18% below last year, so pricing right on day one still beats waiting for rates.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,757.17 +0.58%  |  Dow: 52,856.09 +0.69%  |  Nasdaq: 26,702.86 +1.05%

The AI trade unwinds. Those are Wednesday's closes. At 11:18 AM PT Thursday the Nasdaq was down 2.53%, the S&P 500 1.29% and the Dow 0.10%, with chip stocks leading the way down and Nvidia off about 4%. Meta fell about 7.5% after its results: revenue rose 26% to $64.60 billion, but it narrowed its 2026 spending plan to $135 billion to $145 billion, raising the low end, and announced a new non-voting share class to be issued as a stock dividend that works like a two-for-one split.

Tesla beat on profit, not on cars. Tesla reported after Wednesday's bell. Third quarter revenue was flat at $28.05 billion and deliveries fell 2% to 486,532, but adjusted earnings of $0.48 a share beat the $0.44 Wall Street expected. Capital spending more than doubled to $4.73 billion. Tesla builds cars at its Fremont factory, so its results land closer to home than most.

Up into the report, down after it. The stock rose 3.41% Wednesday to $386.47 and is down about 2% today, still 14.1% lower for 2026 and about 30% above its July low. One correction: Market Briefs said 1.74 million drivers pay $99 a month for Full Self-Driving. Tesla's own 1.74 million counts active FSD subscriptions, including buyers who paid up front.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 5.13% +0.01  |  Gold: $4,150.00 +0.18%  |  Silver: $57.71 -0.50%  |  Brent Crude: $108.82 +0.03%

Money ran to bonds. The numbers above are Wednesday's Treasury close and COMEX and ICE December settlements. Today the 10-Year fell to about 4.96% at 11:18 AM PT, under 5% for the first time since late September, as investors pulled back from technology stocks. Gold and silver did not get the safe haven bid: gold settled at $4,060.40, down 2.2%, and silver at $55.01, down 4.7%.

Oil went the other way. Brent was up about 3.9% near $113 a barrel at 11:18 AM PT, its highest since early May, on worries the conflict in the Middle East could widen. Higher oil feeds inflation, the one risk that could undo the bond rally. Bonds and oil rallied together today, a pairing that rarely stays friendly for long.

A correction. Market Briefs had the 10-Year at 5.09%, down 0.05 point, for Wednesday; Treasury's close was 5.13%, up 0.01. It also had gold down 0.57% and Brent up 1.40%. The settlements were gold up 0.18% at $4,150.00 and Brent up 0.03% at $108.82.

CRYPTOCURRENCY

Bitcoin: $81,022 -4.32%  |  Ethereum: $2,373.40 -5.72%  |  XRP: $1.30 -6.72%

Crypto followed tech lower. Bitcoin is down 4.32% over 24 hours near $81,020, Ether is down 5.72% and XRP 6.72%, live from CoinGecko at 11:18 AM PT.

Tokenized stocks took off. Robinhood's Stock Tokens, blockchain tokens that track shares and ETFs without voting rights and trade mostly overseas, did $11.31 billion of volume on decentralized exchanges on Robinhood Chain in September, 21.5 times July's $526 million, according to Bitwise Asset Management's staking report, which CNBC cited Wednesday. The tokens themselves were worth at least $160.8 million at the end of September, so this is mostly fast trading, not new money. Nvidia was the most traded token of the quarter at $2.50 billion.

Not every company wants in. Sixteen companies, including Goldman Sachs, Microsoft and Alphabet, have objected to tokenized versions of their shares trading on OKXICE, a new venue from OKX and NYSE owner Intercontinental Exchange that plans to list more than 60, CNBC reported. Under the SEC's new innovation exemption, companies get that say. Robinhood shares fell about 6% today, so the tokens are not the only thing trading fast.
