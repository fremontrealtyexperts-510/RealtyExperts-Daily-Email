Mortgage rates set another high for this run. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed rose to 7.61% Monday and the 15-year to 7.23%; MND's consolation is that the new high is only a hundredth of a point above last Wednesday's 7.60%. The 10-Year Treasury closed at 5.31%, its highest close since May 2002, and is a little lower this morning, near 5.26%.
One reason bonds stay on edge is in Economy: service businesses say the prices they pay are rising at the fastest pace since July 2022, and Bay Area consumer prices for services are now rising faster than the nation's. Sticky service prices are what keep the Fed leaning toward higher rates.
Locally, the five city ledger rose to 671 from 664: Newark gained three to 90, Hayward two to 175, and Fremont held at 238. The board wide table eased to 2,661 from 2,688.
What this means for you: at 7.61%, the five city median list price of $1,079,500 with 20% down runs about $6,104 a month in principal and interest, $422 more than on Sept. 1, when the 30-year was 6.89%. Sellers should price for that payment, not for the summer's. Buyers have room to negotiate with the 141 homes that have sat 60 days or longer; ask for seller credits toward a rate buydown.

Service prices are running hot. The Institute for Supply Management's services survey put its prices paid index at 74 in September, up from 72.6 and the highest since July 2022, and fuel costs came up twice as often as any other issue respondents raised. The headline services index slipped to 54.9, still growing.
ISM does not allow charts built from its index, so ours tells the same story with government data: service businesses raised prices 5.4% over the year to August, and Bay Area consumer prices for services rose 3.8%, against 3.1% nationally. A year ago the Bay Area ran a full point below the nation; now it runs seven tenths above, a lead nobody here asked for.
What service businesses charge rose 5.4% over the year to August, up from 3.0% a year earlier. Bay Area consumer prices for services are now rising faster than the nation's, 3.8% against 3.1%.
Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Producer Price Index (final demand services) and Consumer Price Index (services, U.S. and the San Francisco, Oakland, Hayward area).
