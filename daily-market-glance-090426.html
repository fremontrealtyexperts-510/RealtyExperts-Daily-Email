Rates held flat into the jobs report, and the jobs report just took the September cut off the table

15-YEAR FIXED

The 30 year is at 6.88% and the 15 year at 6.48%, both unchanged. A caveat on those: Mortgage News Daily publishes its daily survey around 4 PM Eastern, so what you see above is Thursday's close. Today's reaction to the jobs report is not in that number yet.
Here is what actually matters for anyone shopping right now. Vice President Vance spent yesterday publicly pressing the Fed to cut rates to make housing more affordable, calling it the proper and responsible move. The Fed meets September 15 and 16. Then this morning the economy printed 162,000 new jobs against a forecast of 53,000, with July revised from a loss to a gain.
A jobs number three times consensus does not make a September cut more likely. It makes it less likely. So the political pressure and the economic data are now pointing in opposite directions, and the data usually wins that argument.
The one thing keeping this from being worse for rates: the report was strong because more people came back into the labor force, not because employers were bidding wages up. That is why the ten year barely moved this morning. But the direction of risk for mortgage rates over the next two weeks is now up, not down.
What it costs, concretely: on a $450,000 purchase with 20% down, the payment is about $2,366 a month at today's 6.88%. If you have been floating a rate waiting for a September cut, this morning was the argument for locking.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Board wide: 2,646 listings across the 15 city board, counting everything active alongside everything in contract, with 208 coming soon and 146 brand new today. That is 4 more than yesterday.
• Fremont: 231 active across all property types, made up of 31 townhomes, 76 condos and 124 detached homes, duplexes and half plexes, plus 99 in contract, 41 coming soon and 15 new today.
• Five city live ledger: 694 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, up 8 from yesterday. Fremont 264, Hayward 188, Milpitas 113, Newark 76, Union City 53.
• The read: new listings stayed thin for a second day, 146 board wide against 154 yesterday and 192 on Wednesday, but coming soon climbed to 208 and Fremont alone is holding 41. That is the pipeline rebuilding for the back half of September. Supply is not leaving, it is queuing.
What to tell a client this week: the story changed this morning. All week the market was pricing rate cuts on soft labor data, and one government report undid most of that in a single print. Nobody should panic, and nobody should assume relief is coming in two weeks either. If you are under contract and floating, talk to your lender today. If you are listing in the next month, the coming soon queue says you will have more competition in late September than you would have this week.

Ten year yield and Brent are Thursday's September 3 settlements. Gold and silver are live spot quotes at about 7:58 AM Pacific on September 4, with the change measured from Thursday's settlement.
The August jobs report landed at 5:30 this morning Pacific and it was not close to what anyone expected.
• Payrolls: **162,000** jobs added, against a consensus near 53,000. Roughly three times the forecast.
• Unemployment: 4.1%, unchanged.
• Revisions: July was revised UP to a gain of 21,000 from a loss of 23,000. June was revised up to a gain of 31,000.
Read the internals before you read the headline, because they are the reason the bond market stayed calm. The household survey showed 569,000 more people employed and 683,000 more people entering the labor force. Participation rose two tenths of a point. That is why unemployment held at 4.1% even with strong hiring: the supply of workers grew alongside the demand for them. A jobs report driven by people coming back into the workforce is far less inflationary than one driven by employers bidding for a fixed pool of workers. The broader U6 measure, which counts discouraged and involuntarily part time workers, fell to 7.7%, its lowest since June 2025.
So the ten year Treasury is sitting near 4.77% this morning, essentially where it closed Thursday. Mortgage News Daily called it a balmy report and said bonds were only moderately changed.
What it undoes: yesterday's ADP survey showed just 38,000 private jobs and set off a rate cut rally. That trade is unwinding today. Gold is off about a third of a point, silver three quarters, and all three major cryptocurrencies are lower. We flagged yesterday that Friday's number would either confirm the ADP read or erase it. It erased it.
Two corrections against the source. The newsletter put the ten year at 4.78%; Treasury's own daily curve has Thursday closing at 4.77%, down two basis points, and its own printed change of minus two contradicts its printed level. And it put Brent at $95.85, up 0.23%; ICE front month futures settled at $95.52, DOWN 0.12%. That is the fourth day running Brent has come in wrong from that source.
