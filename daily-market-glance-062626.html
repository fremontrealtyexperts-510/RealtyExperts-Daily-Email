Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.53% As Q1 GDP Beats At 2.1%; Zillow Trims Its 2026 Outlook; The East Bay Board Climbs To 2,964 Active On 211 New And 200 Closed
The 30-year fixed eased to 6.53% and the 15-year to 6.12%, drifting lower with Treasury yields. Even so, that is still well above the rate most owners carry, and the lock-in effect that is thinning resale supply is not going anywhere.
Zillow turned more cautious. It cut its 2026 outlook to a 0.4% decline in home sales and expects values to rise just 0.1% this year, as mortgage rates pushed back toward 7% in the second quarter and slowed the recovery. It now sees about 3.76 million existing-home sales, down from 3.8 million.
At the top of the market, money is still moving. Ultra-wealthy buyers spent $38.6 billion on homes over $10 million in the past year (Compass), including Jeff Bezos at about $237 million near Miami, even as high rates and prices keep many regular buyers, with a median U.S. home around $403,000, on the sidelines.
Locally, the East Bay board climbed to 2,964 active (up 23 from yesterday), with 211 new listings against 200 closings. Oakland again led the board with 38 new on 778 active; Fremont added 23 new on 354 active.

For agents: rates dipped, but the bigger story is steady growth and a patient Fed, so do not promise buyers a quick rate drop. Lean into the fresh supply (2,964 active, 211 new) and price discipline. Well-priced, well-presented homes still sell inside about four weeks, while overpriced listings are sitting, so the coaching is the same: price to today's market and the sale follows.

Stocks were flat to slightly lower. The S&P 500 was essentially unchanged at 7,357, the NASDAQ slipped 0.46% to 25,358, and the DOW edged up 0.14% to 51,920 (figures as of the June 25th close).
Prices are the theme. Microsoft (MSFT -3.46%) is raising Xbox console prices by $100 to $150 starting August 1, blaming component costs that are up more than 2.5 times, and Apple (AAPL -6.15%) lifted prices on MacBooks and iPads the same day. Microsoft also expects memory costs to double again by late 2027.
The AI race got more pointed. Anthropic accused Alibaba (BABA -4.74%) of copying 28.8 million model exchanges in what it called the largest known distillation attack, and Alibaba shares are now down about 30% this year. Amazon (AMZN -3.14%) pledged another $13 billion for AI and cloud in India, bringing its planned 2026 to 2030 spend there to $48 billion.

10-Year, Brent, and the stock indexes as of June 25th (Market Briefs); gold and silver are live spot, June 26th (Fortune); Q1 GDP is the final BEA estimate.
The economy is the headline. The final reading on first-quarter GDP came in at 2.1%, beating the 1.6% forecast and revised up from the earlier 1.6% estimate, largely on AI investment (see the chart below). Consumer spending and fixed investment rose, while retail, wholesale, and finance pulled back. A growing economy with sticky inflation is exactly what keeps the Federal Reserve cautious about cutting rates.
Metals tried to steady. Gold bounced to about $4,052 (+1.17%) and silver to $59.04 (+1.32%) on the day, though both are still down sharply for the week and gold sits roughly 24% below its all-time high as the end of the U.S. and Iran conflict and a tech rebound pull money out of safe havens.
Oil eased again. Brent crude fell 2.03% to $73.73 with the Strait of Hormuz reopened, and the 10-year Treasury yield drifted to 4.37%.
The takeaway for housing: solid growth plus a patient Fed means the path to lower mortgage rates is gradual, not sudden, so the rate-driven standoff in the resale market is likely to persist.
The economy beat expectations. Final first-quarter GDP grew 2.1%, ahead of the 1.6% forecast and revised up on AI investment. Steady growth with sticky inflation keeps the Federal Reserve in no hurry to cut rates, which keeps mortgage rates elevated.

Crypto stayed soft. Bitcoin firmed 0.28% to $59,872 but is back below $60,000, deep into a multi-month slump, with Ether off 1.01% to $1,548 and XRP down 1.44% to $1.03 (figures as of the June 25th close).
A telling stat: about 10.83 million Bitcoin are now held at a loss, a record that tops prior bear-market bottoms. Long-term holders own a record 14.8 million coins and, so far, are not selling.
On the darker side, investigators found that Iranian entities moved more than $3.84 billion through the crypto exchange CoinEx during the recent conflict, the same venue North Korean hackers used to launder funds, a reminder that crypto rails still slip past sanctions.
