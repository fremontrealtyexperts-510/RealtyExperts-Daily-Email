  REAL ESTATE

  
Both mortgage rates are now at 52 week highs, and the 30 year Treasury is at a 19 year high
30-YEAR FIXED
6.87%
15-YEAR FIXED
6.38%
The 30 year fixed rose six basis points to 6.87% and the 15 year rose three to 6.38%, per Mortgage News Daily. Here is the part that matters: on MND's own 52 week range, 5.99% to 6.87% on the 30 year and 5.55% to 6.38% on the 15 year, both rates are sitting exactly at the top. Not near the top. At it.
Why, in one line: the long end of the Treasury curve is at a 19 year high. The chart in the Economy section below shows the 30 year yield peaking at 5.31% in August, a level it has not exceeded since June 2007. Mortgages are priced off that curve, so this is not a mortgage market story, it is a bond market story that mortgages inherit.
Worth noting that Mortgage News Daily attributes yesterday's move mostly to mechanical month end bond trading rather than to fresh economic news. That is a real distinction. Month end mechanics can unwind. A 19 year high in the long bond is a slower thing to reverse.
What that costs a buyer, concretely: on a $450,000 purchase with 20% down, the payment is now about $2,363 a month, roughly $207 more than it was in February.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Board wide: 2,664 listings across the 15 city board, counting everything active alongside everything in contract, with 196 coming soon and 208 brand new today. That is 80 fewer than yesterday, and the first of the month is when you expect it: August's closings came off the board together.
• Fremont: 237 active across all property types, made up of 34 townhomes, 78 condos and 125 detached homes and duplexes, plus 97 in contract, 39 coming soon and 28 new today.
• Five city live ledger: 692 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, down 19 from yesterday.
• Do not read the drop as demand. Board wide supply fell 80 and Fremont's active count fell 10, but 208 listings came on new this morning and 196 more are staged as coming soon. This is the calendar turning over, not the market thinning out.
• Fremont's pending count is 97, under 100 again after yesterday's 101. It has now printed 102, 102, 102, 101, 99, 101, 97 across seven sessions, which is a market drifting sideways with a slight downward tilt, not one that is turning.
• Median asking in Fremont is $1,289,000 at 29 median days on market, up about $39,000 from yesterday's $1,249,800. That is the same mix effect running the other way, and it is exactly why a single day's median move is not a price signal.
• What that means for a seller this week: rates just hit a 52 week high on both terms, and your buyer's monthly payment went up while your asking price did not go down. The listings that move in September are the ones priced for the payment, not for the comp.
• Newark is the quickest of our five at 25 median days on market.

Ten year yield and Brent are Monday's August 31 settlements. Gold and silver are Fortune spot prints this morning, 9:10 AM and 8:00 AM Eastern. Gold futures are trading about 1.7% above that spot print, which is a wider gap than usual: see the commentary.
The metals slide is now three sessions old and it is the biggest thing in this section. On our own series gold has gone $4,608 on Friday morning, $4,438 yesterday, $4,331 today. Silver has gone $70.62 to $67.35 to $65.03. That is roughly 6% off gold and 8% off silver inside three mornings.
One caveat on today's gold figure, in keeping with the rule we set for ourselves on Friday. Fortune's spot print is $4,331 and its own table puts the day at down 2.41% from yesterday's $4,438, which reconciles. But gold futures are at about $4,406 as this is written, some 1.7% above that spot print, and the same page's prose describes the move as a gain when its own numbers show a decline. The direction is not in doubt and neither is the three day trend. The size of today's move specifically is uncertain by about a percentage point, and you should quote it that way.
The ten year finished August at 4.75%, two basis points higher, on Treasury's own daily curve. This morning's newsletter printed 4.77%. The change it quoted was right and the level was not.
Brent settled at $90.49, up 1.32% from Friday. The newsletter reported $91.06 and a 0.63% gain, and that percentage does not reconcile against Friday's settlement on any basis.
Now the chart, which is the one to keep. The 30 year Treasury yield peaked in August at 5.31%, and the last time it traded above that was June 12, 2007. Nineteen years. The supplied version of this graphic showed only 2026, which asserts the claim without letting you see it; this one runs back far enough that you can. Every mortgage in the country is priced off the long end of this curve, which is the bridge to the Real Estate section above.
August's 5.31% peak is a level the 30 year has not exceeded since June 2007. The August 31 close was 5.25%.
