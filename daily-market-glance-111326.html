Existing home sales ran at a 3.75 million annual pace in October, the slowest since August 2010 and down 8.8% from a year ago, the National Association of Realtors said Thursday (chart). That is 39% below 2021's 6.12 million and under the 4.06 million of both 2024 and 2025. Unsold inventory rose 4.6% from a year ago to 1.59 million homes, a 5.1 month supply, and the national median edged up 0.4% to $416,700.
Our own board shows the same slowdown. Across the 14 cities in both our November 13 exports, homes in contract fell to 596 from 838 a year ago, down 29%, while homes on the market rose 2.5% to 1,715. In Fremont, contracts fell to 64 from 101. A year ago there were two listings for every home in contract; today there are nearly three.
Mortgage News Daily's latest reading, Thursday's, has the 30-year at 7.42% and the 15-year at 7.00%. Freddie Mac's survey averaged 7.33% in October, up from 6.25% a year earlier, NAR noted. At 7.42%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,279,000 median with 20% down would pay about $7,098 a month in principal and interest.
The board wide table is 2,419, from 2,412 Thursday, with 141 coming soon and 119 brand new. The five city ledger is 623, from 629: Milpitas (96) added two, while Fremont (216) fell three, Hayward (179) and Union City (59) two each, and Newark (73) one.
What this means for you: with nearly three listings for every contract, buyers have time and room to ask, so ask for a credit toward a rate buydown, not just a lower price. Sellers, price to what went into contract in the last six weeks, not to the spring. The market is not closed, it is just reading the fine print.
