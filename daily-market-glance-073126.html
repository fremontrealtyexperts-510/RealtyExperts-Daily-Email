The 30-Year Fixed Just Hit Its Highest Point In A Year At 6.83%, Pushed Up By Middle East Bond Yields And A Hawkish Fed; Blackstone Bought $36 Billion In Australian Mortgages; Locally, 729 Homes Remain Buyable Across Our Five Cities

Rates went the wrong way. The daily 30-year fixed climbed 6 basis points to 6.83% and the 15-year rose a point to 6.32%, putting the 30-year at its highest level in a year. Rising bond yields out of the Middle East tensions plus a Fed that held while sounding hawkish are doing the pushing.
That matters more than the number itself. High prices had already sidelined millions of buyers, and every tick higher thins the pool further. If you have a buyer sitting on a pre approval written at 6.5%, this is the week to re run their payment and have the honest conversation before they find a house they cannot close on.
Big money is buying mortgages, just not here. Blackstone picked up $36 billion in Australian home loans from HSBC, which is slimming down under new leadership. Blackstone is deliberately diversifying away from the U.S. after new regulations landed on large real estate investors. When the biggest alternative asset manager on the planet goes shopping overseas, it is worth noticing what that says about the domestic return picture.
Why a REALTOR® cares: institutional money stepping back from U.S. residential is not automatically bad news for your buyers. Less institutional competition on entry level inventory is exactly the opening a first time buyer needs, and locally we just added 5 more buyable homes.

The market flipped. One day after its biggest selloff in about a year, Wall Street rebounded across the board: the NASDAQ jumped 2.78% to 25,122, the S&P 500 added 1.66% to 7,437, and the DOW gained 1.19% to 52,208. Chip stocks and Microsoft led the bounce.
Earnings were a split decision. Amazon beat expectations as its AWS cloud business boomed on AI demand and the stock rose 3.90%. Apple, the world's most valuable company, missed as App Store and subscription revenue slowed, and shares eased 1.41%. Reddit beat on revenue but its CEO warned that search traffic coming from Google has turned unreliable. Roblox fell 2.91% on fewer players, with the company noting no new hit game has replaced last year's titles.
Oracle was the day's standout, up 8.34% on a deal to let customers run Google's Gemini AI inside its business software. The bet is that offering a choice between its own AI and Google's keeps customers from leaving, and it puts new features in front of millions of users without making them learn new software.

Gold, silver, and Brent are live quotes taken the morning of July 31 (change versus the same time a day earlier); the 10-Year, stocks, and crypto are as of the July 30 close. Commodities can move fast intraday, so treat these as a same day snapshot.
Growth stalls while inflation sticks. The Bureau of Economic Analysis put second quarter growth at 1.5% in its advance estimate, down from 2.1% in the first quarter, while inflation ran at 3.7%, well above the Fed's 2% target. Slower growth plus stubborn prices is the hard combination, and it is exactly why three Fed officials voted to hike this week.
The chart below shows the full five year run. The 7.0% reopening surge in late 2021 is long gone, and the last four quarters have swung from 4.4% down to 0.5%, back up to 2.1%, and now to 1.5%. Only two quarters in the whole stretch were negative. BEA credits the slowdown to a downturn in government spending plus decelerating investment and exports, partly offset by consumers who actually spent more.
Small businesses are feeling it first. Merchant cash advances, the expensive kind of financing, were used by 12% of small firms in 2025, up from 9% a year earlier. Tariffs are squeezing cash flow and many of these businesses cannot qualify for cheaper bank loans. If the Fed does hike in September, those payments get worse.
Why a REALTOR® cares: 1.5% growth with 3.7% inflation is the setup that keeps mortgage rates elevated without giving buyers the wage gains to absorb them. It also hits the self employed borrower first, and in this market that is a meaningful share of the buyer pool.
Second quarter growth came in at 1.5%, down from 2.1% in the first quarter. Only two quarters in the past five years were negative, and the 7.0% reopening surge of late 2021 is long behind us.

Crypto rode the rebound. Bitcoin added 1.54% to $64,869 for a third straight day of gains, Ether rose 1.21% to $1,921, and XRP held near $1.08. Total crypto market value climbed about 1.35% to $2.21 trillion, helped along by the Fed leaving rates alone.
A scam worth warning clients about. Fraudsters are mailing fake IRS letters carrying QR codes that lead to a site built to drain a crypto wallet. Crypto scam losses hit $11 billion in 2025. The rule is simple: never scan a QR code that arrives unsolicited in the mail or shows up on a sticker in public.
Coinbase had a rough quarter. Falling prices meant less trading, and fewer trades means fewer fees, which is a large slice of its revenue. The bright spot was subscription revenue, which actually grew during the quarter, and the stock still finished up 2.18%.
