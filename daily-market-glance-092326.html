Rates went the wrong way this morning. Mortgage News Daily has the 30-year at 7.26% as of today, up 0.09, and the 15-year at 6.87%, up 0.04. Both are now at the very top of their 52 week range, so this is the most expensive day to lock a rate in a year. The newsletter printed 7.17% and 6.83%, which were yesterday's readings, so the move is newer than the numbers most people read at breakfast.
The cause is in the bond market. The 10-Year closed Tuesday at 4.96%, unchanged on the day, but it pushed to 5.12% by 11:35 AM PT this morning, and MND says mortgage backed securities are significantly weaker. Mortgage rates follow that lead in hours, not days.
Today's national story is about how buyers are stretching to get in the door. ResiClub, working with AEI Housing Center data, looked at which homebuilders lean on FHA loans, the government backed mortgage that allows as little as 3.5% down. At LGI Homes, a starter home builder whose average sale is $367,407, 53% of 2025 buyers used one. At Toll Brothers, where the average sale is $996,400, just 3% did. The split is not strategy, it is price point. The newsletter's graphic also showed Ashton Woods at 39%, and we left that bar out because we could not source it anywhere the number was actually published.
So what does FHA reach here? We ran it on today's board. FHA's 2026 limit for a one unit home in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and Milpitas carries the same Santa Clara County ceiling. Measured against that limit, 413 of our 705 live listings, or 59%, are priced inside it. Hayward is the wide door at 80%. Fremont is exactly half, 126 of 253. Union City is the tightest at 45%. FHA is not a fringe product in this market, it is a door into about six of every ten homes on our board.
• Board wide: 2,701 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 206 coming soon and 158 brand new.

What this means for you: the ledger held flat at 705, but coming soon jumped to 95 from 84, so next week's supply is already stacking up. Buyers: if you are FHA, today's 52 week high in rates costs more than the loan type does, and the 30-year FHA rate is 6.87%, a full 0.39 under the conventional 30-year. Run both. Sellers: 171 homes on our board have been sitting 60 days or longer, and every tick up in rates shrinks the pool that can still afford your price. Price to today's rate, not to August's.
FHA reliance tracks price point, not strategy. The starter home builder runs on it, the luxury builder barely touches it. We dropped the Ashton Woods bar from the original graphic because the 39% figure could not be sourced.

Bases differ today and are stated on purpose. The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve close for Tuesday, September 22, unchanged from Monday. Brent is the ICE front month settlement for Tuesday. Gold and silver are live COMEX front month quotes at 11:35 AM PT Wednesday against Tuesday's settlement, because the hourly data we normally use for the 1:00 PM PT print was unavailable all morning.
One number drove everything today. The 10-Year Treasury yield pushed to 5.12% by late morning, up about 15 basis points from Tuesday's 4.96% close, and the move rippled straight through. Mortgage rates jumped, stocks fell, and metals were hit hardest.
Gold is down 1.20% to $4,324 and silver down 2.23% to $65.05 as of 11:35 AM PT. Both had been grinding higher for weeks, and both sold off as yields rose, which is the textbook reaction: metals pay no interest, so they look worse every time bonds pay more.
Brent settled Tuesday at $99.25, down 1.09%, its second straight decline from last week's run above $100. The newsletter printed Brent at $98.82 and called it a 0.43% fall; the front month settlement says $99.25 and 1.09%. It also put the 10-Year at 4.95% and down 0.01, where the Treasury curve says 4.96% and unchanged. Small gaps, but they are the two numbers that set mortgage pricing, so we go with the primary sources.
