REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.27% -0.02  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.92% -0.03

America's biggest builder expects to sell less. D.R. Horton reported Thursday that orders fell 7% last quarter and cancellations rose to 23% from 20%. It expects 79,000 to 84,000 closings next year, after 84,202 this year, and says incentives will stay elevated.

Here at home. D.R. Horton's Red Tag sale covers Bay Area homes and ends Sunday. Nobody runs a Red Tag event when homes are selling themselves. On our own MLS, 33 of the 599 homes on the market in our five cities were built in 2025 or later, 20 of them townhomes, and they have been listed a median 52 days against 36 for older homes.

Rates got a small break. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.27%, from 7.29%, the lowest since late September and well below this month's 7.61% peak. The 15-year is 6.92%. The 30-year is still nearly a full point above a year ago, and this week's inflation report argues against counting on much more.

On Tuesday's ballot. California's Prop. 37 would let the state housing finance agency sell up to $25 billion in bonds to fund loans of up to 17% of the price for buyers of newly built homes who put at least 3% down and earn no more than double their area's typical income. The Legislative Analyst's Office estimates no direct state or local costs.

What it means for you. Treat the builder's offer as the number to beat. If you are looking at new homes, ask what the incentive is worth in rate and in price, then compare it with what a resale seller will give. If you are selling a newer home, you are up against that offer directly, so price against it, not against last spring.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,580.07 -1.82%  |  Dow: 51,770.31 -1.09%  |  Nasdaq: 25,437.00 -2.86%

A hard Friday, led by chips. Intel fell 28.6%, its worst day since at least 1980, after forecasting fourth quarter revenue of $15.0 billion to $16.0 billion when Wall Street expected about $17 billion. It is still up 78% this year. Nvidia fell 4.2%. The numbers above are Friday's closes.

Amazon's cash goes negative. Over the past 12 months Amazon took in $168.3 billion from operations and spent $210.7 billion on property and equipment, up $94.8 billion in a year, mainly for AI. That left free cash flow at negative $42.4 billion. The business itself is strong: AWS sales rose 39% and the operating margin hit a record 13.9%. The question for every AI spender is the same: when does the cash come back?

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 4.94% -0.04  |  Gold: $3,894.20 -3.45%  |  Silver: $49.40 -5.41%  |  Brent Crude: $117.64 +2.40%

The economy is running hot. GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter, and the PCE price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, rose 0.5% in September and 3.6% from a year ago. Core rose 0.3% and 3.1%. Hot growth and hot prices are what keep mortgage rates high, and two Fed officials dissented this week in favor of a hike. The next meeting is in December.

Gold and silver slide. The 10-Year closed at 4.94%, from 4.98%, as stocks fell. Gold dropped to its lowest since early October 2025 and silver closed under $50 as traders weighed the risk of another hike. Brent's December contract expired at $117.64; January, now the front month, is near $112.40. The numbers above are Friday's Treasury close, COMEX December gold and silver at the 4 PM ET print, and the final ICE December Brent settlement.

CRYPTOCURRENCY

Bitcoin: $76,946 -4.12%  |  Ethereum: $2,239.92 -3.06%  |  XRP: $1.25 -1.64%

The biggest crypto hack on record. Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, was hit Thursday. It puts the loss at about $6.3 billion, more than four times the old record of $1.5 billion at Bybit in 2025, says about $3 billion has been frozen, and says it will cover all losses. Bitcoin is down 4.12% over 24 hours, Ether 3.06% and XRP 1.64%, live from CoinGecko at 1:09 PM PT.

Coinbase slides. It fell 10.6% to $143.54 the day after its earnings and is now down 37% this year. Most of its $719 million profit was gains on crypto it holds; adjusted net income was $78 million, and revenue fell 35% to $1.22 billion.
