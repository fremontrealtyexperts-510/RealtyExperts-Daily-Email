Mortgage rates eased a little. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.32%, down 0.02, with the 15-year at 6.87%. On today's $1,199,999 Fremont median with 20% down, that is about $6,595 a month in principal and interest.
Who owns America's real estate? The Federal Reserve's latest data, for the second quarter of 2026, puts the value of homes owned and lived in by households at $49.8 trillion, and Baby Boomers hold $20.5 trillion of it, 41.3% (chart). Gen X holds 28.7%, Millennials and younger 22.4% (up from 11.5% at the end of 2019), and the Silent generation 7.6%. The version going around uses the fourth quarter of 2025; the Fed has published two quarters since.
Locally the Census Bureau counts homes rather than dollars (chart). Averaging 2020 to 2024, about 31,700 of the 115,000 owner-occupied homes in our five cities had a householder 65 or older, and about 58,000, roughly half, had one 55 or older. Union City runs oldest at 31.6% aged 65 or older, then Hayward 30.3%, Milpitas 26.5%, Fremont 25.9% and Newark 22.8%, all below the national 33.0%.
That handoff is already showing up. Cotality data, as reported by Sherwood News in March, put inherited homes at about 17.5% of California property transfers in 2025, a record and about double the rate in the rest of the country. Some heirs move in, some rent, and some list.
Tuesday's export shows 2,207 board wide, from 2,244 Monday, with 132 coming soon and 79 brand new. The five city ledger is 562, from 560 Monday: Fremont (195), Hayward (162) and Union City (54) each added one, Milpitas (84) lost one, and Newark (67) held.
What this means for you: owners 55 and older can carry their property tax base to a replacement home anywhere in California under Prop. 19, up to three times, which can make a downsize pencil. Families inheriting a home should know Prop. 19 also narrowed the parent to child tax break. Run the numbers with a tax professional before deciding to keep or sell.
