Mortgage rates eased from a near three year high. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year was 7.54% on Thursday, down 0.06 from Wednesday's 7.60%, and the 15-year is 7.18%. On our five city median of $1,079,500 with 20% down, principal and interest come to $6,062 a month, $36 less than Wednesday and still $380 more than when September began at 6.89%.
This morning's jobs report is the kind of news that can move rates. Employers added 29,000 jobs in September, unemployment rose to 4.2%, and July and August were revised down by a combined 60,000. The 10-Year Treasury, which mortgage rates tend to follow, was near 5.24% at 8:00 AM PT, about where it closed Thursday, so the report has not moved rates yet.
Locally, the five city ledger edged up to 679 from 675 and the board wide table rose 28 to 2,700, with 189 brand new listings, up from 166 on Thursday.
What this means for you: buyers who stepped back at 7.60% should keep a preapproval current, because on the median home every quarter point off the rate is worth about $147 a month. Sellers should price for today's rates, not tomorrow's; 140 of the ledger's 604 homes on the market have been listed 60 days or longer.

All four are Thursday, October 1: the Treasury daily yield curve close, the COMEX December gold and silver settlements, and the ICE December Brent settlement, compared with December's own Wednesday settle. Friday is live: at 8:00 AM PT the 10-Year was near 5.24%, gold $4,198.00 (-0.10%), silver $61.41 (+0.38%) and December Brent $100.25 (-2.01%).
Today's second chart started as the ISM factory survey making the rounds: its index was 54.5 in September, the ninth straight month of growth, with the prices gauge jumping to 77.9 from 71.1. ISM does not allow charts built from its index, so we drew the same story from public jobs data. Factories added 9,000 jobs in September, a fourth straight monthly gain, and 72,000 since December.
The local picture splits. Factory jobs in the San Jose metro rose 1.4% in the year to August, while the Oakland-Fremont area, which covers Alameda and Contra Costa counties, lost 5.5%. Its 93,300 factory jobs are the fewest for an August since 2016, down from 113,500 at the September 2022 peak.
The wider jobs report was soft: hourly pay rose 3.0% in a year, below the 3.4% rise in consumer prices. In Europe, today's flash estimate put euro area inflation at 3.8% in September, up from 3.2%, led by energy at 18.8%. December gold settled up 0.37% and silver up 1.01%. Brent's December contract jumped 4.37% Thursday to $102.31 and is giving back part of it this morning.
U.S. factory jobs have risen four months in a row, but the Oakland-Fremont area, Alameda and Contra Costa counties, has 5.5% fewer than a year ago.
Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Current Employment Statistics, national through September and metro through August 2026.
