REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.42% +0.12  |  15-Year Fixed: 7.00% +0.08

Home sales sink to a 16 year low. Existing home sales ran at a 3.75 million annual pace in October, the slowest since August 2010 and down 8.8% from a year ago, the National Association of Realtors said Thursday. That is 39% below 2021's 6.12 million and under the 4.06 million of both 2024 and 2025. Unsold inventory rose 4.6% from a year ago to 1.59 million homes, a 5.1 month supply, and the national median edged up 0.4% to $416,700.

Contracts are slowing here too. Across the 14 cities in both of our November 13 MLS exports, homes in contract fell to 596 from 838 a year ago, down 29%, while homes on the market rose 2.5% to 1,715. In Fremont, contracts fell to 64 from 101. A year ago there were two listings for every home in contract; today there are nearly three.

Rates hold at Thursday's reading. Mortgage News Daily's latest reading, Thursday's, has the 30-year at 7.42% and the 15-year at 7.00%. Freddie Mac's survey averaged 7.33% in October, up from 6.25% a year earlier, NAR noted. At 7.42%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,279,000 median with 20% down would pay about $7,098 a month in principal and interest.

What it means for you. With nearly three listings for every contract, buyers have time and room to ask, so ask for a credit toward a rate buydown, not just a lower price. Sellers, price to what went into contract in the last six weeks, not to the spring. The market is not closed, it is just reading the fine print.

Today's count. Board wide: 2,419 listings across the 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, up from 2,412, with 141 coming soon and 119 brand new. The five city ledger carries 623 live listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, 556 on the market and 67 coming soon, down from 629.

Fremont holds 216 live listings: 168 active, 31 coming soon, 16 brand new and 1 back on the market, with a median on market list price of $1,279,000 at $787 a square foot and a typical listing out 36 days. Hayward carries 179, Milpitas 96, Newark 73 and Union City 59. Across all five cities the median is $999,994 at $666 a square foot, 279 homes are priced under a million, and 183 have been sitting 60 days or longer.

Nvidia erases its 2026 gains. The Santa Clara chipmaker closed Thursday at $166.19, down 4.34% on the day and 10.9% below its $186.50 close on the last day of 2025, after its board accepted chief executive Jensen Huang's resignation. That is its lowest close since March 30 and 30.5% below its October 6 high of $239.24. As of 8:29 AM PT Friday it is trading near $160.18, down 3.62%.

The selling spreads through the AI trade. Cloud provider Nebius fell 16.84% Thursday after its quarterly report and is down 10.03% Friday, and the Philadelphia semiconductor index is down 5.07%. Thursday's index losses were modest, and the numbers above are Thursday's closes; Friday is the rougher session, with the Nasdaq down 1.46% and the S&P 500 down 0.85% as of 8:29 AM PT.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 5.10% +0.12  |  Gold: $3,885.30 -2.88%  |  Silver: $50.07 -3.72%  |  Brent Crude: $95.82 +3.30%

The 10-Year closes above 5%. The 10-Year Treasury yield closed Thursday at 5.10%, up 0.12 from Tuesday's 4.98% and its highest close since October 21. Market Briefs printed 5.12%, up 0.02; Treasury's own close is 5.10%.

The Fed trims its 2027 calendar. The Federal Reserve revised its 2027 schedule Thursday. The October 26 to 27 meeting will be set aside for broad monetary policy strategy with no rate decision, and the December meeting moves up to November 16 to 17, leaving seven rate decisions next year instead of eight. The next decision is at this year's December meeting.

Metals fall, oil rises. Gold settled Thursday at $3,885.30, down 2.88%, and silver at $50.07, down 3.72%; Market Briefs' $3,919 gold is an overnight trade, not the settlement. Brent settled at $95.82, up 3.30%. Live at 8:29 AM PT Friday, gold is $3,880.40 (-0.13%), silver $48.93 (-2.27%) and Brent $96.44 (+0.65%). Yields and oil up, metals down: not the combination a homebuyer would order.
