Mortgage Rates Hold Near 6.65% As Fed-Hike Bets Firm; Congress Passes The 21st Century ROAD To Housing Act; The East Bay Board Eases To 2,895 Active On 175 New And 223 Closed
The 30-year fixed held at 6.65% and the 15-year at 6.19%, with rates sticky as bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike firm up. That is still close to double the rate most homeowners already have, which keeps the lock-in effect firmly in place.
The big housing news is out of Washington. Congress passed the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, and President Trump is expected to sign it today. The bill opens up $200 million for areas that successfully add housing supply, unlocks funding for manufactured and factory-built homes, caps big investors at 350 single-family homes, and speeds up building permits.
A related shift: small-time investors now make up 63% of all investor home purchases, the highest share in 15 years. Investors bought 11.3% of all U.S. homes in 2025, but the mega investors who buy 350 or more homes have pulled back hard, down nearly 70% from their 2021 peak. The small operator, not the giant fund, is the investor buyer in most markets now.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,895 active (down 34 from yesterday), with 175 new listings against 223 closings. Oakland again led the board with 47 new on 770 active; Fremont added 15 new on 350 active.

Stocks closed in the red. The S&P 500 fell 1.44% to 7,365 and the NASDAQ dropped 2.21% to 25,587, while the DOW slipped just 0.09% to 51,666 (figures as of the June 23rd close).
AI memory chip names led the slide. Micron (MU -13.18%) and SanDisk (SNDK -13.63%) both tumbled double digits. Micron reports earnings today and is now the 13th most valuable company in the world, so its print will set the tone for the group.
There was some green under the red. Cerebras (CBRS +1.02%) posted its first earnings since going public, with revenue topping $194 million, double a year ago. The stock still fell about 10% after hours, as the company guided margins down to 36 to 38 percent from 46.5 percent. It also signed a deal worth more than $20 billion to supply OpenAI and will put its chips inside AWS.
SpaceX (SPCX +0.98%) sold $25 billion in bonds, days after its long-awaited IPO, to help pay off a loan it took in March. The company is sitting on more than $100 billion in cash but has still never turned a profit.

10-Year and Brent as of June 23rd (Market Briefs); gold and silver are live spot, June 24th (Fortune); the U.S. Dollar Index is the ICE DXY, June 24th.
The dollar is feeling green. While stocks closed in the red, the U.S. Dollar Index pushed to a 13-month high near 101.6, up about 3.4% on the year (see the chart below). The driver is the Federal Reserve. Wall Street now puts the odds of a rate hike by September at roughly 85%, and higher rates make dollars scarcer and more valuable, so money flows in.
That same Fed bet hammered the metals. Gold fell about 3% to roughly $3,988 an ounce and silver dropped more than 5% to about $58.78, both sliding to multi-month lows as a stronger dollar and a higher for longer Fed pulled money out. Longer term, central banks are still on a record gold-buying run, but day to day the Fed is in the driver's seat.
Oil kept easing. Brent crude slipped 2.26% to $75.36 as supply flows back. Crude is moving through the Strait of Hormuz again, and the UAE has already restored exports to 85% of pre-war levels. The 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.48%.
The takeaway for housing: a strong dollar and cheaper oil help cool inflation, but a Fed leaning toward higher for longer is exactly what keeps mortgage rates sticky.
The dollar is feeling green. The U.S. Dollar Index has climbed to a 13-month high at 101.64, up 3.42% on the year, as Wall Street bets the Fed hikes rates by September. A stronger dollar is also what pulled gold and silver lower the same day.
