Builders are the gloomiest they have been in 14 years. The NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index fell three points to 29 in October, the lowest since June 2012 and below even the 30 of April 2020, when the pandemic hit. Builders in the West, which includes California, are the gloomiest of the four regions at 26 on NAHB's three month average.
Buyer traffic fell to 18, the weakest since April 2012 outside of that pandemic month, and 43% of builders cut prices in October, the highest share at any point since 2020. The average cut held at 6%, and 65% offered sales incentives. NAHB points to mortgage rates above 7%, higher material, energy and labor costs, and buyers who feel less sure of the economy.
Our own MLS shows the same cooling. We pulled one export a month for 2026 and tracked homes built in 2025 or 2026. From January to June, 33% to 36% of the new homes on our 15 city board were in contract at any time, more than resale. Since July it has run 21% to 28%, and today it is 22%, or 31 of 140, against 26% for resale. Meanwhile new homes for sale grew to 109 from 75 in January.
A year apart, the drop is plain: across the 14 cities in both exports, new homes in contract fell to 25 from 45, with about the same 100 for sale. Closer to home it is firmer but cooling. In Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, 19 of 52 new homes are in contract, about 37%, still ahead of resale at 25% but down from about half in January through March. Most of the board's unsold new homes are in the Tri-Valley, where only 10 of 73 are in contract.
Builders here are protecting list prices. Of the homes for sale both a month ago and today, about one in four new homes has cut its price (19 of 77), against one in three resale homes (365 of 1,099).
Permits have not turned yet. Through August, the nine Bay Area counties issued permits for 11,507 homes, 22% more than a year earlier, about 60% of them in buildings of five or more units. Alameda County's single family permits rose 7% to 689. Today's survey is October's mood, so the fall permit numbers are the ones to watch.
Locally, the five city ledger slipped to 680 from 681, with Fremont up to 229 from 227 and Newark down to 92 from 94. The board wide table eased to 2,631 from 2,651, and brand new listings fell to 178 from 198.
What this means for you: new construction is where buyers have the most leverage right now, and it is the one corner of the market where the seller has a sales target. Ask the builder about a rate buydown or closing cost credit and weigh it against a price cut. On the five city median of $1,080,000 with 20% down, principal and interest runs about $6,000 a month at 7.43%, and a permanent buydown to 6.43% would cut that by about $580. Sellers near a new community should price to the comps and expect buyers to ask what the builder down the street is offering.

The government's construction data matched the builders' mood. Housing starts ran at a 1.221 million annual pace in September, 5.2% below August and 7.4% below a year ago, the Census Bureau said this morning, though both changes are inside the survey's margin of error. Single family starts fell 4.6% to an 859,000 pace, and permits, the pipeline for next year, slipped to 1.40 million, 3.0% below a year ago. For builders, the mood and the math finally agree.
Markets eased a little this morning. The 10-Year traded near 5.11% at 8:36 AM PT after Monday's 5.15% close, Brent fell about 1.2% to near $108.25 after settling at $109.55, and silver rose about 1.2% while gold held about flat.
