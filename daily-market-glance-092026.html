Same number of sellers, slower sales: the Tri-City inventory build is a drain story, not a supply story.

Mortgage rates finished the week almost exactly where they started. Mortgage News Daily had the 30-year at 7.20% and the 15-year at 6.83% as of Friday, September 18, and nothing reprices over a weekend. The 10-Year Treasury closed Friday at 5.01%, which is the highest a 10-Year has been since July 2007.
Today we want to spend the space on something more useful than a rate that did not move. Harv pulled twenty months of inventory for Fremont, Newark and Union City, and the three cities are not telling the same story.
Start with the floor rather than the peak. Every year inventory drains through the winter and rebuilds through the spring, so the summer peak is the number everyone quotes and the January trough is the number that actually tells you what carried over. Fremont started 2025 with 150 homes for sale and started 2026 with 216, a 44% higher floor. Newark went from 50 to 57. Union City went from 51 to 50, which is to say it did not build at all.
Now the part that surprised us. Across the three cities, 1,699 new listings came to market from January through August this year against 1,708 over the same eight months last year, a difference of half a percent. The faucet is running at the same rate it always was. What changed is how fast the tub drains.
• What that means in practice: the extra inventory you are seeing in Fremont is not a wave of new sellers. It is the same number of sellers, with their homes staying on the market longer.
• Why Union City looks different: it is the one city of the three that cleared what arrived. Today its typical listing has been out 24 days against Fremont's 30.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Board wide: 2,768 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 190 coming soon and 237 brand new.
• Five city ledger: 716 live listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City. 632 are already on the market and 84 are coming soon.
• Fremont: 255 live, 201 active, 36 coming soon, 18 brand new. The median on market list price is $1,200,000 at $794 a square foot, and the typical active listing has been out 30 days.
• Newark: 91 live, 79 on the market, median $1,198,000 at $685 a square foot, typical listing out 24 days, and only 12 homes sitting 60 days or longer.
• Union City: 68 live, 61 on the market, median $1,250,000 at $767 a square foot, typical listing out 24 days. The highest median of the three, on the smallest inventory.
• The rest of the ledger: Hayward 187, Milpitas 115.
• Across all five cities the median is $1,099,000 at $682 a square foot. 283 homes are priced under a million, and 161 have been sitting 60 days or longer.
• The local math: at Fremont's median list price of $1,200,000, a 20% down loan is $960,000, under Alameda County's $1,249,125 high-balance conforming limit.
• At 7.20%, principal and interest run about $6,516 a month, and every eighth of a point is about $81.
What this means for you: if you are listing in Fremont this fall you are not competing with more sellers, you are competing with sellers who have already been out there a while and are getting tired. That is a pricing conversation, not a timing one. In Newark and Union City the standing inventory is genuinely thinner, so a well priced home still gets its weekend. Sellers: price to the 7.2% a buyer is actually quoted, not to the rate anybody hoped for in June.
Fremont started 2026 with 44% more homes on the market than it started 2025, while its summer peak barely moved. Newark built a smaller floor. Union City built none at all.

The 10-Year Treasury closed Friday at 5.01%. That is the highest close since July 19, 2007, which we checked against the Treasury's own daily yield curve rather than taking a newsletter's word for it. It put gold "near $4,350"; gold actually settled Friday at $4,424.90. And its "record near $5,600 in January" is an intraday tick from January 29, not a close. Gold's record close is $5,318.40 from the same day, which is still a long way above where it sits now.
