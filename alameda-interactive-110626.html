REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.17% -0.05  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.80% -0.05

Rates eased in the selloff. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average finished Thursday at 7.17%, down 0.05, and the 15-year at 6.80%, also down 0.05; Friday's reading was not posted as of 9:33 AM PT. Rates improved through Thursday as money left stocks for Treasury bonds. At 7.17%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,299,000 median with 20% down would pay about $7,033 a month in principal and interest, $312 less than at the 7.61% of a month ago.

The counterweight is jobs. Friday's strong jobs report (below) is the kind of news that usually lifts rates, yet the 10-Year was nearly unchanged at 4.85% this morning, about where it closed Thursday.

What it means for you. The 0.05 drop is worth about $35 a month on the median Fremont home, so if you are close to locking, ask your lender whether today's jobs news has moved their rate sheet. Buyers counting on company stock for a down payment saw Thursday how fast that number can change; talk with your lender about when to move it to cash. Sellers, 165 homes in our five cities have been listed 60 days or longer.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,353.73 -4.36%  |  Dow: 51,523.06 -3.44%  |  Nasdaq: 24,315.00 -5.52%

Thursday was the worst day for stocks since April 2025. The S&P 500 fell 4.36%, the Nasdaq 5.52% and the Dow 3.44%, or 1,833 points, more than erasing Wednesday's post election rally. The Nasdaq now sits 11.9% below its October 6 record, a correction. The selling started in chips: the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 12.25%, its worst day since March 2020, on worries that the election results could slow the building of AI data centers.

It hit close to home. Fremont's Lam Research fell 16.4%, and Milpitas neighbors KLA and Sandisk fell 13.3% and 22.3%; AMD dropped 17.3% and Nvidia 9.3%, both based in Santa Clara. Friday is calmer: as of 9:33 AM PT the Nasdaq is up 1.38%, the S&P 500 up 0.74% and the Dow flat, and Lam has won back 8.6%. One bad day does not make a trend, but it did make a lot of Bay Area stock grants feel smaller.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 4.84% -0.06  |  Gold: $4,113.50 +0.99%  |  Silver: $52.38 +0.18%  |  Brent Crude: $98.29 +3.88%

Hiring beat forecasts. Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, more than double the monthly average of the past year (about 74,000), and unemployment slipped to 4.1% from 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said Friday. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% for the month and 3.1% over the year.

Layoff announcements cooled too. Employers announced 43,994 job cuts in October, Challenger, Gray & Christmas reported Thursday, down 71% from 153,074 a year earlier and the fewest for an October since 2023. California still led all states with 10,032, down 29% from a year ago, and technology, at 171,501 so far this year, is the industry with the most cuts in 2026.

Bonds rallied, oil rebounded. Treasury has the 10-Year closing Thursday at 4.84%, down 0.06, as money moved from stocks to bonds. Brent settled at $98.29, up 3.88% after Wednesday's 8.67% drop, gold at $4,113.50 and silver at $52.38; the numbers above are Thursday's settlements and Treasury's close.
