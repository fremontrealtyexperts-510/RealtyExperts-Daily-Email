REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.33% 0.00  |  15-Year Fixed: 7.01% +0.02

Buyers are pausing, not leaving. The Mortgage Bankers Association's weekly survey, out this morning, shows mortgage applications up 3.4% in the week ending October 23 as its 30-year contract rate eased to 7.37% from 7.41%. Even with that bounce, home purchase applications were 18% below the same week last year, and refinances were 61% lower.

Rates explain most of it. Early this year, with that rate near 6.2%, purchase applications ran as much as 18% ahead of 2025. Then rates rose every week for two months, reaching 7.50% in early October, the highest since November 2023, and purchase applications have trailed last year in each of the past seven weeks. More borrowers are choosing adjustable rate loans, now 11.3% of applications, the most in over a year.

Here at home. The newest full year of federal lending data (HMDA) shows Alameda County lenders took 13,940 home purchase loan applications in 2025, down 6.9% from 2024 even as the nation edged up 1.1%, and about half the 2021 peak. Our own MLS shows the same pause today: on this date a year apart, across the same 14 cities, homes in contract fell 26% to 609 from 820, while homes for sale barely moved, 1,957 from 1,989.

What it means for you. If you are buying, get pre-approved now so you can move when rates dip; this week showed how fast buyers come back on even a small one. If a lender offers an adjustable rate loan, make sure you understand how and when the rate can change. If you are selling, price for today's payment from day one: inventory is about where it was a year ago, but a quarter fewer homes are going into contract.
