Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.70% As New York Pauses Data Center Buildouts For A Year; The East Bay Board Slips To 2,738 Active On 186 New And 203 Closed
The 30-year fixed eased to 6.70% and the 15-year held at 6.20%, a touch lower on the week. Rates in this range keep the lock-in effect firmly in place, so most owners stay put and resale supply stays tight.
The policy headline sits at the intersection of housing and power bills: New York became the first state to pause data center buildouts for a year, after electricity prices there jumped nearly 68% since 2019. With tech giants planning about $500 billion in data center spending in 2026, the fight over who pays for the power (and how it shows up on home electric bills) is only getting started.
Locally, the East Bay board slipped to 2,738 active, down from 2,759, on 186 new listings and 203 closings, one of the first days in a while with more homes going under contract than coming to market. Oakland again led by a wide margin with 55 new on 713 active, followed by Fremont at 26 and Danville at 16.

Stocks ground higher, with the NASDAQ up 0.90% to 26,107, the S&P 500 up 0.38% to 7,543, and the DOW essentially flat at 52,508. The tape was steadier than the headlines, because the day belonged to one name.
IBM (IBM -25.21%) had its worst day since 1987, tumbling about 25% (see today's chart). It missed quarterly earnings and warned results could get worse, and investors who had bet on its AI and chip business scaling up got the opposite message.
The big banks told a happier story. JPMorgan (JPM +2.50%) said AI has let it cut roughly 40% of roles in some departments, Bank of America (BAC +1.88%) posted $3.3 billion from its consumer division on strong spending, and Goldman Sachs (GS +9.00%) rode record stock trading and announced a $10 billion bond sale.
IBM had its worst day since 1987, closing at $217.07, down 26.72% year to date after an earnings miss.

Inflation is back in the spotlight, and so is the Fed. New Chair Kevin Warsh told the House that the central bank will not back down on inflation, calling high prices a real burden on American households. With a rate decision due later this month, a growing number of investors now expect at least one more hike in 2026. Headline CPI is running about 3.5%, still well above the Fed's 2% target and stubbornly sticky after the 2022 spike (see today's chart).
There was a reminder of how the world still finances America. Foreign holdings of U.S. Treasuries climbed to a near-record $9.37 trillion, with Canada alone adding $38.7 billion, as the dollar keeps its safe-haven crown. Steady foreign demand helps hold down U.S. borrowing costs, which feeds through to mortgage and credit card rates.
In the markets, the 10-year Treasury held at 4.59%. Gold held near $4,061 while silver eased 0.9% to about $58.45. Brent crude jumped 2.55% to $85.42 after President Trump dropped his proposed Strait of Hormuz toll but the blockade stayed in force.
Headline CPI inflation is running about 3.5%, still above the Fed's 2% target after the 2022 spike.
Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (CPI-U, not seasonally adjusted).

Crypto had a strong day, with Bitcoin up 3.62% to $64,517, Ether up 5.74% to $1,874, and XRP up 4.11% to $1.11.
One theme was crypto muscling into betting. Blockchain.com teamed up with Polymarket to let users wager on World Cup matches inside its app, as crypto firms chase a slice of a projected $10 billion World Cup betting frenzy and prediction markets keep gaining ground.
The quieter theme was silence. Mentions of Bitcoin on X fell to roughly 130,000 last month, the fewest since 2020. Retail traders have stepped back and big institutions have stepped in, so the market has cooled from its highs even as the plumbing keeps getting built out.
