Friday's selloff pulled mortgage rates down with Treasury yields. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average fell 0.12 to 7.33%, with the 15-year at 6.89%, and Freddie Mac's weekly survey on Thursday was 7.28%. On today's $1,242,321 Fremont median with 20% down, that is about $6,834 a month in principal and interest.
Realtor.com's October rental report shows the median asking rent for 0 to 2 bedroom units in the 50 largest metros at $1,692, up 0.1% from a year ago, the first annual increase since June 2023. Here the climb is steeper (chart): Zillow's rent index has Milpitas up 9.4%, Union City 7.1%, Fremont 6.8%, Newark 5.5% and Hayward 3.9%, against 2.9% nationally on the same measure.
In realtor.com's latest weekly data, homes for sale are up 8.3% from a year ago, new listings are down 10.3%, and the median list price is down 1.2%, the 43rd straight week below last year. Its new construction report finds builders holding their prices while existing home prices slip.
Here, supply is up 3% from this date last year across the 14 cities in both of our exports (1,710 from 1,659), while homes in contract are down 29% (567 from 803). New homes are the ones giving ground: the board's median new listing (built 2025 or 2026) asks $1,249,000, down from $1,382,750 for the newest homes a year ago, while resale rose to $899,694 from $825,000. Part of that is mix, since many new homes are townhomes.
Saturday's export shows 2,262 board wide, from 2,313 Thursday, with 125 coming soon and 102 brand new. The five city ledger is 565, from 589 Thursday: Hayward (161) fell nine, Milpitas (88) six, and Fremont (194), Newark (69) and Union City (53) three each.
What this means for you: renters, local rents are rising faster than the national headline, so price out a purchase before your lease renews. Buyers, rates just eased and fewer people are signing contracts, so there is room to negotiate.

Friday's close: the S&P 500 fell 3.70%, the Dow 1.93% and the Nasdaq 5.28%, after Thursday's drop of about 2% across the board. For the week the Nasdaq lost 10.0% and the Dow 8.0%, both their worst weeks since March 2020, and the S&P 500 lost 8.6%, its worst since April 2025 (chart). The S&P 500 is now 11.6% below its October 6 record and the Dow 11.4% below its August 5 record, both in correction territory, and the Nasdaq is 18.1% off its high and below where it started the year.
Chips led the fall. Santa Clara's Nvidia dropped 14.01% to $125.53 on reports that SEC staff are examining its accounting, which neither the SEC nor Nvidia has confirmed; it lost 22.9% on the week. AMD fell 13.25% and Broadcom 12.19%, and the VIX fear gauge closed at 35.89, its highest since April 2025.
