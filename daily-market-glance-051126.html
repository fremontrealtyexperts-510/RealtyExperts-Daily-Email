Builders Cut New-Home Prices To 5-Year Low As 9-In-10 Voters Call Buying Harder Than Ever
Two national signals worth carrying into Bay Area conversations this week. New home prices fell to a five-year low in March, with 682,000 new homes sold and many now getting price cuts. That's the new-construction segment caving on price, which historically pressures resale within six to nine months. Buyers watching the builder discount tape have a window before resale repositions.
The NAR voter poll: nine in ten say buying a home is harder now than ever. Only 17% think now is a good time to buy, down from 69% in 2013. Just 9% think federal housing policy is helping. That's the consumer mindset framing every showing. The qualified buyer still in the hunt has internalized both the rate environment and the affordability math, they're not waiting for a print.
Rates eased a fraction: 30-year 6.42% (-2 bps), 15-year 5.99% (-1 bp). Holding near 6.4% gives buyers time to underwrite a known number.
