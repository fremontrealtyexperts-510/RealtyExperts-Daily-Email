About one in three local sellers cut their asking price this past month, and about as many have every month since March.

Sellers are meeting today's rates partway. In Fremont, Hayward, Newark and Union City, 105 of the 308 homes on the market at both our Sept. 24 and Oct. 24 MLS exports lowered their asking price in between, or 34.1%, and the typical cut was 3.8%. Newark sellers cut the most, 22 of 48; Hayward the least, 27 of 107.
It is not a one month blip. In every month since March, between 33.2% and 40.4% of the listings in those four cities have cut, against 16% to 28% last fall and winter. Board wide, across 14 cities, it was 34.2% this month.
Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average ended the week at 7.33% and the 15-year at 6.98%; it does not update on weekends. Freddie Mac's weekly average was 7.32% Thursday. The Fed meets Tuesday and Wednesday, its first meeting since raising its rate in September.
Locally, inventory held steady. The board wide table rose to 2,640 from 2,636, with 133 coming soon and 205 brand new. The five city ledger rose to 688 from 684: Milpitas to 102 from 95 and Hayward to 198 from 194, while Fremont slipped to 230 from 235, Newark to 91 from 92 and Union City to 67 from 68.
What this means for you: a typical cut is worth real money. On our five city median of $1,075,000 with 20% down, a 3.8% cut takes about $40,850 off the price and about $225 off the monthly principal and interest at 7.33%, roughly what a rate near 6.94% would do. If you are buying, ask about price before you shop for a lower rate; if you are selling, a third of your competition has already cut, so price for today's payment from day one.
