Buyers are signing fewer contracts. Redfin says pending home sales fell 4% from August to September, the biggest monthly drop in more than three years, to the lowest level since 2014 outside the pandemic lockdown. West Coast metros slowed the most, with Seattle down 24.4% from a year ago and Los Angeles down 16%.
Rates are the reason. Freddie Mac's weekly average held at 7.40% on Thursday, its highest since November 2023 and up from 6.27% a year ago. MND's daily index ticked up Friday to 7.37% for the 30-year and 7.04% for the 15-year, from 7.33% and 6.98% Thursday. On our five city median list price of $1,088,000 with 20% down, principal and interest run about $6,009 a month, roughly $661 more than at last October's 6.23%. The house did not get pricier; the money did.
Locally, supply is still building. The five city ledger reads 687, up from 683: Newark rose to 95 from 90 and Union City to 66 from 64, while Fremont slipped to 237 from 239 and Milpitas to 100 from 101, and Hayward held at 189. The board wide table rose to 2,681 from 2,645 as coming soon homes went live: brand new listings jumped to 196 from 153 while coming soon fell to 133 from 155.
What this means for you: buyers who can carry today's payment face fewer rivals and more choice, with 165 homes on the five city ledger listed 60 days or longer. Sellers should price for the buyers shopping now, not the ones waiting on rates.
• Board wide: 2,681 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 133 coming soon and 196 brand new.

Gold and silver are Friday, October 16 settlements (COMEX December); the 10-Year is Friday's Treasury close. The Brent card is Thursday's ICE December settlement; Friday it settled at $109.86, up 3.72%.
Holiday shoppers are expected to spend more. The National Retail Federation forecast Thursday that November and December sales will grow 4.2% to a record $1.013 trillion, after 3.1% last year. Bain sees 4.5%, Deloitte 4.0% to 4.8% and Mastercard 5.5%, the most upbeat of the group.
One fix to the newsletter's chart: it measured every forecast against a single 2025 figure of 3.1%, which is NRF's own number. Each firm counts differently, so we set each forecast next to its own 2025 result. Three of the four still speed up, while Deloitte's range sits around its 4.1% from last year. About half the gain is prices: Mastercard says 2.8 points of its 5.5% is inflation, and Bain says more than half. Santa's sleigh costs more to fuel this year.
Oil jumped Friday. Brent settled at $109.86, up 3.72%, as attacks on tankers around the Strait of Hormuz picked up. Bonds held steady, with the 10-Year closing at 5.09% after Thursday's drop to 5.10% from 5.19%.
A correction: Market Briefs showed Thursday's 10-Year at 5.07% (down 0.01), gold up 0.21% and Brent at $105.30, down 0.59%. Treasury's close was 5.10%, down 0.09; gold settled up 1.51%; and Brent settled at $105.92, down 0.13%.
Three of four forecasters expect faster holiday sales growth than they measured last year; Deloitte's range sits around its 4.1% from 2025. About half the gain is higher prices.
