Knowing When To Bail: Burry Cashes Out, California Stalls
Market Briefs led with Michael Burry today, and the framing matters more than the trade: the Big Short investor sold his entire GameStop stake because he believes the proposed $55B eBay buyout would pile on too much debt. Knowing when to bail is the whole game. Bay Area sellers face the same logic with one structural difference, there is no clean exit button.
The data backs that up. Home prices rose in 71% of U.S. metros in Q1, with the biggest gains in the affordable middle of the country: Akron, Ohio +12%, Anchorage +10.4%, Albany NY +9.3%. The West slipped 2.9% as California stalled. Affordability is winning the cycle, and the Bay Area is on the wrong side of that print.
Then there is the bend in the road. Nvidia and PulteGroup are teaming with a startup called Span to install mini data centers inside homes, 6x faster and 5x cheaper to deploy than a 100-megawatt facility, with homeowners paid for the power and the internet. That is a long-cycle Bay Area story, but it is the first credible signal that residential silicon could become a yield asset, not just a consumer one.
Rates eased a touch today. The 30-year sits at 6.44% (-0.10%), the 15-year at 6.00% (-0.04%). The buyer who is shopping right now has already accepted the rate environment. They are qualified, committed, and not waiting for a cut.

US 10-Year and Gold as of 4:00 PM ET May 5th; WTI Crude reflects today's intraday move via investing.com.
Crude cracked today. WTI fell -6.91% to $95.20 on softer demand prints, even with the Strait of Hormuz still closed. The move tells you traders are starting to price in the softer global growth picture more than the supply shock, and Saudi Arabia is the canary, the kingdom just posted its widest Q1 deficit since 2018 because the closed strait cut its export volume in half. Brent at $110.46 (-3.48%) confirms the same de-rating across the curve.
The U.S. trade deficit climbed to $60.3B in March, up 4.4% month over month. Underneath, capital goods imports including computer chips and AI servers hit a record $120.7B, mostly from Taiwan. The takeaway is that the AI build cycle is now showing up as a hard line on the trade balance, not just on earnings calls.
Hiring picked up. U.S. firms added 5.6 million people in March per the BLS, up from February. But job openings stayed flat and quits dropped to 3.2 million year over year. Companies are filling existing roles, not posting new ones, and workers are not voluntarily moving. That posture matters for housing because it caps the relocation buyer pool.
U.K. gilt yields hit multi-decade highs ahead of local elections, the 30-year jumped to 5.76% and the 10-year to 5.08%, the priciest G7 sovereign debt. Uncertainty equals volatility, and the U.K. is the global tell on what voters do to bond markets when the fiscal arithmetic gets squeezed.
Closer to home, the 10-Year barely moved at 4.42% (-0.02%) and gold ticked up +0.72% to $4,566. Mortgage rates respond to that 10-Year first, which is why we got a 10-bp dip on the 30-year today.
