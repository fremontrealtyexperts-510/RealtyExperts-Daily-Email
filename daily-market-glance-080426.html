Rates eased a hair. The daily 30-year fixed slipped a basis point to 6.82% and the 15-year held at 6.32%. Not relief, but not another climb either.
Wall Street is buying California's wildfire risk, and that is good news for homeowners. Insurers have sold $5.18 billion of catastrophe bonds carrying wildfire exposure so far in 2026, already 93% of last year's full year record with half the year still to run. See the chart below: this market has more than doubled since 2023.
Here is why it matters more than it sounds. A catastrophe bond lets an insurer hand wildfire risk to investors who want the yield. The more of that capacity exists, the more willing carriers are to keep writing policies in fire prone areas instead of walking away. Anyone who has tried to place coverage in a California hillside neighborhood in the past two years knows the problem is availability, not just price. This is the plumbing behind that, and right now the plumbing is getting bigger.
The caution: the newsletter reported this market at $2 billion for 2026. Artemis, which actually tracks these deals, puts it at $5.183 billion. Worth knowing the real number is more than double, because the direction of travel is the whole story.
Builders are pulling back. Residential construction spending fell for a fourth straight month in June to an annual pace of $889.3 billion, which is 4.7% below last year. Single family took the bigger hit, with mortgage rates above 6.5% for 11 straight weeks now. Slow sales are turning into fewer starts and fewer permits, which tightens supply 12 to 18 months out.
A scam to warn clients about: fraudsters are now paying U.S. freelancers to post fake rental listings, because a domestic poster does not trip the platform fraud alarms that a foreign one does. Consumers reported about 65,000 rental scams from 2020 through mid 2025, losing $65 million, and half of the recent ones started with a fake Facebook ad.
Why a REALTOR® cares: builders cutting starts today is next year's inventory shortage. If your buyer is waiting for prices to break, the supply side is quietly moving against them even while rates stay high.

Crypto is a live quote from this morning. Commodities can move fast intraday, so treat these as a same day snapshot.
The 10-Year backed off. Treasury put the 10-year yield at 4.70% at Monday's close, down 5 basis points from Friday's 4.75%. Small, but it is the right direction for anyone shopping a mortgage. Note the newsletter printed 4.67% here; Treasury's own daily curve says 4.70%, and we go with Treasury.
Oil fell off a cliff. Brent dropped 5.77% to $78.94 a barrel, a second straight brutal session, on hopes that the U.S. and Iran are heading back to the negotiating table. Two days ago it was $83.73. Cheaper crude eventually feeds into cheaper everything, though as we noted yesterday, refining capacity is the bottleneck between crude and the pump.
Metals both moved up. Gold added $19 to $4,070 an ounce and silver gained 1.19% to $58.63, its second straight gain.
Summer travel is cooling. International flight bookings by Americans fell 8% year over year, with airfare to Europe up 12% and a weaker dollar making everything abroad pricier. Delta and United both warn that elevated jet fuel prices are here for a while.
Why a REALTOR® cares: a 10-Year drifting down while oil collapses is the combination that eventually pulls mortgage rates lower. It has not happened yet, and one day is not a trend, but this is the shape of the setup you want to see if you are waiting on rate relief for a buyer.

Mostly flat. Bitcoin near $63,959 and Ether around $1,868, both slightly up, with XRP easing under a percent to just over $1.08.
A hardware wallet got drained, which is the part worth reading twice. Attackers pulled more than 1,000 Bitcoin out of Coldcard wallets in 41 minutes on July 30, roughly $89 million across about 1,200 wallets. A hardware wallet is supposed to be the safe option because it keeps the keys offline. The failure was a coding mistake in certain versions that made the recovery phrase guessable. If a client mentions they hold crypto on a hardware device, this is the week to tell them to check their firmware version.
Selling pressure continues. Strategy offloaded about $105 million of Bitcoin last week, and American Crypto posted a $47 million quarterly loss. When the market slumps, the companies holding the most are the ones who have to sell to raise cash.
