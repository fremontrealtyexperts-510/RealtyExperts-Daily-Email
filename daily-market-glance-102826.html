Mortgage applications rose 3.4% last week as rates eased, but home purchase applications are still 18% behind last year's pace.

Today's question is mortgage applications, and the short answer is that buyers are pausing. The Mortgage Bankers Association's weekly survey, out this morning, shows applications up 3.4% in the week ending October 23 as its 30-year contract rate eased to 7.37% from 7.41%. Even with that bounce, home purchase applications were 18% below the same week last year, and refinances were 61% lower.
Rates explain most of it. Early this year, with that rate near 6.2%, purchase applications ran as much as 18% ahead of 2025. Then rates rose every week for two months, reaching 7.50% in early October, the highest since November 2023, and purchase applications have trailed last year in each of the past seven weeks. More borrowers are turning to adjustable rate loans, now 11.3% of applications, the most in over a year.
Locally, the newest full year of federal lending data (HMDA) shows Alameda County lenders took 13,940 home purchase loan applications in 2025, down 6.9% from 2024 even as the nation edged up 1.1%, and about half the 2021 peak (chart). Our own MLS shows the pause today: on this date a year apart, across the same 14 cities, homes in contract fell 26% to 609 from 820, while homes for sale barely moved, 1,957 from 1,989.
Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average held at 7.33% Tuesday and the 15-year is 7.01%, with the Fed's decision at 11 AM our time (more in Economy). The board wide table slipped to 2,554 from 2,578, with 144 coming soon and 148 brand new. The five city ledger is 674, from 678: Fremont (226) and Newark (89) held, while Hayward eased to 196, Milpitas to 103 and Union City to 60.
What this means for you: buyers are waiting on rates, not walking away, and even a small dip brought them back this week. Keep buyers pre-approved so they can move on the next one, and if an ARM comes up, have them go over the adjustment terms with their lender. Sellers should price for today's payment from day one, because inventory is flat from a year ago while contracts are down a quarter.
