The 30-year fixed eased to 6.54% and the 15-year to 6.11% — a touch lower than last week — though all eyes are on the Fed's rate decision later today.

Stocks were mixed — the S&P 500 slipped 0.57% and the NASDAQ fell 1.15% as chip names pulled back, while the DOW added 0.64%. The Fed meets today, with a rate decision due this afternoon.
The big theme is AI spending. Microsoft (MSFT -1.48%) is reportedly in talks to rent about $3 billion of computing power from rival Oracle (ORCL -2.24%) — a cloud giant renting cloud — because AI is eating up capacity faster than it can build, and Microsoft says it will stay short on capacity into mid-2026. The hyperscalers are pouring record sums into data centers and chips: Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and Meta are each projected to spend roughly $145–200 billion on AI this year (see the chart below).
Chips are the bottleneck. Nvidia (NVDA -2.37%) CEO Jensen Huang asked Korea's SK Hynix to make more memory chips at Taiwan's biggest tech show this month, while U.S. memory maker Micron (MU -6.18%) — up more than 250% this year — gave back some of its run.
Dealmaking stayed hot: SpaceX (SPCX +4.83%), fresh off the biggest IPO in history, said it will buy four-year-old AI coding startup Cursor for $60 billion to feed its Grok AI; and GM (GM -1.86%) struck a deal with defense giant Lockheed Martin (LMT +1.05%) to scale U.S. munitions manufacturing — Lockheed spending $9 billion through 2030 and GM putting $7 billion into U.S. R&D.
Big Tech's 2026 AI bet: Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and Meta are each projected to spend roughly $145–200 billion on AI and data centers this year — demand so intense that Microsoft is reportedly renting about $3 billion of computing power from rival Oracle.

For housing, the supply picture is flashing caution. Crypto pulled back across the board: Bitcoin at $64,625 (-1.51%), Ether at $1,764 (-1.42%), and XRP at $1.19 (-2.15%). The bigger story is where crypto and traditional markets are merging — Coinbase (COIN -0.21%) is launching tokenized U.S. stocks, backed one-for-one by real shares and paying dividends automatically, pitched as real ownership with "no derivatives, no IOUs" and 24/7 trading. The catch: it starts outside the U.S., so American users wait on regulators while Kraken and Robinhood (HOOD -1.44%) already offer their own versions.
Overseas, Binance is preparing to exit the European Union after Greece signaled it may cancel the platform's license — and because one EU license normally covers all 27 countries, a single rejection can shut the whole bloc. Rivals Coinbase and Kraken could scoop up the users Binance loses.
