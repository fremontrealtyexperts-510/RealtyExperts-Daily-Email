Mortgage rates eased again. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed average slipped to 7.36% today from 7.40% Wednesday, and the 15-year to 7.00% from 7.04%.
Buyers have not come back yet. Mortgage applications fell 5.3% in the week ending Oct. 16, the Mortgage Bankers Association said Wednesday, even though the 30-year contract rate in its survey dipped to 7.41% from 7.50%, the first decline in over two months. Purchase applications fell 7% and ran 18% below a year ago, and refinances fell 2%. MBA's Joel Kan said "higher rates are pricing buyers out of the market." About one application in ten was an adjustable rate loan, priced almost a full point below the 30-year fixed.
Today's bond market could change that math. The 10-Year Treasury yield, which mortgage rates follow, fell to about 4.96% at 11:18 AM PT from Wednesday's 5.13% close, its first time under 5% since late September, as investors sold technology stocks and bought bonds. If it holds, lenders have room to price lower in the days ahead.
Locally, the market held still. The board wide table is at 2,631, the same count as Tuesday's report, while coming soon listings fell to 130 from 141 and brand new listings to 167 from 178. The five city ledger slipped to 679 from 680: Fremont rose to 231 from 229 and Hayward to 197 from 192, while Milpitas fell to 97 from 103 and Newark to 89 from 92.
What this means for you: watch the 10-Year this week more than the headlines. On the five city median of $1,075,000 with 20% down, principal and interest runs about $5,931 a month at 7.36%, and every quarter point in rate moves that by about $146. Buyers who are ready can ask their lender about a float down, which lets a locked rate fall if the market improves before closing. Sellers should keep in mind that with purchase applications 18% below last year, pricing right on day one still beats waiting for rates.

The AI trade is unwinding today. Chip stocks led the Nasdaq down about 2.5% by late morning, with Nvidia off about 4%, and Meta fell about 7.5% after its results. Meta's revenue rose 26% to $64.60 billion, but it narrowed its 2026 spending plan to $135 billion to $145 billion, raising the low end, and announced a new non-voting share class to be issued as a stock dividend that works like a two-for-one split.

Levels are Wednesday, October 21: the 10-Year is Treasury's close, gold and silver are COMEX December settlements, and Brent is the ICE December settlement.
Money ran to bonds today. The 10-Year Treasury yield fell to about 4.96% at 11:18 AM PT from Wednesday's 5.13% close, under 5% for the first time since late September, as investors pulled back from technology stocks. Gold and silver did not get the safe haven bid: gold settled at $4,060.40, down 2.2%, and silver at $55.01, down 4.7%.
Oil went the other way. Brent was up about 3.9% near $113 a barrel at 11:18 AM PT, its highest since early May, on worries the conflict in the Middle East could widen. Higher oil feeds inflation, the one risk that could undo the bond rally. Bonds and oil rallied together today, a pairing that rarely stays friendly for long.
A correction: Market Briefs had the 10-Year at 5.09%, down 0.05 point, for Wednesday; Treasury's close was 5.13%, up 0.01. It also had gold down 0.57% and Brent up 1.40%. The settlements were gold up 0.18% at $4,150.00 and Brent up 0.03% at $108.82.

Crypto followed tech lower. Bitcoin is down 4.32% over 24 hours near $81,020, Ether is down 5.72% and XRP 6.72%, live from CoinGecko at 11:18 AM PT.
Tokenized stocks are the fastest growing corner of crypto trading. Robinhood's Stock Tokens, blockchain tokens that track shares and ETFs without voting rights and trade mostly overseas, did $11.31 billion of volume on decentralized exchanges on Robinhood Chain in September, 21.5 times July's $526 million, according to Bitwise Asset Management's staking report, which CNBC cited Wednesday. The tokens themselves were worth at least $160.8 million at the end of September, so this is mostly fast trading, not new money. Nvidia was the most traded token of the quarter at $2.50 billion.
Not every company wants in. Sixteen companies, including Goldman Sachs, Microsoft and Alphabet, have objected to tokenized versions of their shares trading on OKXICE, a new venue from OKX and NYSE owner Intercontinental Exchange that plans to list more than 60, CNBC reported. Under the SEC's new innovation exemption, companies get that say. Robinhood shares fell about 6% today, so the tokens are not the only thing trading fast.
Stock Token trading on Robinhood Chain hit $11.31 billion in September, 21.5 times July. The tokens themselves were worth at least $160.8 million at Sept. 30.
