The daily 30-year fixed eased to 6.80% and the 15-year to 6.33%, both down a hair as oil pulled back. Small relief, but relief.
The housing market is not just slow, it is getting smaller. Residential investment fell 8% in the first quarter of 2026, squeezed by mortgage rates above 6.5%, climbing property taxes, and a new housing law pushing some investors to sell.
Builders are responding the only way that works. They cut the median new-home price 2.7% to $398,300 in June, on top of the rate buy-downs and cash credits they were already offering. When a builder needs to move inventory, the buyer gets leverage, and right now builders need to move inventory.

Stocks were mixed to close the week. The DOW added 0.46% to 51,947 and the S&P 500 edged up 0.05% to 7,411, while the NASDAQ slipped 0.64% to 24,975.
Oracle landed a big one. The company signed a roughly $7 billion, 10-year deal to build software for the Pentagon. Analysts read it as steady, predictable revenue at a moment when Oracle is taking on debt to fund AI data centers. The stock has not been rewarded for any of it: shares fell 4.21% on the day and are down about 41% for the year (chart below), a long way off their June peak near $250.
Nvidia made a bigger one. It locked in a contract with SK Hynix to secure the memory chips its AI processors need, a deal that could be worth $500 billion over several years. SK Hynix only went public in the U.S. a few weeks ago, raising more than $26 billion in its IPO. And on the policy front, President Trump opened a trade investigation into the European Union over the fines it has levied on U.S. tech giants, including a $1 billion antitrust penalty against Google.

Gold and silver are live spot quotes taken the morning of July 27 (change versus the same time a trading day earlier); the 10-Year, Brent, and stocks are as of the July 24 close. Metals can move fast intraday, so treat these as a morning snapshot.
AI has quietly become an everyday habit. Market Briefs asked its readers how often they use it, and about 63% said daily, with another 18% weekly. That is roughly 8 in 10 using AI at least once a week, while 13.9% still do not use it at all (chart below). Not everyone is thrilled about it either: librarians in Maine are now running seminars teaching people how to switch off unwanted AI features on their devices.
The money it created is showing up in strange places. AI wealth has minted a new wave of millionaires, and top auction houses pulled in around $6 billion in the first half of 2026, a record, as buyers chased rare fossils and expensive watches as stores of value. Dinosaur bones as an asset class is a sentence we did not expect to write.
Oil finally cooled. The U.S. paused airstrikes on Iran for a second night and Brent eased 1.00% to $96.78, though crude is still up about 27% in two weeks with the Strait of Hormuz blocked. The 10-Year Treasury slipped to 4.68%.
The metals kept climbing. Gold rose 0.62% to $4,077 and silver added 0.95% to $58.53 as easing Middle East tension pulled oil down and softened the inflation worry ahead of this week's Fed meeting.
Why a REALTOR® cares: tariffs on more than 80 countries now cover 99.4% of U.S. trade, and small businesses are suing to overturn them. That fight runs straight through construction costs, which is what turns into the price of a new home.
About 8 in 10 Market Briefs readers now use AI at least weekly, with 63.3% using it daily. Only 13.9% say they do not use it at all.
