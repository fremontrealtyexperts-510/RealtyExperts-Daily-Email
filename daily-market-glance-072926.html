Americans Are Sitting On A Record $17.9 Trillion In Home Equity, 71.6% Of All Home Value, But Low Rates Have Them Locked In; Locally, 728 Homes Remain Buyable Across Our Five Cities

The daily 30-year fixed held at 6.76% and the 15-year at 6.31%.
Homeowners are rich on paper and stuck in practice. Americans now hold a combined $17.9 trillion in home equity, and equity as a share of total U.S. real estate value sits at 71.6%, right near its all time high (chart below). For perspective, that share bottomed at 46.2% in 2011 after the crash. The problem is access: most owners carry an ultra low mortgage rate they would surrender by selling, so they simply do not list.
That is the whole lock-in market in one number. It also points at where the business is. A homeowner who wants to tap equity without giving up a 3% note is a HELOC conversation, a rental conversion conversation, or an ADU conversation, not necessarily a listing today. Stay in that relationship and you are there when it does become a move.
Also worth watching: college towns are running hot while the rest of the market crawls. Morgantown, West Virginia jumped 12.9% year over year to $275,000, against a national median up just 2%.

Gold and silver are live spot quotes taken the morning of July 29 (change versus the same time a day earlier); the 10-Year, Brent, stocks, and crypto are as of the July 28 close. Metals can move fast intraday, so treat these as a morning snapshot.
All eyes on the Fed. The central bank wraps its meeting today and is expected to hold rates steady, with a decision due at 2:00 PM ET. Inflation is still running at 3.5%, well above the 2% target, and tariff costs are part of why.
On that front, the Senate is weighing a bill that would let the President impose a 500% tariff on Russian goods and 100% on its top energy buyers. Critics warn it would not stop there and could ratchet rates higher on other countries too.
Oil turned back up. Brent jumped 4.70% to $88.04, snapping a three day slide after renewed strikes in the Middle East, including intercepted attacks on U.S. troops and drone strikes on Saudi oil facilities. Metals split ahead of the Fed: gold eased 0.33% to $4,029 while silver rebounded 0.90% to $58.05.
One to file away: PJM, the operator of America's largest electric grid, said it will cut power to big data centers if the grid gets strained, starting June 2027. Facilities drawing 50 megawatts or more go first.
Why a REALTOR® cares: grid strain is becoming a siting issue. Where data centers cluster, they compete with housing for power and land, and that shows up in what a jurisdiction will approve and how fast.

Crypto drifted again. Bitcoin eased 0.29% to $63,662, Ether slipped 0.65% to $1,907, and XRP fell 0.45% to $1.07.
Washington is closing a favorite loophole. Congress is moving to end the wash sale exemption that lets crypto investors sell at a loss, buy right back immediately, and still claim the deduction, something stock investors cannot do. The Treasury estimates closing it raises $24 billion over a decade.
The big money got a little shy. Wealthy investors trimmed crypto from 7% to 6% of their portfolios last year, though 45% say they plan to increase again next year. Small and large holders alike seem to be treating this slump as temporary.
