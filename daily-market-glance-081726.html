Rates gave back a little. The 30 year fixed rose 2 basis points to 6.71% and the 15 year added 3 to 6.29%, ending a two day decline. The cause is in the Economy section: the ten year yield climbed 5 basis points and Brent crude rose 1.67%, so both of the forces that pulled rates to a four week low on Friday reversed.
Two national datapoints landed today, and together they describe a market tilting toward buyers.
• Sellers now outnumber buyers by 51%. High prices and high rates pushed 967,000 would be buyers to the sidelines in July. When that many people step back, the remaining buyers gain leverage they have not had in years. The biggest price drops are showing up in Miami and Houston, both markets that built aggressively into the boom.
• Flipper confidence hit a record low. Only 26% of investors who buy and renovate single family homes think conditions are better than a year ago, down from 35% last quarter, and 45% now say the market has gotten worse. That has a second order effect worth understanding: fewer flips means fewer renovated homes coming to market, which thins the move in ready supply that a certain kind of buyer wants, and props up prices and rents on what is left.
Why a REALTOR® cares: national leverage numbers are not local ones, and the gap is your value. Our board is not Miami or Houston. Fremont's median asking price is still near $1.3 million and the median listing goes pending in 25 days. When a client arrives quoting a national headline about a buyer's market, the honest answer is that the leverage is real in some places and thin here, and the difference is exactly what they are paying you to know.

A soft finish to last week. All three indexes gave back a fraction: the NASDAQ eased 0.28% to 26,729, the Dow slipped 0.20% to 53,732, and the S&P 500 lost 0.17% to 7,785. Small moves in the same direction, which is what a market with nothing new to react to looks like.
Anthropic put a number on the AI spending boom. Q2 revenue hit $11.5 billion, a 14 times increase from a year ago, driven by businesses paying for Claude to handle coding and daily work. The company is still private and could go public as early as this year. Whatever you think of the valuations being floated, revenue growing 14 times in four quarters is not a rumor.
Lockheed unveiled ACE, a new interceptor priced around $2 million, and rose 1.78%. The problem it solves is almost comic: the Pentagon has been shooting down cheap drones with its most expensive missiles. Cheaper options exist on paper but are years from production, and stockpiles are thinning because of the war, so defense production is ramping.
Alibaba crossed 3 billion downloads of its free Qwen models and added 1.35%, pressuring Google, whose Gemini recently passed 1 billion active users. Free and modifiable is beating polished and closed, at least on volume. Alibaba reports earnings Thursday.

Ten year yield is the Treasury close for August 14th. Brent is the August 14th settlement. Gold quoted at 9:05 AM Eastern and silver at 6:00 AM Eastern on August 17th.
The dollar is the quiet story. It fell to its weakest point since May, with the dollar index down 0.4% on Friday, and the reason is that Americans are spending less. Softer spending gives the Fed less reason to raise rates, and a currency with less rate support drifts down. A weaker dollar makes imports dearer, which is worth remembering the next time an appliance or a light fixture quotes higher than it did in the spring.
The ten year yield rose to 4.68%, up 5 basis points, which is the opposite of last week's direction. The newsletter printed the level correctly and then called it unchanged. It was not unchanged, and a rising ten year is why mortgage rates ticked up rather than continuing down.
Brent settled at $88.52, up 1.67%. The newsletter reported $88.76 and a 0.35% gain. Both figures are off, and the direction matters here: last week cheaper oil was helping pull rates lower, and this week it stopped helping.
Gold is at $4,384, up 0.30% from Friday morning, and silver jumped to $65.48, up 1.01%. Worth a note for anyone comparing sources: the newsletter quoted gold at $4,460 as of 4 PM Friday. That is not a contradiction, it is a different moment. Gold ran up during Friday's session and has eased back this morning.
Electric vehicles are getting more expensive on both ends. A new EV averaged $56,126 in July, up 1.6% from a year ago, while incentives on them fell to $6,626, down 24.3% year over year now that the $7,500 federal credit is gone. Used EVs turned up too, and the chart below shows how sharply. Prices bottomed in March and have risen four months straight.
The useful lesson is about what a subsidy actually does to a price. Remove $7,500 of help from the new side and the used side gets bid up, because the cheaper substitute suddenly looks better. Housing policy works the same way, which is why every proposal to help buyers deserves the question of who ends up capturing the help.
Used EV listing prices bottomed at $34,653 in March and have climbed four straight months to $38,342, up 10.6% off the low and 7.0% above a year ago. The October 2025 spike is the $7,500 federal tax credit expiring.

A modestly green morning. Bitcoin is near $63,548, Ether is around $1,899, and XRP sits right at a dollar.
A crypto firm tied to Trump and his family, which owns 38% of it, received a conditional national bank charter. That is one of the last steps before becoming a national bank, and it would let the firm issue stablecoins directly. Whatever your politics, the direction is clear: stablecoin issuance is moving inside chartered banks rather than staying outside them.
The week's cautionary tale is about where the risk actually sits. Wallet makers SafePal and Trezor both leaked customer records, 53,487 of them, including names, home addresses and order details. The blockchain itself was not breached. The companies holding the customer list were. A hardware wallet protects your coins and does nothing to protect the fact that you own them, and a list of people known to hold crypto along with their home addresses is its own kind of hazard.
The parallel for our business is direct. We hold client files full of addresses, financial details and move dates. The lesson is not that the technology failed. It is that the customer list is always the softest target.
