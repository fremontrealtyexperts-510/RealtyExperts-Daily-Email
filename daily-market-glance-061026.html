Mortgage Rates Hold At 6.68% As Chips Drag Stocks And Mideast Tension Lifts Oil; East Bay Board Eases To 2,855 Active On A Quieter Wednesday Of 201 New And 229 Closed
The 30-year fixed held at 6.68% and the 15-year at 6.20% — flat for a second straight day, tracking a 10-year Treasury that sat still at 4.53%. The day's cross-currents mostly canceled out: AI chip stocks sold off (see Stocks) while Middle East tension nudged oil higher (see Economy), but neither moved the bond market enough to budge mortgage rates. For the rate-watching buyer, 'flat' has been the story two days running.
The national housing data, though, gave buyers something to cheer: existing-home sales jumped 3.2% in May, the best month since December and well past forecasts. Most of those deals were signed back in April, when mortgage rates briefly dipped — proof that even a short window of cheaper money pulls buyers off the fence. Homes over $1 million sold 11% more than a year ago, and the median existing-home price hit $429,000.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,855 active, down 31 from Tuesday. The midweek slowdown landed squarely on new supply: just 201 new listings came on — down sharply from Tuesday's 252 — while 229 sales closed (up 5). For the first time this week, closings outran new listings, and that's what pulled the board lower. Fremont alone logged 47 of those closings.

For agents: a Wednesday with 201 new and 229 closed is a market digesting the week's early surge — buyers are still closing (229 of them, 47 in Fremont alone) even as sellers paused on new listings. Rates are flat and national sales just popped 3.2%, so momentum sits on the buy side. Tell sellers this is a window to list into thinner competition; tell buyers the spring rate-dip that fueled May's sales jump has already passed — don't wait on a repeat.

Tuesday belonged to the chipmakers — and not in a good way. AI chip stocks sold off hard, dragging the NASDAQ down 0.97% to its weakest session in weeks, while the S&P 500 slipped 0.26% and the DOW eked out a 0.17% gain. The selling traced back to a soft Broadcom (AVGO -1.12%) report from last week that spread to Micron (MU -1.41%) and AMD (AMD -3.02%), and didn't help that Taiwan signaled it may restrict some chip exports to China.
There was drama off Earth, too. SpaceX is set to launch its IPO this Friday, but the rest of the space sector fell back to the ground — Planet Labs (PL -4.80%), Rocket Lab (RKLB -4.77%), and Intuitive Machines (LUNR -7.62%) all dropped. And Rivian (RIVN -6.59%) unveiled its R2 SUV, a ~$45,000 model it says will finally turn a profit on each sale — a tall order given it lost roughly $6,000 per vehicle last quarter. The R2 will be built at a new multi-billion-dollar Georgia plant opening in 2028.

US 10-Year, Gold, and Brent Crude as of 4:00 PM ET, Tuesday, June 9th (via Market Briefs).
The 10-year Treasury held flat at 4.53%, but two stories underneath are worth a client's attention. First, the retirement math got worse: Social Security's trust fund is now projected to run dry in late 2032 — three months sooner than last year's estimate. If Congress does nothing, the program could pay just 78% of promised benefits, roughly $500 a month less for the average retiree. That matters here because Social Security is the main income source for 43% of seniors — and a big driver of when and where people downsize.
Second, the labor market is quietly shifting under AI. Tech just logged its worst month for job cuts since 2024, with AI behind 40% of May's layoffs — the third straight month it topped the list. Companies cut 97,000+ jobs in May (up 16% from April), even as the broader economy still added 172,000. Gold fell 1.9% to $4,204, while Brent crude firmed to $92.26 after President Trump said an Iran deal could be days away — only for Iran to down a helicopter over the Strait of Hormuz hours later, sending markets down and oil up.
Social Security's trust funds are projected to run out of money in the early 2030s — a slow-building squeeze on retiree budgets that shapes downsizing decisions.

Crypto drifted lower alongside risk assets: Bitcoin eased to $61,270 (-0.68%), Ether to $1,624 (-0.81%), and XRP to $1.12 (-1.67%). The bigger story is adoption — a new study found about 20% of Americans have now used crypto at least once, and the politics are flipping: Republicans (22%) now use it more than Democrats (17%), with men under 50 the single biggest cohort.
On the product side, Circle (CRCL +1.73%) launched cirBTC, a coin backed one-to-one by Bitcoin but running on Ethereum — in plain terms, it lets Bitcoin that would otherwise just sit in a wallet be put to work inside crypto apps. Coinbase (COIN -4.08%) is chasing the same market. For Bay Area buyers and sellers the link is indirect, but crypto's risk appetite still colors a slice of the move-up market.
