ELECTION RESULTS

Thank you for voting. Prop. 37 is projected to pass.

Thank you to everyone who voted on Tuesday. California's Prop. 37, the loan program for middle income buyers of newly built homes, is projected to pass: the Associated Press called it at 9:06 PM PT Tuesday. The Secretary of State's count as of 8:08 AM PT today has it at 61.0% Yes (4,605,778 votes) to 39.0% No (2,946,506), and Alameda County at 67.5% Yes.
What it does: the state housing finance agency, CalHFA, may sell up to $25 billion in bonds to fund loans of up to 17% of the price for the first buyer of a home who puts at least 3% down and earns no more than double the area's typical income. The Legislative Analyst's Office expects no direct state or local cost, because buyers' payments repay the bonds.
The count is not final. Mail ballots postmarked by Election Day still count if they arrive within seven days, and the state certifies the result by Dec. 11.
Sources: California Secretary of State (semi-official results); Associated Press race call via KQED; Legislative Analyst's Office.

Prop. 37 (details at the top) is written for one kind of home: the loans go only to the first buyer, which in practice means new construction. In our five cities, 32 of the 570 homes on the market were built in 2025 or later, 20 of them townhomes, with a median list price of $1,128,500 and 14 priced under a million. They have waited a median 57 days, against 40 for the market as a whole. CalHFA still has to sell the bonds and open the program, so it is not available to buyers yet.
Rates eased the morning after. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.25% and the 15-year 6.86%, both down 0.05 from Tuesday. At 7.25%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,312,500 median with 20% down would pay about $7,163 a month in principal and interest.
The board wide table is 2,438, from 2,457 Tuesday, with 152 coming soon and 133 brand new. The five city ledger is 642, from 648: Fremont (218) and Newark (81) each dropped three, Hayward (182) one, Milpitas (100) added one and Union City (61) held.
What this means for you: if new construction is on your list, ask builders now how they plan to work with Prop. 37 once it opens. It is a loan that gets repaid, not a grant, so read its terms like a second mortgage's. Resale sellers compete with those builders, and 177 homes in our five cities have been sitting 60 days or longer, so price to this market, not last spring's.
