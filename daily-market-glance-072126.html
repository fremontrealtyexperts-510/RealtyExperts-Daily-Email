The daily 30-year fixed nudged up to 6.71% and the 15-year to 6.18%, both a touch firmer than yesterday. Small moves, but they land on budgets that were already stretched.
Two national threads are worth watching. First, where you shop matters more than ever. Sales of homes under $350,000 are down 7.2% this year, with the South leading the drop while parts of the Northeast are still seeing prices rise. The starter rung is not one national market anymore, it is a patchwork. Second, a wave of new AI brokerages is trying to slash the standard 3% commission and hand buyers cash back, which on a $500,000 home could mean around $10,000 toward a smaller down payment or closing costs. Worth knowing, though the value of an agent who knows your specific street tends to show up right when the negotiation does.

Gold and silver are live spot quotes taken the morning of July 21 (change versus the same time a day earlier); the 10-Year, Brent, stocks, and crypto are as of the July 20 close. Metals can move fast intraday, so treat these as a morning snapshot.
Gas is back over $4. The national average for regular unleaded returned to about $4.00 a gallon, up from an early July dip near $3.78 and closing in on the spring high of $4.50 (chart below). The squeeze is coming from two directions at once: tensions around Iran are pinching fuel supply, and there simply are not enough refineries to turn crude into gasoline fast enough.
Oddly, crude itself eased. Brent slipped 0.85% to $88.45, so the pressure at the pump is a refining story more than a barrel story right now.
The metals ran the other way, hard. Silver spiked about 4.9% to $59.12 and gold added roughly 1.1% to $4,054 as investors weighed the odds of renewed peace talks and what they would mean for inflation and rates. The 10-Year Treasury sat still at 4.59%.
Why a REALTOR® cares about a gallon of gas: it is one of the few prices households feel every single week, and it feeds the inflation read the Fed is watching. That same read sets mortgage rates, so what happens at the pump does not stay at the pump.

Crypto had a quiet, green day. Bitcoin added 0.16% to $65,318, Ether rose 0.30% to $1,909, and XRP ticked up 0.28% to $1.12.
The policy news was louder than the price action. Russia is drafting a law that would allow only Bitcoin and Ether for legal crypto trading, since a proposed floor of a $65 billion market cap and five years of history rules almost everything else out. The goal is to route cross border payments around Western sanctions. Meanwhile South Korea's main stock index has been swinging harder than Bitcoin this year, with volatility above 60%, because two chip names, Samsung and SK Hynix, make up more than half the index, so every AI headline shakes the whole market.
