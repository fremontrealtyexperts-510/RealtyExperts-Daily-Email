REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 6.61%  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.13%

The 30-year nudged up to 6.61% and the 15-year to 6.13% — a modest move higher after holding flat earlier in the week. The backdrop explains the drift: fresh tariff threats on 60 trading partners and a PCE inflation reading at a three-year high keep upward pressure on rates, even as the 10-year Treasury sat roughly steady at 4.49%. The buyer waiting for a clean break lower is still waiting.

Local board: 2,901 active (up 52 from Wednesday), 214 new listings, 204 closed sales. Inventory is building again — new supply held about steady while closings cooled, so more homes are coming on than are clearing.

National: In San Francisco, a home listed at $7.95 million just sold for $15 million — and prices there are up nearly 15% year-over-year, the fastest of any major U.S. metro, with the AI-IPO wave not even fully underway yet. Closer to the investment side, firms Kayne Anderson and BKM just bought a $1.8 billion light-industrial portfolio, a sign the warehouse trade is back in favor.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,554  |  DOW: 50,687  |  NASDAQ: 26,854

It was a red session for the major indexes, all three closing lower. Broadcom (AVGO -0.49%) posted record quarterly sales of $22.2 billion — up 48% from a year ago — but the stock still slid as much as 8% after hours, having already run up 15% and added ~$280 billion in market value over the prior four sessions; holding its $100 billion AI-chip target steady was enough for traders to take profits.

GameStop (GME +6.02%) went the other way after authorizing a $2 billion share buyback, funded from the $8.4 billion in cash it has held since the 2021 meme rally. Fewer shares outstanding means each remaining share is a slightly bigger slice of the company.

ECONOMY

US 10-Year: 4.49%  |  Gold: $4,483  |  Brent Crude: $97.24

Trade tension is back in the headlines. The White House is proposing tariffs of at least 10% on imports from 60 trading partners — including the E.U., China, Canada, and Mexico — this time framed around a 1974 trade law arguing those partners rely on forced labor. Two earlier tariff attempts were struck down in court, so the legal footing matters as much as the economics.

The household picture is softening underneath. The personal savings rate fell to 2.6% in April, the lowest since June 2022, even as the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge (PCE) rose 3.8%, its fastest pace in three years. Abroad, the OECD warned a prolonged Iran conflict could drag global growth to 1.8% — outside of COVID and 2008, the weakest in 40 years.

CRYPTOCURRENCY

BTC: $64,253  |  ETH: $1,814  |  XRP: $1.22

Crypto firmed slightly but stayed under pressure. Bitcoin ticked up 0.3% to $64,253 yet remains pinned near the floor of its “Power Law corridor,” a model mapping where Bitcoin has historically traded; the only two prior touches of that level were the COVID crash in March 2020 and the FTX collapse in late 2022. Ether ($1,814) and XRP ($1.22) posted similarly modest gains.
