Friday's soft jobs report was supposed to help mortgage rates. It did not. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year rose to 7.57%, up 0.03 from Thursday and just under the 7.60% high of September 30, and the 15-year is 7.19%. On our five city median of $1,079,000 with 20% down, principal and interest come to $6,077 a month, $398 more than when September began at 6.89% and $700 more than the same loan cost a year ago at 6.36%.
Employers added just 29,000 jobs in September and unemployment rose to 4.2%, news that usually pulls rates down. Bonds did rally early Friday, then gave it back: the jobless rate edged up because the labor force grew by 485,000, and 406,000 more people reported having a job. The 10-Year Treasury, which mortgage rates tend to follow, closed at 5.28%, up from 5.24%. Today's two charts, in the Economy section, show the slowdown and who is still hiring.
Locally, the five city ledger slipped to 664 from 679 on Friday, and the board wide table held at 2,700 with 212 brand new listings, up from 189.
What this means for you: buyers should not count on soft jobs data to bring rates down, because Friday's did not. If the payment works, lock it and keep a preapproval current; on the median home a quarter point is worth about $147 a month. Sellers should price for today's 7.57%; 134 of the ledger's 590 homes on the market have been listed 60 days or longer.
• Board wide: 2,700 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 161 coming soon and 212 brand new.

All four are Friday, October 2: the Treasury daily yield curve close, the COMEX December gold and silver settlements, and the ICE December Brent settlement. Markets are closed for the weekend.
Friday's jobs report showed hiring nearly stalled. Employers added 29,000 jobs in September, below the 45,000 a month of the prior year, and July and August were revised down a combined 60,000, turning July into a loss of 10,000. Unemployment rose to 4.2% from 4.1%, and hourly pay was up 3.0% from a year earlier.
The second chart shows who is still hiring. If the economy has a hiring desk, it is in a hospital: health care and social assistance added 520,000 jobs over the past year, more than the 496,000 added overall, so everything else combined slipped. Government, information and finance each lost more than 100,000. Locally, jobs in the San Jose metro rose 1.6% in the year to August, while the Oakland-Fremont area, Alameda and Contra Costa counties, was nearly flat at 0.2%.
Gold settled down 0.95% Friday and silver down 1.24%, and December Brent was little changed at $102.25.
