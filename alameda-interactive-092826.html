Most California homes lost to fire were underinsured. A California Western School of Law professor, Kenneth Klein, obtained state Department of Insurance data on 74,070 fire claims on owner-occupied homes from 2018 to 2023. Of the homes destroyed, 71.3% were underinsured, by about 19% on average. Wildfire losses came up short 78% of the time and ordinary house fires 68%.

The weak link is the estimate. The insurer's own rebuild estimate at the point of sale ran below the final loss 75% to 95% of the time, and roughly 90% of the policies already carried extended or guaranteed replacement cost. Most homes destroyed in catastrophes still came up short.

Hayward carries 182, Milpitas 121, Newark 88 and Union City 69. Across all five cities the median is $1,089,500 at $679 a square foot, 291 homes are priced under a million, and 167 have been sitting 60 days or longer.

What it means for you. If you own, ask your insurer for a fresh replacement cost estimate rather than trusting the one written with the policy, and check whether extended replacement cost is on it. If you are buying, line up insurance quotes before your contingencies come off. If you are selling, a recent estimate is one less worry you can hand a buyer.
