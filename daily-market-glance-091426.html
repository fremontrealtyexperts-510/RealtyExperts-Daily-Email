Mortgage News Daily has the 30-year at 7.17% and the 15-year at 6.70%, both up 0.05 from Friday and the highest readings of the past year. The newsletter's 7.12% and 6.65% were Friday's numbers.
The reason is inflation. Consumer prices rose 3.4% over the past year in August, and the Fed meets Wednesday. Kalshi and Polymarket both put the odds of a quarter-point hike at 84.5% this morning. The Fed does not set mortgage rates, but a hike keeps the pressure on the bond yields that do, and the 10-Year closed Friday at 4.96%, its highest close since at least January 2024.
• The local math: at Fremont's median list price of $1,249,000, a 20% down loan is $999,200. That fits under Alameda County's $1,249,125 high-balance conforming limit, so it is not a jumbo. At MND's 30-year of 7.17%, principal and interest run about $6,762 a month, and high-balance loans often price a little above that.
• Every eighth of a point on that loan is about $84 a month, which is why lock timing around Wednesday matters.

Levels are Friday, September 11 at the closing bell. Monday is live: at 11:19 AM PT the S&P was 7,635.80 (-0.28%), the Dow 52,437.72 (-0.26%) and the Nasdaq 26,298.74 (-0.13%).
Friday snapped a four day losing streak, all three indexes up close to 1%. Monday is giving a little of it back, with all three modestly lower at midday.
Dell was Friday's star, up 11.98% to a record $567.29 after RBC Capital Markets started coverage at outperform with a $640 target, pointing to the AI server business. The newsletter's text said 10%; its ticker had it right.
Today's chart is the one making the rounds, and its numbers are right: Dell's AI server backlog went from $43 billion in January to $51.3 billion in May to $95 billion at the end of July. Nearly doubled is a stretch; it rose 85% in one quarter. Our version adds the why. Dell booked $60.9 billion of AI server orders last quarter, more than its entire $47.0 billion of revenue, while shipping $16.4 billion, about the same as the quarter before. Orders are outrunning shipping, and Dell raised its AI server forecast for the fiscal year to $74 billion from $60 billion. The shares were down about 4% Monday late morning.
Oracle went the other way. A filing Friday showed Larry Ellison planned to sell up to 50 million Oracle shares, about $7.5 billion; on Saturday he canceled the plan, and Oracle says none were sold. The stock fell 1.74% Friday and was down about 4.5% Monday.

All four are Friday, September 11. The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve close, gold and silver are the COMEX front month at Friday's 1:00 PM PT print measured against Thursday's same time print, and Brent is the ICE front month settlement. Monday so far: at 11:10 AM PT gold was $4,352.50, silver $64.19, and Brent was trading near $105.66, a live tick, not a settlement.
Inflation is the story. Consumer prices rose 0.4% in August and 3.4% over the past year. Energy did most of the damage: the energy index is up 16.3% in a year, gasoline 27.4% and fuel oil 52%. Strip out food and energy and core prices are up 2.4%, the lowest reading this year.
Today's chart is the second one making the rounds, and all sixteen of its points match BLS. Its headline is right about the year: the gap between the two lines is energy. What it leaves out is the month. Core rose 0.3% in August, its biggest monthly gain since April, and at 2.4% it is still above the Fed's 2% goal. That goal is set for a different gauge, PCE, so read it as a yardstick.
That is why the Fed is expected to hike Wednesday, not cut. Kalshi and Polymarket both put a quarter-point hike at 84.5% this morning; the newsletter said 90%. The 10-Year closed Friday at 4.96%, its highest close since at least January 2024.
Oil eased from its high. Brent settled at $104.61 Friday, down 2.8% after Thursday's jump to $107.63, and was trading near $105.66 Monday. Metals bounced: at Friday's 1:00 PM PT print gold rose 1.2% to $4,408.90 and silver 1.7% to $65.19, and both were lower again Monday morning.
A note on the newsletter's numbers: its stock, CPI and fuel oil figures check out. It had the 10-Year at 4.97% against Treasury's 4.96%, gold at $4,386, down 0.51%, where the same time prints show $4,408.90, up 1.16%, and Brent at $107.39, up 2.66%, where the settlement was $104.61, down 2.81%. Its mortgage rates were Friday's, and silver was left out again.
Headline inflation is 3.4% and core is 2.4%; the gap between them is energy, up 16.3% in a year. Core still rose 0.3% in August.
