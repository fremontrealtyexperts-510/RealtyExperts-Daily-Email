REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.37% +0.04  |  15-Year Fixed: 7.04% +0.06

Fewer buyers are signing. Redfin says pending home sales fell 4% from August to September, the biggest monthly drop in more than three years, to the lowest level since 2014 outside the pandemic lockdown. West Coast metros slowed the most, with Seattle down 24.4% from a year ago and Los Angeles down 16%.

Rates are the reason. Freddie Mac's weekly average held at 7.40% on Thursday, its highest since November 2023 and up from 6.27% a year ago. Mortgage News Daily's index ticked up Friday to 7.37% for the 30-year and 7.04% for the 15-year. On our five city median list price of $1,088,000 with 20% down, principal and interest run about $6,009 a month, roughly $661 more than at last October's 6.23%. The house did not get pricier; the money did.

Supply keeps building here. The board wide table rose to 2,681 from 2,645 as coming soon homes went live: brand new listings jumped to 196 from 153 while coming soon fell to 133 from 155. Newark rose to 95 live listings from 90 and Union City to 66 from 64.

What it means for you. If you can carry today's payment, you face fewer rivals and more choice, with 165 homes across the five cities listed 60 days or longer. If you are selling, price for the buyers shopping now, not the ones waiting on rates.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 5.09% -0.01  |  Gold: $4,181.10 +0.26%  |  Silver: $57.31 +0.26%  |  Brent Crude: $105.92 -0.13%

Holiday sales should speed up. The National Retail Federation forecast Thursday that November and December sales will grow 4.2% to a record $1.013 trillion, after 3.1% last year. Bain sees 4.5%, Deloitte 4.0% to 4.8% and Mastercard 5.5%.

Compare like with like. The newsletter's chart set every forecast against NRF's 3.1%, but each firm counts differently, so I put each forecast next to its own 2025 result. Three of four still speed up; Deloitte's range sits around its 4.1% from last year. About half the gain is prices, per Mastercard and Bain. Santa's sleigh costs more to fuel this year.

Oil jumped, bonds held. Brent settled Friday at $109.86, up 3.72%, as attacks on tankers around the Strait of Hormuz picked up; the card shows Thursday's $105.92 settlement. The 10-Year closed at 5.09%, after Thursday's drop to 5.10% from 5.19%. Gold and silver are Friday's settlements.

A correction. The newsletter showed Thursday's 10-Year at 5.07%, gold up 0.21% and Brent at $105.30. Treasury's close was 5.10%, gold settled up 1.51%, and Brent settled at $105.92, down 0.13%.
