Mortgage Rates Edge Up To 6.68% As Oil Slides And CPI Looms Wednesday; East Bay Board Eases To 2,886 Active On A Steady Tuesday Of 252 New And 224 Closed
The 30-year fixed edged up to 6.68% and the 15-year to 6.20%, both just a hair above yesterday. The reason it wasn't more is the 10-year Treasury, which held flat at 4.55% — mortgage rates take their cue from that 10-year, so when it sits still, they sit still. The real swing factor is tomorrow (Wednesday): the May CPI report. Working in buyers' favor, oil slid hard as the Middle East de-escalated and OPEC moved to pump more (see Economy); if that holds, it eases the inflation pressure that has kept rates elevated.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,886 active, down 26 from Monday's 2,912. The day's flow was steady and healthy: 252 new listings came on and 224 sales closed. New listings cooled slightly from Monday's 258, while closings actually rose (224 vs 217) — buyers kept clearing inventory even as fewer fresh homes hit the market, which is why the board edged lower.
A national note for clients: co-buying is on the rise. The share of buyers purchasing a home with someone other than a spouse climbed from 25% in 2021 to 30% in 2025, and nearly 60% of renters say they'd buy with a friend, per a Rocket Mortgage survey. The math is simple — one income rarely clears today's Bay Area prices, but two or three can. New platforms are even matching strangers who want in. It's a trend worth raising with first-time buyers who feel priced out solo.

Stocks steadied on Monday after Friday's sharp sell-off, but the bounce was uneven. The NASDAQ led with a 0.86% gain as beaten-down chip and tech names found buyers, the S&P 500 edged up 0.30%, and the DOW slipped 0.16% — a split that reads as the AI trade stabilizing rather than roaring back.

The 10-year Treasury held steady at 4.55%, leaving the rate backdrop quiet for a change — but two macro shifts are worth watching. First, oil slid: both Israel and Iran said they are halting strikes for now, and OPEC announced it will raise output by 188,000 barrels a day in July. Brent crude eased to $93.42 on the news. Lower oil tends to filter through to gas prices, grocery bills, and headline inflation, so a sustained pullback would be welcome relief.
Second, the next big test lands tomorrow (Wednesday): the May CPI report. Closer to home, a New York Fed survey shows household money worries just hit their highest level since 2022 — the share of people who feel "much worse" off than a year ago jumped to 13.3%, even as the one-year inflation outlook slipped to 3.5%. Gold eased slightly to $4,345 (-0.21%).

Crypto was quiet, drifting modestly lower across the board: Bitcoin held $63,136 (-0.12%), Ether eased to $1,682 (-0.41%), and XRP slipped to $1.17 (-0.24%). The notable move came from Strategy (MSTR +5.61%) — the company formerly known as MicroStrategy — which scooped up another 1,550 Bitcoin for $10 million, roughly 50 times what it had just sold a week earlier in its first sale since 2022. The firm also built its cash pile to $1 billion to cover dividend payments; it is still down about $10 billion on its Bitcoin position and still buying.
Meanwhile, the ETF picture is shifting. Money is pouring into a new kind of crypto fund tracking the HYPE token — close to $150 million since May — even as BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust fell about 16% in a week. For Bay Area buyers and sellers the tie-in is indirect, but crypto swings still color the risk appetite of a slice of the market.
