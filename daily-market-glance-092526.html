Rates finally took a breather. Mortgage News Daily has the 30-year at 7.43% today, down 0.02, and the 15-year unchanged at 7.10%. Thursday's 7.45% was the top of the 52 week range. The newsletter printed 7.45% and 7.10%, which were Thursday's readings.
Sellers are starting to respond. The newsletter's lead is price cuts multiplying as rates climb, and today's MLS export shows 95 listings across our 15 city table with a price change status, 22 of them in Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City. The 10-Year closed Friday at 5.17%, a hair under Thursday's 5.18%, the highest close since 2007, so the pressure has not lifted.
Here is what that means on a real Fremont home. At today's median on market list price of $1,199,000, with 20% down, the loan is $959,200, which is high balance conforming here, not jumbo. Principal and interest is $6,661 a month at 7.43%, against $5,745 at 5.99%, the 52 week low. That is $916 more every month for the same house.

The 10-Year, gold and silver are Friday, September 25: the Treasury daily yield curve close and the COMEX front month settlements against Thursday's. Brent is Thursday's settlement of the ICE November contract, the front month.
Layoffs are still rare. First time jobless claims fell to 197,000 in the week ending September 19, down 1,000, and the 4-week average slipped to 202,250, its lowest since early August. The same week a year ago was 219,000. Closer to home, California logged 34,931 claims, 6.6% fewer than the same week of 2025.
The 10-Year closed Friday at 5.17%, down 0.01, after Thursday's 5.18%, the highest close since 2007. The newsletter printed 5.19% and up 0.03 for Thursday; the Treasury curve says 5.18% and up 0.07. Gold settled up 0.54% at $4,321.20 and silver up 1.25% at $64.80.
The Brent card is Thursday's settlement, $106.60, up 3.41%; the newsletter had $105.97 and down 0.58%. Friday it gave back 2.14% to settle at $104.32, on hopes that shipping through the Strait of Hormuz could reopen.
Weekly claims are at 197,000 and the 4-week average at 202,250, both below the 219,000 of the same week last year. California's own count fell 6.6% from a year earlier.
Source: U.S. Department of Labor weekly claims via FRED (ICSA, IC4WSA, CAICLAIMS), week ending September 19, 2026.
