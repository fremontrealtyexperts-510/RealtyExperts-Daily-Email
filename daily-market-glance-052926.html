Rates Hold At 6.59% Into The Weekend; East Bay Board Builds A Third Day To 2,964 Active (216 New); SF Rents Tie NYC As Priciest In The Nation
The 30-year fixed is flat at 6.59%, the 15-year at 6.11%, a quiet end to the week on rates. The bigger rate story is brewing in the data: Core PCE ticked up to 3.3% (more in Economy), and the market is now leaning toward the Fed's next move being a hike, not a cut. The buyer waiting for lower rates is waiting on something that may not arrive.
Local board: 2,964 active (up 21 from yesterday), 216 new listings (up 23), 216 closed sales. New supply and closings ran dead even at 216 each, so the board keeps building but it is clearing what it adds.
National: San Francisco just tied New York as the most expensive rental market in the country, both at $5,500 a month for a 2-bedroom (see chart below). The driver is AI hiring at OpenAI, Anthropic, and the rest, which pushed SF rents up 20-25% in a year with vacancy down near 3.3%. The robots aren't taking the jobs so much as taking the apartments. Separately, Florida's governor wants to scrap property taxes for 60% of homeowners by raising the homestead exemption to $250,000.

The inflation print is the story. Core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose to 3.3% annually in April, well above the 2% target. Wall Street's read: the Fed's next move is now more likely a hike than a cut. Consumer spending rose 0.5% while incomes stayed flat, dropping the savings rate to 2.6%, its lowest since 2022. Households are spending down their cushion.
Oil eased on reports of a possible Strait of Hormuz reopening. Brent slipped to $92.76 and WTI to $88.70, though Iran launched fresh attacks even as a deal was floated, so nobody is keeping the story straight yet. Gold held near $4,530.
