Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.58% — But The “Lock-In Effect” Keeps Supply Tight; The East Bay Board Climbs To 2,956 Active On 229 New And 210 Closed
The 30-year fixed eased to 6.58% and the 15-year to 6.15% — a touch of relief, though still roughly double the rate most homeowners are sitting on.
And that gap is the whole story. Homeowners pulled $47 billion in equity in Q1 — the biggest first-quarter haul since 2021 — rather than refinance into today's far higher rates. Around 20% still hold a mortgage near 3% (see the chart below), and those locked-in low rates are a big reason so few homes come to market. The squeeze is generational: the U.S. is short about 4 million homes, roughly 25 million adults under 35 have paused the search to live with parents, and the typical first-time buyer is now 40 years old.
Locally, the East Bay board climbed to 2,956 active (up 59 from the June 18th report), with 229 new listings against 210 closings. Oakland again led the board with 56 new on 781 active; Fremont added 16 new on 360 active.

New-listing flow rose to 229 (up 25 from the June 18th report's 204), led by Oakland (56), Livermore (28), and Danville (20). Closings held near steady at 210.
For agents: rates ticked down but barely, and the lock-in effect isn't breaking soon — so the move is to work the inventory that IS coming (2,956 active, 229 new). Well-priced, well-presented homes still sell inside about four weeks. Coach sellers that they're competing against thin supply, not just rates; remind buyers that today's softer prices — more than the rate — are where the opening is.
About 70% of U.S. homeowners hold a mortgage rate under 5% — and roughly 1 in 5 are still locked in below 3%. With today's 30-year near 6.6%, few have any reason to sell and reset, which keeps for-sale inventory unusually tight. The “lock-in effect” in one picture.

Oil is back in focus: Iran again moved to close the Strait of Hormuz — a chokepoint for a large share of the world's oil — just as its first talks with the U.S. opened in Switzerland. Traffic slowed to a single small tanker, yet crude actually fell on the week on hopes for a deal, with Brent easing to $78.96. (The U.S. maintains the Strait never truly closed.)
The bond market held steady, with the 10-year Treasury at 4.48%. Gold eased to $4,227 (-0.44%) while silver firmed to about $66.43 (+2.36%).
Two data points underscore a stretched consumer. Household debt hit a record $19.9 trillion last quarter even as the savings rate slipped to 2.6% — near a 65-year low — leaving families with a thin cushion if jobs soften. At the same time, May retail sales jumped 0.9% (nearly double expectations) to $763.7B, with a third of the gain coming from gas stations, up 26.5% from a year ago. Solid spending only strengthens the Fed's case to keep rates higher for longer.
