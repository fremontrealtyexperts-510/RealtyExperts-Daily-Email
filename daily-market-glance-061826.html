Mortgage Rates Tick Up To 6.62% As The Fed Holds — And Hints At Hikes; The East Bay Board Firms To 2,897 Active On 204 New And 206 Closed
The 30-year fixed ticked up to 6.62% and the 15-year to 6.16% after the Fed held rates and signaled it may even hike later this year — a reminder that the path to cheaper mortgages isn't a straight line.
Nationally, the affordability story keeps stretching. The median U.S. home — around $403,000 today, up from about $90,000 in 1990 — is projected to reach roughly $1 million by 2050 (see the chart below). And the space economy is spilling into real estate: Florida's Space Coast is becoming a hot hotel market, with a new $420M Westin going up for the crowds flying in for SpaceX and Blue Origin launches.
Locally, the East Bay board firmed to 2,897 active (up 39 from yesterday), with 204 new listings against 206 closings. Oakland again led the board with 54 new on 760 active; Fremont added 18 new on 356 active.

For agents: rates nudged up and the Fed even hinted at hikes, so the buyer waiting for a big drop may keep waiting — but inventory is healthy (2,897 active, 204 new) and well-priced, well-presented homes still move inside about four weeks. Coach sellers to price right and show well; remind buyers that today's softer prices, more than rates, are where the opening is.

Stocks fell across the board — the S&P 500 down 1.21%, the DOW 0.98%, and the NASDAQ 1.34% — after the Fed held rates and signaled a possible hike later this year.
SpaceX (SPCX -4.95%) finally "landed" on Wall Street: options on the stock launched Tuesday and broke the single-day trading record (previously held by Meta), but the shares slipped for the first time since listing last week. It remains one of the most valuable public companies in the world.
The memory-chip squeeze is reaching your pocket. Apple (AAPL -1.10%) CEO Tim Cook said the company will raise prices on many devices soon, citing a memory shortage driven by AI demand — some analysts see the next iPhone up as much as $270. Samsung, Microsoft, Sony, and Dell have already hiked prices for the same reason.
On trade: President Trump said he wants the USMCA pact terminated (its review is July 1st), and the G7 agreed no country should import more than 60% of its rare earths from a single source — a shot at China, which refines roughly 70% of the world's critical minerals.

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh led his first meeting yesterday, and the Fed left interest rates unchanged. The bigger picture: roughly half the committee now thinks rates may need to rise by year-end — a hawkish shift, with the Bank of Japan and European Central Bank having recently hiked. Higher-for-longer rates mean less spending, which could slow both AI build-outs and home buying. The chart below traces the Fed's benchmark rate from near-zero through the pandemic up to its 5.33% peak and back down to 3.63% today.
Metals were mixed. The 10-year Treasury held at 4.45% and gold tumbled 2.64% to $4,265 as the rate outlook firmed, while Brent crude eased to $78.55. Silver bucked the trend, firming about 1.5% to $68.91. Separately, a new ADP survey found just 22% of workers worldwide feel their job is safe — a quiet drag on productivity even with unemployment near record lows.
Warsh's first meeting ended with no change. Here's the Fed's benchmark rate over the past decade — near zero through the pandemic, a sharp climb to a 5.33% peak in 2023–24, and a glide down to 3.63% today. With half the committee now eyeing a hike, the next move is the one to watch.
