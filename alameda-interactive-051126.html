Builders Cut New-Home Prices To 5-Year Low As 9-In-10 Voters Call Buying Harder Than Ever

Two national signals worth carrying into Bay Area conversations this week. New home prices fell to a five-year low in March, with 682,000 new homes sold and many now getting price cuts. That’s the new-construction segment caving on price, which historically pressures resale within six to nine months. Buyers watching the builder discount tape have a window before resale repositions.

The NAR voter poll: nine in ten say buying a home is harder now than ever. Only 17% think now is a good time to buy, down from 69% in 2013. Just 9% think federal housing policy is helping. That’s the consumer mindset framing every showing. The qualified buyer still in the hunt has internalized both the rate environment and the affordability math, they are not waiting for a print.

Rates eased a fraction. The 30-year sits at 6.42% (-0.02%), the 15-year at 5.99% (-0.01%). Holding near 6.4% gives buyers time to underwrite a known number.

Indices Close Green; Burry Calls Market 1999-2000 Bubble; TTEC Suspends 401k Match For AI Cash

Tech led Friday’s tape with NASDAQ +1.71%. The S&P closed at 7,398 (+0.84%), DOW at 49,609 (+0.02%), NASDAQ at 26,247 (+1.71%).

Two threads worth watching under the price. Michael Burry, the Big Short investor shorting Palantir (PLTR +0.54%) and exited his GameStop stake, just called this market the late innings of the 1999-2000 bubble. Take Burry as a sentiment read, not a trade signal, but he is worth tracking.

The other thread is the workplace cost side of AI. TTEC (TTEC -11.44%) suspended its U.S. 401k company match through 2026 to free cash for AI investments. Deloitte and Zoom have pulled back parental leave and pension benefits this year. AI is no longer a future P&L line item, it is actively redirecting compensation budgets. Toyota (TM -0.78%) missed the quarter and cut full-year guidance, citing U.S. tariffs and Chinese rivals, while committing $10B to U.S. plant investment over the next decade.

WTI Rips +4.43%; April Payrolls Double Consensus; Consumer Sentiment Fresh Record Low

The 10-Year barely moved at 4.39% (+0.02%), gold cooled to $4,697 (-0.70%), and WTI ripped to $99.65 (+4.43%) intraday. Brent moved in lockstep at $105.03 (+3.69%). That is the kind of one-day energy jump that shows up in inflation prints two cycles out, worth watching.

April payrolls came in at 115,000, double the consensus, with unemployment held at 4.3%. Strong on the surface. The fine print: information services shed another 13,000 jobs and is now down 342,000 since November 2022. The AI displacement story now has a number on it.

University of Michigan consumer sentiment just hit a fresh record low in April, breaking the record set in March. One in three Americans blamed gas, one in three blamed tariffs. With gas already at $4.56 nationally and crude up another 4% this morning, the sentiment ceiling stays capped through summer.
