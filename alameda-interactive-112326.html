REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.34% +0.01  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.89% 0.00

Young adults here are more likely to live with a parent. Realtor.com's featured report this week maps where young adults live with their parents. In the Northeast, 36.9% of 18 to 34 year olds do, well above the national rate, though it is also the only region that has improved. Our cities run higher still on the Census Bureau's own measure: averaging 2020 to 2024, 48.0% of Union City's 18 to 34 year olds lived in a parent's household, Hayward 40.9%, Newark 38.9%, Fremont 36.4% and Milpitas 33.0%, against 39.3% for California and 32.8% for the nation.

More choice, slower sales. In realtor.com's latest weekly data, homes for sale are up 8.6% from a year ago, new listings are down 3.5%, and the median list price of $405,000 is down 2.4%, the 44th straight week below last year. Homes are taking 66 days to sell, two days longer than a year ago.

Rates held. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.34%, up 0.01 from Friday, with the 15-year at 6.89%, and Freddie Mac's weekly survey last Thursday was 7.28%.

What it means for you. If you are a parent with a grown child at home, this is a good week to run the numbers on a first purchase together. If you are buying, listings are sitting longer, so there is room to negotiate.

Today's count. Board wide: 2,244 listings across the 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, down from 2,262 Saturday, with 126 coming soon and 91 brand new. The five city ledger carries 560 live listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, 496 on the market and 64 coming soon, down from 565.

Fremont holds 194 live listings: 159 active, 23 coming soon and 10 brand new, with a median on market list price of $1,248,000 at $774 a square foot and a typical listing out 41 days; with 20% down at 7.34%, that median runs about $6,872 a month in principal and interest. Hayward carries 161, Milpitas 85, Newark 67 and Union City 53. Across all five cities the median is $1,000,000 at $668 a square foot, 248 homes are priced under a million, and 184 have been sitting 60 days or longer.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,237.17 +4.73%  |  Dow: 49,595.31 +3.01%  |  Nasdaq: 24,089.56 +6.51%

Huang is back. In a filing with the SEC this morning, Nvidia said its board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as President, CEO and director on Sunday, 11 days after he stepped down. Colette Kress, who led the company in the meantime, stays on as CFO, and the board's independent committee will carry its investigation to a conclusion. Nvidia jumped 31.48% to $165.05, its biggest one day gain in more than ten years and one of its four best since it went public in 1999. That is roughly $952 billion of market value added in a single day. The stock is still 31.0% below its Oct. 6 record.

The best day since April 2025. Everything that fell last week bounced. The S&P 500 rose 4.73% and the Nasdaq 6.51%, both their best days since April 9, 2025, and the Dow gained 3.01%. Chips led, this time upward: the Philadelphia semiconductor index gained 14.03%, Micron 19.04% and AMD 6.85%, and the VIX fear gauge fell to 26.50. Anthropic went the other way, down 1.43% to $87.64, still above its $83 offering price. The S&P 500 (7.4% below its record) and the Dow (8.7% below) are back out of correction territory, while the Nasdaq, 12.7% below, is still in one. One great day does not erase a rough month.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 4.92% 0.00  |  Gold: $3,651.40 +3.87%  |  Silver: $43.18 +5.13%  |  Brent Crude: $92.57 +0.64%

Bonds sat this one out. The 10-Year Treasury closed Monday at 4.92%, unchanged from Friday, so mortgage rates barely moved even as stocks soared.

Metals bounced. Gold settled at $3,651.40, up 3.87%, and silver at $43.18, up 5.13%, winning back most of Friday's losses. Brent's card is Friday's ICE settlement, $92.57.

The Fed is next. Its Dec. 8 to 9 meeting is the next test for markets. October consumer prices rose 0.4%, and minutes from the last meeting showed almost all officials expect further hikes. Good news for stocks is not yet good news for rates.
