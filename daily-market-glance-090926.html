Mortgage rates did not move again. Mortgage News Daily has the 30-year at 6.89% and the 15-year at 6.49%, both unchanged on Tuesday, which makes three sessions running at the same level. The 10-Year Treasury drifted up to 4.80%, so if anything the pressure is gently upward from here.
The story worth your attention has nothing to do with rates. Canada's counter tariffs took effect yesterday, and the rates land on things that go into houses: 50% on steel and aluminum, 25% on appliances like washers and dryers, 15% on machinery parts and air conditioners.
• Read the chart below carefully, because the tidy version circulating today is misleading. These are rate tiers, not category rates. Canada set each product's rate to mirror the matching U.S. tariff on the same good, so one everyday category straddles tiers: an air conditioner is taxed at 15% while a washing machine is taxed at 25%.
• Direction matters too. These are Canadian tariffs on goods going north, so the direct hit is to U.S. exporters. What reaches a Bay Area remodel is the second order effect, through cross border supply chains and steel pricing that is already at records.
• One number to correct: the order covers C$27.6 billion of imports, Canadian dollars. Reported as an unmarked "$27.6B" it reads as U.S. dollars to an American audience. At today's exchange rate it is about US$20 billion, so the unmarked figure overstates the scope by roughly 38%.

All four are Tuesday, September 8. The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve close, gold and silver are the COMEX front month at Tuesday's 4:00 PM ET print measured against Friday's same time print, and Brent is the ICE front month settlement.
Gold gave back 1.7% on Tuesday to close near $4,400 an ounce, its sharpest drop in over a week, while silver held up better at a little over $66. Brent settled at $97.92, up 1.7%, and is trading above $100 this morning. The 10-Year crept up to 4.80%, the highest close in more than a week.
The survey behind today's second chart is a genuinely interesting read on how new technology actually gets adopted. McKinsey asked 5,500 farmers across 10 countries about AI, and 17% now use generative AI in farm work, making it the fastest spreading technology in agriculture. The catch is in the split: of that 17%, about 12 points are people using free versions and only 4 points are paying for a tool.
The more interesting number is trust. Just 6% of farmers treat an AI chatbot as a trusted input when deciding what to buy. That sounds dismissive until you see it was 1% in 2024, which makes it the fastest growing purchasing input in the whole survey. For scale, web search sits at 18% and social media at 9%. Trust is being earned quickly, from almost nothing.
It is a useful mirror for any business, including this one. People will try a new tool enthusiastically, use the free version indefinitely, and take considerably longer before they let it influence a decision that costs money.
A note on the newsletter's numbers, because two did not hold up. It put the 10-Year at 4.79% and up 0.01, while Treasury's own daily curve shows 4.80% against Friday's 4.78%, a move of 0.02. It also had Brent at $99.27 against the ICE front month settlement of $97.92. Gold at $4,401 was right this time, and silver was left out again, so we sourced it ourselves.
