The 30-year fixed ticked up to 6.65% and the 15-year to 6.19%. Rates drifting higher keeps the lock-in effect firmly in place, with owners staying put rather than trading a low existing rate for a higher new one, which keeps resale supply tight.
At the high end, luxury agents are dressing for success and then some. One reportedly spent over $100,000 on clothes last year, chasing the wave of newly minted millionaires from this year's IPO boom who expect to be wowed from the house to the agent.
Capital is also moving south. Billionaire Peter Thiel just signed the most expensive office lease in Miami history, part of a broader migration of tech founders fleeing California's proposed wealth tax. If more follow, it could push Florida home prices and rents even higher for locals.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,794 active (down 11 from yesterday), with 143 new listings against 212 closings. Oakland again led the board with 30 new on 741 active; Fremont added 18 new on 335 active.

Stocks slipped to open the third quarter. The S&P 500 eased 0.22% to 7,483, the NASDAQ fell 0.66% to 26,040, and the DOW held roughly flat at 52,305 (figures as of the July 1st close).
The chip rally hit a wall. Micron (-10.57%), Sandisk (-10.62%), and other memory makers tumbled after a report that Meta (+8.81%) may rent out unused AI capacity, raising worries that the supply of processing power is finally catching up to demand. Meta is launching a cloud business to sell that spare compute, one way to earn back some of the billions it has poured into AI.
A blast from the past also went public: Bending Spoons (+39.66%), the owner of AOL, Vimeo, and Eventbrite, IPO'd and raised $1.68 billion, betting it can convert some of its 500 million free users into paying members.

Stocks, 10-Year, Brent, and crypto as of the July 1st close (Market Briefs); gold and silver are spot as of July 2nd (Trading Economics).
Metals jumped. Gold rose about 2.01% to $4,112 and silver surged 3.88% to $61.38 as a weak jobs report cooled bets that the Fed will hike, while the 10-year Treasury yield held at 4.48% and Brent crude eased 0.91% to $70.92.
Hiring slowed. Companies added just 98,000 private jobs in June according to ADP, short of the 110,000 estimate, as businesses stay cautious with the Fed holding rates high. The level of employment is still near a record, but the pace of hiring is clearly cooling (see today's chart).
Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation is still too high but would not signal where rates go next, instead standing up five task forces to study how the Fed operates. Even so, CME data shows nearly half of Wall Street now expects at least one rate hike by the fall.
For housing, a softer labor market is the kind of thing that eventually pulls mortgage rates down, but not yet. The 30-year actually ticked up this week, so relief stays on the horizon rather than at the closing table.

Crypto firmed up. Bitcoin added 0.28% to $60,133, back above $60,000, while Ether rose 0.53% to $1,616 and XRP gained 0.46% to $1.06 (figures as of the July 1st close).
Coinbase (+8.93%) teamed up with Visa (+2.33%) and Mastercard (+1.72%) to launch a new U.S.-backed stablecoin. The Open USD coin is already backed by 140 companies, including Google and BlackRock, and pegs its value to the dollar, which may look more appealing to investors after Bitcoin's recent slump.
On the policy side, federal filings show President Trump earned over $1 billion from crypto in 2025, most of it tied to World Liberty Financial, as the administration keeps pushing the stalled Clarity Act.
