  REAL ESTATE

  
U.S. home prices slipped below a year ago and rents rose above it, both for the first time in three years. In our five cities that flip is at least a year old, and rents are now rising faster.

Today's theme is the flip. Redfin says the typical U.S. home sold for $389,601 in the four weeks to Oct. 25, down 0.1% from a year earlier, the first annual decline in more than three years. Apartment List says the national median rent, $1,384, is now 0.1% above a year ago, its first annual gain in over three years. Both are year-over-year changes; Market Briefs called them last month's moves, and October rents actually slipped 0.3% month to month.
Here the flip is at least a year old (chart). Zillow's September data has rents up in all five of our cities, from 3.9% in Hayward to 10.1% in Milpitas, and typical values down in all five, from 1.2% in Milpitas to 2.4% in Hayward and Newark. A year earlier, values were falling 3% to 7% and rents rising 1% to 3.5%, so the declines are shrinking while rent gains speed up. Next door the picture is mixed: Redfin has San Jose sale prices down 4.9%, the second steepest drop among big metros, and San Francisco's up 15%.
Rates held. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.27% and the 15-year 6.92%, unchanged since Friday. At 7.27%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,329,000 median with 20% down would pay about $7,267 a month in principal and interest, about 2.1 times Zillow's $3,388 typical Fremont rent. The gap is still wide, but it is closing from both ends.
The board wide table is 2,457, from 2,482 Monday, with 155 coming soon and 139 brand new. The five city ledger is 648, from 650: Fremont (221) added one, Union City (61) dropped three, and Hayward (183), Milpitas (99) and Newark (84) held. Coming soon rose to 73 from 67, led by Fremont's 34, so more supply is on deck.
What this means for you: the renewal notice is the new open house. Renters facing increases like these should price a purchase now, while values sit below last year; 272 of the 575 homes on the market in our five cities are priced under a million. Sellers, buyers can see these numbers too, and 180 homes have been sitting 60 days or longer, so price to this year's market, not last year's.

Levels are Monday, November 2 at the closing bell. Live at 8:00 AM PT Tuesday: S&P 500 up 0.58%, Dow down 0.20%, Nasdaq up 1.03%.
Tech is bouncing after the Nasdaq's lowest close since July 30. As of 8:00 AM PT the Nasdaq is up 1.03%, the S&P 500 up 0.58% and the Dow down 0.20%. Monday was a split day: the Dow gained 0.41% while the Nasdaq fell 1.07% and the S&P 500 0.51%.
Apple and Palantir reported after Monday's close, and both are lower anyway. Apple, under CEO John Ternus, posted record September quarter revenue of $114.5 billion, up 12%, with iPhone sales up 17% to $57.6 billion and Greater China up 21%; the stock is down 2.7%. Palantir's revenue rose 96% to $2.31 billion, and its U.S. commercial sales, up 148% to $984 million, passed its U.S. government sales for the first time. Its shares are down 1.5%, a reminder that a great quarter is not the same as a surprise.

Oil's slide held. Brent settled Monday at $104.77, down 7.6%, after Iran signaled talks that could reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and it is down another 1.59% this morning near $103.10. Cheaper oil would help with inflation, which ran 3.7% in September with core at 2.6%. With inflation there, two Fed officials voted for a rate hike last week, and mortgage rates are still above 7%.
Metals bounced Monday, with gold up 1.81% to $3,969.60 and silver up 3.27% to $50.89 after Friday's selloff, and both are giving some back this morning. Bitcoin is taking the exodus calmly: it is up 0.28% over 24 hours near $77,635, Ether is up 1.17% and XRP is up 2.53%, live from CoinGecko at 8:00 AM PT.
Binance reopened withdrawals Sunday after last week's record $6.3 billion hack, and in the first 9.5 hours customers pulled out $4.65 billion and put in $1.01 billion, a $3.64 billion net outflow (chart). That was 2.1% of the exchange's $171 billion in assets, and Binance says it has now covered about 95% of the stolen amount, up from 90%.
