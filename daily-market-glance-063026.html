Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.52% As A Soft Jobs Report Looms; Wall Street Keeps Rolling Up Real Estate Managers; The East Bay Board Slips To 2,901 Active On 217 New And 198 Closed
The 30-year fixed ticked down to 6.52% and the 15-year held at 6.12%. With this week's jobs report in focus, a soft number could pull rates a little lower, but the lock-in effect keeping resale supply tight is not going anywhere until the gap to owners' existing rates narrows.
Wall Street keeps buying the plumbing of real estate. Private equity firm Bridgepoint agreed to buy Kayne Anderson's real estate arm for $1.4 billion, the latest in a string of deals, with Berkshire having scooped up homebuilder Taylor Morrison for $8 billion a few weeks back, as big firms race to offer clients more ways to invest in property.
On the other side of the ledger, Google co-founder Sergey Brin sold his stake in a fund tied to rent-controlled New York apartments for just six cents on the dollar, a reminder that when the rules cap the rent, they cap the value too.
Locally, the East Bay board slipped to 2,901 active (down 44 from yesterday), with 217 new listings against 198 closings. Oakland again led the board with 44 new on 772 active; Fremont added 28 new on 348 active.

The jobs report is the week's big tell. Traders on the Kalshi betting market put only a 42% chance on June adding more than 125,000 jobs, well short of May's surprising 172,000. A soft number would nudge the Fed toward cutting sooner, which would be welcome news for mortgage rates, even if it signals a cooling economy.
Metals slipped. Gold fell 1.54% to $3,976 and silver eased to about $58.50, while copper held just above $6 a pound. Brent crude dipped 0.70% to $72.64 and the 10-year Treasury yield sat flat at 4.37%. For housing, a cooler read on the economy is a double-edged sword: lower rates would help, but only because growth is slowing.
