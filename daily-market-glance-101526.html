Mortgage rates hit 7.5%, and local homes in contract are down 20% from a year ago.

Buyers are pulling back nationally. The Mortgage Bankers Association's 30-year contract rate reached 7.50% last week, its highest since November 2023 and more than a point above a year ago. Purchase applications fell 4% on the week and are 16% below the same week last year, and adjustable rate loans rose to 10.5% of applications as borrowers hunt for a lower payment.
We checked our own MLS export against the same day last year, and the slowdown is here too. Across the board's 14 cities, homes in contract fell 20%, to 686 from 858, while homes for sale slipped just 4%, to 1,979 from 2,072. Newark (35 to 20) and Union City (30 to 23) saw the sharpest drop in signed deals even as their for sale counts rose 36% and 56%. Hayward held up best, with contracts down 6%.
MND's latest reading is Wednesday's: 7.45% for the 30-year and 7.14% for the 15-year. On our five city median list price of $1,064,944 with 20% down, principal and interest run about $5,928 a month, roughly $649 more than at last October's 6.31%. That is a car payment, minus the car. The 10-Year fell to about 5.10% this morning, so Thursday's reading could come in lower.
Locally, the five city ledger reads 683, up from 677. Fremont rose to 239 from 234 and Newark to 90 from 89, while Hayward (189), Milpitas (101) and Union City (64) held. Of the 606 homes on the market, 167 have been listed 60 days or longer, up from 165. The board wide table edged up to 2,645 from 2,639, and brand new listings fell to 153 from 169.
What this means for you: with fewer buyers signing, the ones still shopping have room to negotiate, especially in Newark and Union City where supply has grown. Sellers should price for the market we have now; 167 homes have already sat 60 days or longer.
• Board wide: 2,645 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 155 coming soon and 153 brand new.

Levels are Wednesday, October 14 at the closing bell. Thursday at 8:49 AM PT: S&P 500 up 0.09%, Dow up 0.95%, Nasdaq down 0.39%.
AI hardware took the hit Wednesday. Dell fell 11.27% and CoreWeave, which rents out AI computing power, fell 15.46% as investors questioned how much businesses will really spend on AI and how much tech companies are borrowing to build it. Nvidia slipped 3.16% and Meta 3.27%. The S&P 500 lost 1.11% and the Nasdaq 1.88%, while the Dow, lighter on tech, eased just 0.09%. Bank of America fell 4.76% after its $1.2 billion penalty dented the quarter.
The selling kept going in AI gear this morning: at 8:49 AM PT Dell is down 7.89% and CoreWeave down 3.28%, while the Dow is up 0.95% as money rotates out of tech rather than out of stocks. Even a building boom needs a buyer at the end.

Gold, silver and Brent cards are Wednesday, October 14 settlements (COMEX December gold and silver, ICE December Brent); the 10-Year is Wednesday's Treasury close. At 8:49 AM PT Thursday: gold $4,171.10 (up 1.54%), silver $57.23 (up 2.41%), 10-Year 5.10%, Brent $106.04.
Wholesale prices ran hot. The producer price index for final demand rose 1.3% in September and 6.3% from a year earlier, the fastest annual pace since December 2022. It lands a day after consumer prices rose 3.7%.
Bonds rallied anyway. The 10-Year fell to about 5.10% this morning from Wednesday's 5.19% close as investors moved into Treasurys during the tech selloff. Fed Governor Michelle Bowman said the Fed has time to wait after September's hike, and San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said another hike is likely needed but not urgent. CME FedWatch odds of a hold at the Oct. 27 to 28 meeting rose to 68% from 46%. A lower 10-Year usually pulls mortgage rates down with it, with a lag.
Metals swung both ways. Gold settled down 1.66% and silver down 6.13% Wednesday; this morning gold is up 1.54% and silver up 2.41%.
A correction: Market Briefs showed the 10-Year at 5.10%, gold up 1.16% and Brent down 1.38% for Wednesday. Treasury's close was 5.19%, and the settlements show gold down 1.66% and Brent up 0.35%.
