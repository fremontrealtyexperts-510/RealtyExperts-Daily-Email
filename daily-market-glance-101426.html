Buyers are reaching for adjustable rate mortgages again. ICE's October Mortgage Monitor says ARMs made up more than 11% of rate locks, their largest share in nearly four years, as fixed rates climbed past 7%. About 150,000 ARMs reach their first reset this year and roughly 180,000 next year, a small slice of the market, but some resets bite: ICE expects 7-year ARMs taken out in 2020 to jump a median $1,066 a month, or 36%.
Here is the trade on our five city median list price of $1,050,000 with 20% down. At Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed of 7.46%, principal and interest run about $5,850 a month. Its 7/6 ARM at 6.88% runs about $5,521, roughly $329 less, or about $27,700 over the seven fixed years. After that the rate follows the market.
MND's latest reading is Tuesday's: 7.46% for the 30-year and 7.14% for the 15-year. This morning's hotter inflation report pushed the 10-Year to about 5.23%, so Wednesday's reading could come in higher.
Locally, the five city ledger reads 677, down from 683. Fremont slipped to 234 from 241 and Newark to 89 from 90, Union City rose to 64 from 62, and Hayward (189) and Milpitas (101) held. Of the 601 homes on the market, 165 have been listed 60 days or longer, up from 161. The board wide table eased to 2,639 from 2,652, and brand new listings fell to 169 from 207.
What this means for you: an ARM can lower the payment if you expect to sell or refinance within seven years, but price the reset before you sign. Ask your lender for the caps and the worst case payment. With 165 homes sitting 60 days or longer, sellers are listening.
• Board wide: 2,639 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 163 coming soon and 169 brand new.

Gold, silver and Brent cards are Tuesday, October 13 settlements (COMEX December gold and silver, ICE December Brent); the 10-Year is Tuesday's Treasury close. At 9:31 AM PT Wednesday: gold $4,097.80 (down 1.90%), silver $55.80 (down 6.26%), 10-Year 5.23%, Brent $106.33.
Inflation picked back up. Consumer prices rose 0.6% in September and 3.7% from a year earlier, up from August's 3.4% and a tenth above the 3.6% forecast in Tuesday's report. Gasoline jumped 9.1% in the month and accounted for over half of the increase. Core prices, without food and energy, rose 0.3% in the month and 2.6% from a year earlier. The 10-Year climbed to about 5.23% this morning, and metals slid: gold was down 1.90% and silver down 6.26% at 9:31 AM PT.
The same numbers set retirees' raise. Social Security benefits rise 3.6% in January, the biggest increase since 2023's 8.7%, which lifts the average retired worker to $2,170 a month from $2,095, about $75 more. Market Briefs had estimated about 3.5% and $68. For Bay Area retirees, local prices rose 3.4% in the year to August, so the raise roughly keeps pace here.
Oil supply is climbing back. Saudi Arabia told OPEC it pumped 8.37 million barrels a day in September, up about a third from August's 6.24 million, which was its lowest since 1990. That is still about 2.5 million short of February's 10.9 million, before the conflict with Iran began Feb. 28, so Market Briefs' "close to pre-war levels" is a stretch. Two fixes to the supplied graphic: July was 8.2 million as Saudi Arabia reported it, not 8.1, and the kingdom's own May figure was never published, so our chart leaves May out.
A correction: Market Briefs showed gold down 0.02% and Brent up 1.17% for Tuesday; the settlements show gold down 0.32% and Brent down 0.90%. Its 10-Year of 5.21% was right, but that is down 0.03 from Friday, not flat.
Saudi output rebounded to 8.37 million barrels a day in September, still well below February's 10.9 million.
