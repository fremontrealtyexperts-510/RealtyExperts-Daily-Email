Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average held at 7.17% Friday, unchanged on the day, and the 15-year rose 0.03 to 6.83%. MND does not post on weekends, so these are Friday's rates. The 30-year is 0.10 lower than a week ago and 0.39 lower than a month ago (7.56%). At 7.17%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,298,000 median with 20% down would pay about $7,027 a month in principal and interest, $276 less than a month ago.
Friday's strong jobs report (see Economy) moved the outlook for the Federal Reserve. Before the report, CME's FedWatch tool put the chance of a quarter point increase at the December 9 meeting at 66.4%. By our math from Friday's settlement in fed funds futures, it is now about 77%. The Fed's target range is 3.75% to 4.00%. The Fed does not set mortgage rates; they track the 10-Year Treasury, which edged up to 4.86% Friday. But a December increase would make it harder for rates to keep falling.
Paychecks are the other half of a buyer's budget (chart). Average hourly pay rose 3.1% over the year to October, while consumer prices were up 3.7% in the year to September, the latest reading. Prices have risen faster than pay in each of the six latest months of data, April through September. Before this year, the last month that happened was April 2023.
The board wide table is 2,456, from 2,464 Friday, with 145 coming soon and 179 brand new. The five city ledger is 625, from 628: Milpitas (93) fell two, Fremont (212) and Newark (80) one each, Hayward (179) added one, and Union City (61) held.
What this means for you: if you are under contract or close to it, ask your lender what a lock through early December would cost, since the next Fed meeting lands December 9. Every quarter point on the median Fremont home is about $176 a month. Sellers, 163 homes in our five cities have been listed 60 days or longer.

Levels are Friday, November 6: the 10-Year is Treasury's close, gold and silver are COMEX December settlements, and Brent is the ICE January settlement. Markets are closed for the weekend.
Here is what was inside Friday's jobs report. Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, the third strongest month this year after March (214,000) and August (186,000), and unemployment eased to 4.1% from 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said (chart).
Professional and business services led with 70,000 new jobs, followed by health care and social assistance at 55,100 and transportation and warehousing at 24,800 (chart). Leisure and hospitality, which covers restaurants, bars and hotels, lost 13,000, and wholesale trade lost 9,100. Construction slipped 3,000. These are first estimates, and BLS revises each month twice.
Treasury has the 10-Year at 4.86% Friday, up 0.02 after the report. Gold settled at $4,074.90, down 0.94% on the day but up 4.5% for the week; silver at $51.94, down 0.83%; and Brent at $98.32, up 0.03%.
