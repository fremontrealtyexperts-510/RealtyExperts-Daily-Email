Foreclosures Hit Five-Year High: Carrying Costs Are The New Squeeze
Market Briefs led with the Chipotle CEO telling customers to "just ask for more" when their bowls feel small. That works in fast-casual, it does not work in housing. Bay Area sellers cannot ask the market for more if the comps do not support it. And the buyer pool has its own version of the same problem, locked-in low-rate owners who cannot ask their way out of insurance and tax bills that have outrun their original budgets.
Q1 foreclosures hit 119,000 nationally, the highest count since early 2020. The kicker: most of those owners hold sub-3% mortgages. The pinch is not the rate, it is everything around it. Average home insurance jumped 12% to $2,948 in 2025. Property tax assessments on appreciated values stack on top. Owners locked in at 3% are getting broken by the carrying costs.
The Duke of Westminster's Grosvenor Group is cashing out about $954 million in U.S. real estate, mostly in the Bay Area, and shifting to fund local developers instead. Foreign owners pulling back from American commercial property is a multi-quarter trend now, this is the largest single move in it. The Bay Area weighting is the part to flag.
Rates eased a touch. The 30-year sits at 6.40% (-0.04%), the 15-year at 5.98% (-0.02%). Buyers shopping right now have already accepted the rate environment. They are qualified, committed, and not waiting for a Fed cut.

US 10-Year and Gold as of 4:00 PM ET May 6th; WTI Crude reflects today's intraday move via investing.com.
The 10-Year barely moved at 4.35% (-0.02%) and gold notched up to $4,704 (+0.22%). WTI was essentially flat at $95.00. The bigger move is in the supply chain.
Ford (F) guided this morning that 2026 commodity costs will exceed $2 billion, double its prior estimate. Aluminum is up 19% since the U.S.-Israel strikes on Iran took roughly 3% of global supply offline. Molson Coors (TAP) flagged a $30 million aluminum hit on Q1 alone. The strikes are now a balance-sheet event, not just a geopolitics story.
The U.S. is filling the world's gas tank. Oil-product exports hit a record 8.2 million barrels per day last week, with diesel leading at 1.9 million. The catch: U.S. diesel stockpiles are now at their lowest level since 2005. We're exporting reserves that take quarters to refill.
European jet fuel doubled year-over-year to $187 a barrel. Lufthansa has already cut 20,000 flights through October. U.S. exports to Europe jumped 400% February to April to backstop the gap.
Taiwan's $286B Bureau of Labor Funds is cutting U.S. dollar and equity exposure. When a public pension that size tilts off the dollar, it draws copycats.
