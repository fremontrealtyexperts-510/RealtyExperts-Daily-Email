REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.30% +0.12  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.92% +0.09

Rates jumped on inflation. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average rose to 7.30% Tuesday, up 0.12, and the 15-year to 6.92%, up 0.09, after October's consumer prices rose faster than forecast. Wednesday is Veterans Day and the bond market is closed, so no new reading is posted yet. At 7.30%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,299,444 median with 20% down would pay about $7,127 a month in principal and interest, $85 more than at Monday's 7.18%.

Owners are tapping their equity. Home equity line balances rose $23 billion in the third quarter to $481 billion, the largest increase since 2005, the New York Fed reported Tuesday, while mortgage balances rose $257 billion to $13.4 trillion.

What it means for you. A 0.12 jump adds about $85 a month on the median Fremont home, so if you are ready to lock, talk with your lender about locking now or floating. On Veterans Day, a note for those who served: Mortgage News Daily's 30-year VA average is 6.90%, 0.40 below the conventional rate. STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,520.93 -0.38%  |  Dow: 52,231.89 -0.30%  |  Nasdaq: 25,065.87 -0.74%

Chip stocks are roaring back. As of 10:59 AM PT the Philadelphia semiconductor index is up 7.3%, the Nasdaq 2.22%, the S&P 500 1.63% and the Dow 0.50%, after TSMC reported record October sales and CoreWeave, which rents out AI computing power, raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion. CoreWeave is up 25.8% and Fremont's Lam Research 8.2%. The numbers above are Tuesday's closes, which slipped after the inflation report.

Anthropic set its IPO terms. The San Francisco AI company plans to sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, or $57.8 billion to $64.4 billion, to trade on Nasdaq as ANT, according to its SEC filing Tuesday. Even the low end would make it the second largest IPO ever, behind SpaceX's $75.0 billion in June. Up to 20% of the shares are expected to go to individual investors, through brokers including Schwab, Fidelity and Robinhood. A deal that size creates a lot of paper wealth within a BART ride of Fremont, though insiders are locked up for months after the listing.

TSMC had its biggest month ever. October revenue was NT$586.89 billion, up 59.7% from a year earlier and 14.7% from September. TSMC builds the chips that Bay Area companies such as Nvidia and Apple design, so its sales are a read on AI demand here.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 4.98% +0.10  |  Gold: $4,030.20 -2.05%  |  Silver: $51.23 -2.79%  |  Brent Crude: $91.49 -1.17%

Inflation ran hotter than forecast. Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% that economists polled by Dow Jones expected, and core prices, which leave out food and energy, rose 0.3% against a forecast of 0.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said Tuesday. Gasoline rose 1.0% and groceries 0.4%. There is no annual rate this month because of the 2025 government shutdown.

The Fed is leaning toward a hike. Treasury has the 10-Year closing Tuesday at 4.98%, up 0.10, and Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a quarter point rate increase would be appropriate in December. Household debt rose $334 billion in the third quarter to $19.1 trillion, the New York Fed said, with credit card balances up $22 billion to $1.29 trillion.

Metals and oil slipped. Gold settled Tuesday at $4,030.20, down 2.05%, silver at $51.23, down 2.79%, and Brent at $91.49, down 1.17%; the numbers above are Tuesday's settlements and Treasury's close.
