Mortgage Rates Hold Near 6.67% As A Hot 4.2% CPI Rattles Markets; East Bay Board Firms To 2,884 Active On 198 New And 216 Closed
The 30-year fixed held at 6.67% and the 15-year at 6.20% — but the bigger force this week is inflation. May CPI came in hot at 4.2% (see Economy), pushing the 10-year Treasury to 4.59% and trimming the odds of a Fed cut at next week's meeting. Mortgage rates take their cue from that 10-year, so a reaccelerating CPI is exactly the wrong direction for the buyer waiting on cheaper money.
And yet buyers are leaning in anyway. Mortgage demand jumped almost 11% last week even as rates ticked up: refinancing rose 15% week-over-week (and 20% from a year ago), while purchase applications climbed 7%. The read-through is that some borrowers are making one last spring push before the summer market — and before inflation pushes rates any higher.
Locally, the East Bay board firmed to 2,884 active, up 29 from Wednesday. New listings held near flat at 198 (vs 201) while 216 sales closed — more came on than cleared, which nudged the board back up after midweek's dip. Fremont alone logged 45 of those closings.

For agents: rates held, but inflation just reaccelerated to 4.2% and the 10-year jumped to 4.59% — yet mortgage demand still spiked 11% last week, a sign buyers aren't waiting. The board firmed to 2,884 as new listings outpaced closings. Tell buyers the "wait for lower rates" bet is getting riskier with inflation heating back up; tell sellers demand is real (refis +20% year-over-year, purchases +7%) and well-priced homes are still moving.

Stocks fell broadly after a hotter-than-expected inflation read (see Economy) rattled the market: the S&P 500 dropped 1.62%, the DOW 1.87%, and the NASDAQ 1.98%. A reaccelerating CPI revived worries that the Fed will keep rates higher for longer, and the selling was wide rather than concentrated.
There were company stories cutting both ways. Oracle (ORCL -2.21%) actually beat on both the top and bottom lines and inked a $16 billion data-center deal in Michigan, but its plan to raise $20 billion in fresh capital spooked investors and the stock slipped after hours. And Amazon (AMZN -2.53%) opened its less-than-truckload freight service to any business — not just its own sellers — which sent traditional freight names tumbling: Old Dominion (ODFL -5.14%), Saia (SAIA -3.25%), and XPO (XPO -4.47%). All eyes now turn to SpaceX, which goes public Friday.

US 10-Year, CPI, and Brent Crude as of June 10th (via Market Briefs).
Prices heated back up. The May Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 4.2% over the past year — the fastest pace since 2023 — and climbed 0.5% from April alone. Energy did most of the damage, jumping 23.5% year over year; new-vehicle prices dipped while airfares soared. Hotter inflation matters directly for housing: it pushed the 10-year Treasury up to 4.59% and trimmed the odds of a near-term rate cut, with the Fed set to meet next week (and widely expected to hold).
The pain is showing up at the grocery store, too. Ground beef averaged a record ~$7.00 a pound in May, up roughly 13% from a year ago, after a flesh-eating screwworm parasite turned up in Texas cattle for the first time in decades — an outbreak that could cost the state $1.8 billion and push prices higher still. Brent crude firmed to $94.72.
CPI inflation reaccelerated to 4.2% in May — the fastest since 2023. Rising inflation tends to keep mortgage rates elevated, since the 10-year Treasury that rates track moves with it.

Bitcoin firmed to $62,350 (+1.47%), with Ether ($1,627, +1.68%) along for the ride and XRP slipping to $1.12 (-1.33%). But the bigger crypto story is money rotating out: Bitcoin now sits about 50% below its October 2025 record as cash rushes toward Friday's blockbuster SpaceX IPO, which reportedly drew $250 billion in interest at a $1.75 trillion valuation. With a wave of large IPOs lining up, some analysts expect more money to keep leaking out of crypto and into new public listings.
In a sign of how mainstream the space is going, the Solana Foundation just became the title sponsor of the 2026 World Series of Poker — players can buy in with SOL or stablecoins at zero fees via MoonPay. For Bay Area buyers and sellers the connection is indirect, but big swings in crypto and IPO money still shape the risk appetite of a slice of the move-up market.
