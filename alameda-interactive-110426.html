REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.25% -0.05  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.86% -0.05

Thank you for voting. California's Prop. 37, a loan program for middle income buyers of newly built homes, is projected to pass. The Associated Press called it at 9:06 PM PT Tuesday, and the Secretary of State's count as of 8:08 AM PT today has it at 61.0% Yes to 39.0% No, with Alameda County at 67.5% Yes. Ballots are still being counted, and the state certifies the result by Dec. 11.

What it does. CalHFA, the state housing finance agency, may sell up to $25 billion in bonds to fund loans of up to 17% of the price for the first buyer of a home who puts at least 3% down and earns no more than double the area's typical income. The Legislative Analyst's Office expects no direct state or local cost, because buyers' payments repay the bonds. The program still has to be set up, so it is not available yet.

Where it could matter here. In our five cities, 32 of the 570 homes on the market were built in 2025 or later, 20 of them townhomes, with a median list price of $1,128,500 and 14 priced under a million. They have waited a median 57 days, against 40 for the market as a whole.

Rates eased. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.25% and the 15-year 6.86%, both down 0.05 from Tuesday. At 7.25%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,312,500 median with 20% down would pay about $7,163 a month in principal and interest.

What it means for you. If you are shopping new construction, ask builders now how they plan to work with Prop. 37 once it opens; it is a loan that gets repaid, not a grant. If you are selling a resale home, you compete with those builders, and 177 homes in our five cities have been sitting 60 days or longer, so price to this market, not last spring's.

The five city ledger carries 642 live listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, 570 on the market and 72 coming soon, down from 648.

Fremont holds 218 live listings: 164 active, 34 coming soon, 19 brand new and 1 back on the market, with a median on market list price of $1,312,500 at $823 a square foot and a typical listing out 37 days. Across all five cities the median is $1,049,975 at $666 a square foot, 276 homes are priced under a million, and 177 have been sitting 60 days or longer.

Gas set an Election Day record. EIA's survey put the U.S. average for regular at $4.15 a gallon in Election Day week, the highest for any Election Day since its records began in 1990, and San Francisco's $5.70 is its own Election Day high. Both are down from the week before, and after inflation 2012's $3.49 would be about $5.11 today, so this is a record in dollars, not in buying power.

Oil slid, metals jumped. Brent is down 9.03% this morning near $94.24 after settling Tuesday at $103.60, extending a slide that began Monday on hopes for U.S. and Iran talks. Gold is up 3.39% near $4,073.90 and silver up 5.06% near $52.27. The numbers above are Tuesday's settlements and Treasury's close.
