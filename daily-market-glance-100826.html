Mortgage rates posted their biggest one day drop in three months Thursday, and Zillow shows how little a six figure income rents in the Bay Area.

Mortgage rates finally caught a real break. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed fell to 7.50% Thursday from Wednesday's 7.59%, its biggest one day drop in three months, and the 15-year eased to 7.17%. The 10-Year Treasury closed at 5.22%, down from 5.28%.
MND credits investors who decided bond yields were finally high enough to buy, with some help from midday Middle East headlines. It is not calling a trend: next week's inflation reports, due Wednesday and Thursday, are the next real test.
What does $100,000 rent? Zillow says a household earning that can spend about $2,500 a month under the 30% rule. In Memphis that rents a median 2,119 square foot, four bedroom house. In the San Francisco metro, which includes Fremont, Hayward, Newark and Union City, it rents a 729 square foot one bedroom, and in San Jose's, home to Milpitas, 650 square feet. Only 5% of what that budget rents in either metro is a house.
Locally, the five city ledger reads 682, down from 685. Milpitas slipped to 101 from 108 and Fremont to 240 from 244, while Hayward rose to 190 from 183. The board wide table rose to 2,656 from 2,643.
What this means for you: at 7.50%, the five city median list price of $1,088,000 with 20% down runs about $6,086 a month in principal and interest. That is $54 less than at Wednesday's rate, still $359 more than on Sept. 1, when the 30-year was 6.89%, and more than twice the $2,500 a six figure renter can budget. Buyers who are close to a lock should talk to their lender on a day like this. Sellers should note the 149 homes that have sat 60 days or longer and price for today's payment.
• Board wide: 2,656 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 151 coming soon and 193 brand new.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Live ledger: 682 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City. 611 are on the market and 71 are coming soon.
• Fremont: 240 live, 197 active, 26 coming soon and 17 brand new. The median on market list price is $1,286,500 at $797 a square foot, and the typical active listing has been out 31 days.
• The rest of the ledger: Hayward 190, Milpitas 101, Newark 89, Union City 62.
• Across all five cities the median is $1,088,000 at $676 a square foot. 285 homes are priced under a million, and 149 have been sitting 60 days or longer.
On a $100,000 income, the rent budget tops out near $2,500 a month. In Memphis that is a four bedroom house. In the San Francisco and San Jose metros, it is a one bedroom of 650 to 729 square feet.
Source: Zillow, "What $100K rents," Oct. 7, 2026, from Zillow rental listings priced $2,333 to $2,500 a month in the 50 largest metros.

AI stocks stumbled Thursday. The Nasdaq fell 1.25%, its biggest one day loss since mid August, after CNBC confirmed that OpenAI told investors it reached $50 billion in annualized revenue at the end of September, short of the $68 billion figure widely reported last month. CoreWeave fell more than 7%, Oracle, Intel and Super Micro Computer about 5%, and Nvidia almost 3%.
The S&P 500 slipped 0.47% for a second straight loss, while the Dow edged up 0.10%. Delta's earnings and October consumer sentiment are due Friday morning. One rough day is not a trend, but the AI trade has carried this market, so it is worth watching.

The 10-Year, gold and silver are Thursday, October 8: the Treasury daily yield curve close and the COMEX December settlements. Brent is Wednesday's ICE December settlement; Thursday it settled at $104.28, up 4.07%. Market Briefs printed Wednesday's gold at $4,130, down 0.25% (the settle was $4,140.70, down 1.11%), and Brent at $101.22, up 1.02% (settle $100.20, down 0.38%), and left silver out.
Used car prices are cooling. Cox Automotive's Manheim index of wholesale used vehicle prices fell 1.1% in September to 205.9, down 4.4% from March's peak and 0.6% below a year ago, the first year over year drop of 2026. Cox pointed to record diesel prices, rapidly climbing interest rates and the Middle East conflict, and cut its outlook: it now expects the index to end 2026 up only about 0.2%, down from the 2% it forecast in July. Auctions are where dealers restock, so softer wholesale prices tend to reach the lot later.
The Fed is not done. Minutes of the September meeting, released Wednesday, say most officials thought another increase in the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate by year end, after September's quarter point hike to a 3.75% to 4% range. Oil did not help the inflation fight Thursday: Brent settled at $104.28, up about 4%.
Wholesale used vehicle prices fell 1.1% in September and sit 4.4% below March's peak. Cox Automotive now expects the index to end 2026 up only about 0.2%.
Source: Cox Automotive, Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index September 2026 data file and Q3 2026 press release, Oct. 7, 2026. Adjusted for mix, mileage and season.
