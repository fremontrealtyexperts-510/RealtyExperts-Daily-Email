Mortgage Rates Tick Up To 6.69% As Trump Rings The Opening Bell; Wall Street Landlords Start Selling Faster Than They Buy; The East Bay Board Eases To 2,725 Active On 114 New And 223 Closed
The 30-year fixed ticked up to 6.69% and the 15-year to 6.16%. Rates in this range keep the lock-in effect firmly in place, so owners stay put rather than trade a low rate for a higher one, and resale supply stays tight.
A shift worth watching for buyers: Wall Street's biggest home landlords are now selling faster than they are buying, for the first time in years. Eight large operators sold a net 3,011 more homes in the second quarter, a 408% jump from a year ago, as a proposed federal ban on institutional buying rattles the model. If it holds, that means more homes and less competition from deep-pocketed investors down the road.
The equity picture is a tale of two markets. Most U.S. owners are sitting on plenty of equity, but a few Sunbelt cities are slipping underwater. In Cape Coral to Fort Myers, 11.1% of mortgages are now worth more than the home after prices fell 18.9%. The Bay Area, where supply stays tight and values hold, sits on the other side of that ledger.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,725 active (down 23 from yesterday), with 114 new listings and 223 closings. Oakland again led the board with 26 new on 715 active; Fremont added 12 new on 329 active.

Stocks opened the week higher, and they had an unusual guest at the podium. President Trump became the first sitting president to ring the opening bell, doing it from the Oval Office. The S&P 500 rose 0.72% to 7,537, the NASDAQ climbed 1.12% to 26,121, and the DOW added 0.29% to 53,055. He also plugged Dell (DELL +4.40%) and declared himself "a big crypto guy" (more on that below).
The unicorn stampede has slowed to a trot. The AI boom has minted 90 new unicorns (startups worth over $1 billion) so far this year, a far cry from the 622 created back in 2021 (see today's chart). Money is pickier now, and a billion-dollar valuation is harder to come by.
Deals and cuts filled the tape too. Lockheed Martin (LMT -1.45%) is buying submarine-detection firm Ultra Maritime for about $3.4 billion as navies race to guard the deep. Nvidia (NVDA +0.37%) pushed its Kyber supercomputer rack system out to 2028 on manufacturing snags. And Microsoft (MSFT -0.96%) is cutting roughly 4,800 jobs and spinning off four game studios, with its shares down more than 18% on the year.
New unicorns have cooled sharply since the 2021 boom. The AI wave has produced 90 so far in 2026, a fraction of the 622 minted in 2021.

Metals eased off recent highs. Gold slipped 0.25% to $4,157 and silver gave back 1.03% to $61.36, while the 10-year Treasury yield held at 4.49% and Brent crude firmed 0.90% to $72.64.
The big personal-finance story is student loans. Servicers are telling more than 6.9 million borrowers that the SAVE plan is ending, and they have 90 days to choose a new one. Do nothing and you get moved to the Standard Plan, usually the priciest option. Americans hold about $1.7 trillion in federal student debt across 43 million borrowers, so this reshuffle touches a lot of household budgets and, by extension, how much buyers can carry toward a home.
