REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.61% +0.04  |  15-Year Fixed: 7.23% +0.04

Another high for this run. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed rose to 7.61% Monday and the 15-year to 7.23%, a hair above last Wednesday's 7.60%. The 10-Year Treasury closed at 5.31%, its highest close since May 2002, and is a little lower this morning, near 5.26%.

Why bonds stay on edge. Service businesses say the prices they pay are rising at the fastest pace since July 2022, and Bay Area consumer prices for services are now rising faster than the nation's. The chart in the Economy section has the detail.

What it means for you. At 7.61%, the five city median list price of $1,079,500 with 20% down runs about $6,104 a month in principal and interest, $422 more than on Sept. 1. If you are selling, price for that payment. If you are buying, the 141 homes that have sat 60 days or longer are the place to ask for seller credits toward a rate buydown.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,773.95 +0.66%  |  Dow: 51,267.90 +0.18%  |  Nasdaq: 27,477.31 +1.05%

The Nasdaq set a record. It closed Monday up 1.05% at 27,477.31, past its Sept. 22 high, as the AI trade led again: SpaceX jumped more than 7%, Meta rose almost 2%, and Nvidia and Tesla gained 2% each.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 5.31% +0.03  |  Gold: $4,156.80 -0.13%  |  Silver: $61.30 +1.46%  |  Brent Crude: $100.32 -1.89%

Service prices are running hot. The Institute for Supply Management's services survey put its prices paid index at 74 in September, up from 72.6 and the highest since July 2022. Fuel costs came up twice as often as any other issue respondents raised.

Hotter here. ISM does not allow charts built from its index, so ours uses government data: service businesses raised prices 5.4% over the year to August, and Bay Area consumer prices for services rose 3.8%, against 3.1% nationally. A year ago the Bay Area ran a full point below the nation.
