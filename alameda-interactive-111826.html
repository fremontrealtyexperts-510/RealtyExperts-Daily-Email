30-Year Fixed: 7.46% -0.02  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.98% -0.04

Builders are the gloomiest since 2012. Builder confidence fell four points to 25 in November, the lowest since April 2012, according to the NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index. 46% of builders cut prices, the most since the pandemic, by an average of 6%, and 68% offered incentives. In the West, which includes California, the three month reading fell to 23, the weakest of the four regions.

Here, new homes lost their edge. In our five cities, 10 of 42 homes built in 2025 or 2026 are in contract (24%), the same rate as resale; on October 20 it was 19 of 52 (37%). Across the Bay East board, 21 of 136 new homes are in contract (15%), against 25% for resale. Census figures out this morning show single family permits fell 8.7% in October to a 772,000 annual pace, which NAHB calls the slowest since January 2023.

What it means for you. If you are buying, a builder with finished homes to move is negotiating, so ask for a rate buydown or closing costs before a price cut. If you are selling a resale home, nearby new construction is competing with incentives, so price to today's comps. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.46% this morning, with the 15-year at 6.98%.

A record cycle for political ads. Political advertisers spent a record $12.06 billion in the 2025 to 2026 cycle, according to AdImpact, the first midterm cycle to outspend a presidential one ($11.20 billion in 2024). California led every state at $1.32 billion, the first state ever past $1 billion in a single cycle, and broadcast TV took the biggest share, $5.85 billion.

Shoppers slowed down. Retail sales rose 0.2% in October after 0.8% in September, the Census Bureau reported, a gain too small to rule out no change at all.
