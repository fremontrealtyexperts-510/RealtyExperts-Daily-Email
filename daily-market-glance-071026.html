Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.65% As The Median Home Price Sets A Record $440,600; Sales Slip 2.4% On Tight Supply; The East Bay Board Jumps To 2,811 Active On 245 New And 181 Closed
The 30-year fixed slipped to 6.65% and the 15-year to 6.20%, a touch lower on the week. Even so, rates in this range keep the lock-in effect firmly in place, so most owners stay put and resale supply stays tight.
The headline from NAR this week: home prices set another record. The national median existing-home price hit an all-time high of $440,600 in June, up 1.8% from a year ago and the 36th straight month of annual gains (see today's chart). At the same time, sales slipped 2.4% from May, as high mortgage rates and thin supply keep squeezing affordability. Fewer sales at higher prices is a supply story, not a demand boom.
Locally, the East Bay board jumped to 2,811 active, up from 2,777 yesterday, on a busy 245 new listings and 181 closings. Oakland led the board by a wide margin with 58 new on 726 active, followed by Danville at 29 and Fremont and Livermore at 26 each.

Stocks powered higher across the board, led by tech. The NASDAQ jumped 1.30% to 26,206, the S&P 500 added 0.81% to 7,543, and the DOW rose 0.27% to 52,487, as the AI trade found another gear.
Meta (META +4.70%) lit the fuse. For the first time it is charging developers to use its AI, but at roughly a quarter of what rivals charge, a deliberately aggressive move to grab market share from higher-priced competitors. It also plans to roll out a custom chip built with Broadcom (AVGO +3.20%) later this year.
Micron (MU +4.52%) added to the momentum, spiking more than 9% at one point after unveiling plans to spend $250 billion on U.S. plants through 2035. The AI boom is eating memory chips faster than factories can make them, and more of that production is now heading onshore.

Change may be coming to the Fed. New Chair Kevin Warsh has assembled a team and five task forces to rethink how the central bank operates, from its inflation target to how it communicates. If those panels recommend changes, shifts in mortgage and savings rates could arrive sooner than expected.
The car itself may change too. Regulators are weighing whether to let driverless vehicles drop the steering wheel entirely. Tesla (TSLA +3.20%) is already building its Cybercab, a two-seater with no wheel or pedals, a redesign that could eventually lower manufacturing costs.
In the markets, the 10-year Treasury held steady at 4.55%, while gold climbed 1.28% to $4,134 and silver firmed to about $60.30. Brent crude slipped 2.56% to $76.02, easing back even as Iran rushed 11 million barrels onto tankers ahead of a possible U.S. blockade.

Crypto edged higher, with Bitcoin up 1.65% to $63,263, Ether up 0.38% to $1,748, and XRP up 0.70% to $1.10.
The theme of the day was tokenizing the real world. One startup wants to let anyone own a barrel of oil through a digital token, with its WTIC token holding about $80,000 in on-chain value and a goal of round-the-clock oil trading.
Not everyone is convinced. Singapore's state investment giant is swearing off crypto after losing $275 million in the FTX collapse, and now plans to grow its AI holdings to 15% of its portfolio by 2031. It is part of a broader pivot from tokens to AI as some coins sit near two-year lows.
