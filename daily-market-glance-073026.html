The daily 30-year fixed eased to 6.77% and the 15-year to 6.31%, each down a basis point. That is the calm before the read through: yesterday's hawkish hold pushed September hike odds to about 70%, and the 30-year tends to move well ahead of the Fed itself.
International buyers are stepping back. Foreign purchases of U.S. homes dropped $11 billion from a year ago, and Chinese buyers alone spent close to half what they spent last year. High prices, tight inventory, and political tension are all cooling overseas demand. For local buyers that means less competition in exactly the markets that used to draw the most foreign money, California among them.
Housing is also losing some of its shine as a wealth engine. Boomers built net worth on appreciation, but over the past decade stocks have outpaced home appreciation once you account for taxes, insurance, and repairs. Inflation pushed ownership costs up while the market ran, and more Americans are renting and investing the difference rather than buying.
Why a REALTOR® cares: that comparison leaves out leverage, the mortgage interest deduction, and the fact that nobody lives inside an index fund. It is a real objection you will hear this year, and the answer is a side by side on the specific property, not a defense of housing in general.

The DOW fell more than 1,100 points, its worst single day since April 2025. The Fed left rates alone, but three officials voted for a hike and the market read the whole meeting as hawkish. Yields rose and stocks slid across the board, with the S&P 500 off 1.52% to 7,316, the DOW down 2.19% to 51,594, and the NASDAQ down 1.74% to 24,443.
M&M earnings, and not the kind you eat. Meta and Microsoft both reported after the bell. Microsoft beat, spending more on data centers and driving cloud revenue higher. Meta went the other way: shares fell more than 10% at one point in after hours trading once the company said its AI investments are draining free cash flow. That caps a rough year for the stock, down 11.28% and $74.48 a share since January, with a February high of $740 and an April low of $527 along the way (chart below). Apple reports after the bell today.
The chip trade is unwinding fast. The sector has shed more than $1 trillion since last Friday, with Nvidia alone giving up $238 billion in market value. It was the biggest weekly selloff in chip stocks since 2020, and experts read it as a confidence problem rather than a numbers problem, which usually means more volatility ahead.

Gold and silver are live spot quotes taken midday July 30 (change versus the same time a day earlier); the 10-Year, Brent, stocks, and crypto are as of the July 29 close. Metals can move fast intraday, so treat these as a same day snapshot.
Warsh waits. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh and the committee left rates unchanged in a 9 to 3 vote, the fifth straight meeting with no move, holding the target range at 3.50% to 3.75%. Three officials wanted a hike outright. Traders now put the odds of a September increase near 70%, and that flows straight through to mortgage rates, auto loans, and home equity lines.
The chart below puts the decision in context: near zero through the pandemic years, a fast climb to 5.33% in 2023, and a slow glide down to the 3.63% effective rate we sit at today. The last five meetings are the flat stretch on the right side.
Oil is the wild card now. Brent jumped 7.90% to $90.74 after President Trump threatened to strike Iran, which followed overnight Iranian strikes on U.S. assets. Iran controls the Strait of Hormuz, where roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes. Higher pump prices and pricier flights are the likely follow through.
Refineries are running hot and it still is not enough. U.S. plants are processing about 17 million barrels of crude a day, the most since 2019, but global fuel supply was already tight from the war in Ukraine and there is simply not enough refining capacity to catch up.
Metals liked the hold. Gold crested $4,100 and trades at $4,131 this morning, up 0.60%, after rising nearly 2% on the Fed news itself. Silver added 1.30% to $58.81 and still cannot reclaim $60, a level it has not opened above since July 8.
Why a REALTOR® cares: a 70% chance of a September hike is the number to watch, not yesterday's hold. If that bet firms up, the 30-year moves before the Fed ever does, and today's rate becomes the good one your buyers wish they had taken.
The Fed held again, the fifth straight meeting with no change. The effective rate sits at 3.63%, down from the 5.33% peak in 2023 but still far above the near zero pandemic years.
