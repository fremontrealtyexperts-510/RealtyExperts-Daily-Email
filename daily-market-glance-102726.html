Today's housing number is prices. The S&P Cotality Case-Shiller index for August, out this morning, has national home prices up 2.1% from a year earlier, up from 1.9% in July. The San Francisco metro index, which takes in Alameda County, is up 3.4% from a year earlier, though before seasonal adjustment it slipped 0.7% from July, the usual late summer cooling.
Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average held at 7.33% Monday and the 15-year is 6.99%, with Tuesday's reading due later today and the Fed's decision on Wednesday (more in Economy). Locally, inventory thinned: the board wide table fell to 2,578 from 2,623, with 144 coming soon and 185 brand new. Levels are Monday, October 26 at the closing bell. Live at 8:07 AM PT Tuesday: S&P 500 up 0.49%, Dow down 0.03%, Nasdaq up 0.97%.
Stocks are mixed a day before the Fed: as of 8:07 AM PT the S&P 500 is up 0.49%, the Dow down 0.03% and the Nasdaq up 0.97%, after Monday's broad gain. Wednesday is a double feature: the Fed decides at 11 AM our time, and Microsoft reports its quarter the same day.
Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, roughly a quarter of its workforce, in the U.S., U.K., Canada and India at level 70 and below, on terms comparable to its standard severance. Sales, some marketing and some engineering teams are left out, and a company spokesperson said artificial intelligence is not driving the program. For our clients, a buyout is a lump sum, not income, and lenders qualify buyers on steady income, so anyone taking one who plans to buy should talk to a lender first.
