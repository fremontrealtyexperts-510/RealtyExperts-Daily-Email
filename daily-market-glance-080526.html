Rates sat still. The daily 30-year fixed is 6.75% and the 15-year is 6.29%, both unchanged on the day, with mortgage backed securities flat. After the 10-Year's move down, flat is a little disappointing, but it beats another climb.
NAR published quarterly metro prices, and California owns the list. Eight of the ten most expensive metros in the country are in California. See the chart below.
• San Jose is number one in America at $2,050,000, and it is the only metro in the top ten that went DOWN, off 4.2% year over year.
• San Francisco is number two at $1,500,000 and went the other way, up 5.2%.
• Los Angeles was exactly flat at $879,900.
• Nationally the median existing single family home hit $434,900, up 1.5%, with prices rising in 80% of metros.
• The West was the only region in the country to fall, off 0.8%, while the Northeast led at up 3.8%.
Read that top of the list again, because we sit directly between those two numbers. San Jose and San Francisco are the two most expensive metros in the United States and they just moved in opposite directions by nearly ten points of spread. Our five cities are the affordable middle of that sandwich, which is exactly the position that holds up best when the very top of a market softens.
The split is showing up in what is for sale, too. Zillow says starter home listings rose 4.5% year over year in June while luxury listings fell 5.2%. Wealthy buyers have stock gains and are still transacting. Everyone else is squeezed by inflation and a shaky job market. That is the same story as San Jose falling while San Francisco rises, just told through inventory instead of price.
Why a REALTOR® cares: when the priciest metro in the country drops 4.2% and its neighbor gains 5.2%, averages stop being useful. Your buyer does not purchase a region, they purchase a city and a street. This is the quarter to price off the actual comps in front of you rather than off any headline about the West being down.

All three indexes closed at record highs. The S&P 500 rose 1.79% to 7,736, the Dow added 1.71% to 54,085, and the NASDAQ led again with 2.59% to 26,584. That is the second straight session of green across the board, and it puts the Dow up roughly 12.5% for the year.
The reason was one sentence. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said an Iran deal could land within a day or two. Markets have heard almost deal before and nothing has stuck yet, so treat the size of the move as sentiment rather than settled fact.
SpaceX posted its first earnings as a public company and gave everyone a number to argue about. It spent $15.83 billion on AI in a single quarter, out of $18.37 billion of total capital spending. The chart below is the whole story: the Connectivity and Space segments barely moved over the past year, going from $1.13 billion to $1.37 billion and from $0.95 billion to $1.17 billion. AI went from $0.75 billion to $15.83 billion, a jump of about 2,000%. Revenue grew 92%, which is excellent, and the stock still rose 9.43% during the session and then fell after close. Wall Street's worry is not whether the spending is happening, it is whether it pays off before a share lockup expires and insiders get to sell.
AMD beat expectations and got punished anyway. It guided to about $13 billion this quarter, up from $11 billion, but analysts wanted $14 billion, so the shares fell more than 10% after hours despite gaining 7.00% in the regular session. Two AI names, two strong reports, two after hours selloffs. The market is no longer paying up for AI on faith alone.

The 10-Year is the August 4 Treasury close from the daily yield curve. Gold is a 9:00 AM Eastern quote on August 5 against the same hour a day earlier, and silver is a 5:30 AM Eastern quote on August 5 on the same basis. Stocks are as of the August 4 close and crypto is a live quote from this morning. Metals move fast intraday, so treat these as a same day snapshot.
The 10-Year came down again. Treasury's daily curve put the 10-year yield at 4.63% at Monday's close, down 7 basis points from 4.70%. That is the second meaningful step down in a week and it is the direction that matters for anyone shopping a mortgage. The newsletter printed 4.62% and called the change flat at 0.00%; Treasury's own curve says 4.63% and down 7 basis points, and we go with Treasury.
Metals had a big day, and this is where today's newsletter went furthest off. Gold jumped 2.83% to $4,184 an ounce. The newsletter reported gold at $4,149 and DOWN 0.09%, which is wrong in both level and direction on a day gold gained more than $115. Silver ran even harder, up 4.82% to $61.46, its third straight gain, and the newsletter left silver out entirely. When money moves into both metals this hard on the same session, it usually says something about how much conviction there really is behind a record stock close.
A note on oil, because we would rather tell you than guess. Brent came back four materially different ways this morning, from the high $70s in the newsletter to $88.90 in the federal daily series for August 3, and the benchmarks have swung more than $20 a barrel in two weeks on Iran headlines. We could not reconcile them to one defensible number, so we left the oil card off today's report rather than print a figure we cannot stand behind. The direction is not in doubt: crude is violently volatile right now and it is trading on whether that Iran deal is real.
The trade gap shrank. June's deficit came in at $73.3 billion, down 5.6%, with imports off 1.8% and exports off 0.9%. A smaller deficit sounds like good news, and it can be, but a deficit that shrinks because Americans are buying less tends to show up later as softer corporate orders.
Why a REALTOR® cares: a 10-Year at 4.63% and falling is the single best leading indicator you have for where mortgage rates go next. Rates have not followed yet. They rarely follow on the same day. But two down moves in the 10-Year inside a week is the setup you want if you have a buyer waiting on the sidelines for relief.

The professionals now own the tape. Institutions account for 72% of Wintermute's spot trading volume, up from 59%. That matters more than the day's prices. Professional money trades through options and funds rather than chasing headlines, so as their share climbs, crypto should set prices with less retail hype behind it and, over time, swing less violently. That is a slow structural change, not a today change.
Nigeria is letting small businesses sell tokenized shares over the counter. More than 60% of Nigerians are under 30, and the point is to give young investors and small companies a cheaper route into markets. Worth watching as a template for what tokenized ownership looks like when a country builds it on purpose rather than around the rules.
