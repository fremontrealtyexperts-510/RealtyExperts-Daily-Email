Mortgage Rates Tick Up To 6.68% As Buyers And Sellers Stay Locked In A Standoff; Gen Z Grabs 20% Of Purchase Locks; The East Bay Board Climbs To 2,777 Active On 197 New And 198 Closed
The 30-year fixed ticked up to 6.68% and the 15-year to 6.22%. Rates in this range keep the lock-in effect firmly in place, and nearly 80% of homeowners still carry a mortgage below today's levels, so most stay put and resale supply stays tight.
That standoff is the story of this market. Goldman Sachs now expects home prices to rise just 0.8% by the end of 2026, as would-be buyers wait for a break that is barely coming. Prices are holding, not falling, which keeps both sides parked.
There is a fresh face at the table, though. Gen Z buyers accounted for about 20% of purchase rate locks last week, leaning on FHA loans and family down-payment help to get in. The typical first-time buyer keeps getting older, so a younger wave stepping up is worth watching.
Locally, the East Bay board climbed to 2,777 active, up from 2,724 yesterday, on 197 new listings and 198 closings. Oakland again led with 40 new on 715 active, followed by Danville at 30 new and Livermore at 23; Fremont added 19 new on 334 active.

Stocks finished mixed as investors weighed fresh uncertainty over the Middle East. The S&P 500 eased 0.28% to 7,482 and the DOW fell 1.09% to 52,348, while the NASDAQ bucked the trend, up 0.20% to 25,870.
Levi's (LEVI -1.18%) gave the tape a bright spot. The denim maker topped Wall Street's expectations, selling more product even after raising prices, with its Beyond Yoga athleisure line doing much of the lifting. It also raised its dividend and its outlook, a sign management expects steady cash to keep flowing.
SpaceX (SPCX -0.81%) went the other way. After a red-hot IPO debut a few weeks ago that briefly made Elon Musk a trillionaire on paper, the stock has now slipped below its offering price. Some investors worry it is spending too heavily on AI, and a wave of newly unlocked early shares added to the selling pressure.

The Fed is split. Policymakers held rates steady last month but are now openly divided on what comes next, with the latest projections pointing to just one move this year. Some officials see inflation cooling enough to cut, while others worry that tariffs and the Iran flare-up will keep prices sticky.
Households are feeling the squeeze in one very visible place: the driveway. The typical payment on a new car just hit an all-time high of $770 a month, up 2.9% from a year ago, as high prices, high rates, and thin discounts all pile on. It is part of a longer climb. Outstanding auto loan debt has risen every year since 2016 and now sits at a record $1.66 trillion (see today's chart).
In the markets, the 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.57%, gold firmed to about $4,110, and silver pushed up to $60.36, still up roughly 65% over the past year. Brent crude added 0.82% to $78.66 as traders braced for more Middle East volatility.

Crypto slipped across the board. Bitcoin eased 0.63% to $61,845, Ether slipped 0.69% to $1,730, and XRP dipped 0.62% to $1.08.
The mood shift shows up in where the smart money is going. Paradigm, one of crypto's best-known venture firms, just raised a $1.2 billion fund aimed at AI and robotics rather than tokens. AI startups now pull in about 70% of all global startup funding, a reminder of how quickly the spotlight has moved.
On the caution side, scammers are increasingly using crypto kiosks to drain bank accounts, taking roughly $56.8 million from about 1,200 victims last year and often targeting older people. A good reminder that anything promising fast, guaranteed returns deserves a second look.
