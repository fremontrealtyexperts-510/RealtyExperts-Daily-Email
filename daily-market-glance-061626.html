Mortgage Rates Slip To 6.56% While Builder Confidence Sinks To 35 — Its Longest Funk Since 2012; The East Bay Board Eases To 2,882 Active On 248 New And 216 Closed
The 30-year fixed slipped to 6.56% and the 15-year to 6.12%. Builders, though, are still glum: homebuilder confidence fell to 35 in June (see the chart in the Economy section) — anything under 50 means more builders feel bad than good, and the index has now sat below 40 for 14 straight months. With high rates and building costs keeping buyers cautious, about 35% of builders are cutting prices to move inventory.
Affordability is the throughline. A "starter home" now costs $1 million in 242 U.S. cities — triple the count in 2020 — and 26 states have at least one such city, up from 9. California alone has 105 of them. The cause is the same story we keep telling: not enough homes are getting built, which keeps that crucial first rung out of reach for many buyers.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,882 active (down 37 from yesterday), with 248 new listings against 216 closings. Oakland again led the board with 76 new on 757 active; Fremont added 23 new on 356 active.

For agents: the national mood is cautious — builders are gloomy and discounting, and the $1 million starter-home milestone shows just how stretched affordability has become. But locally the board is steady (2,882 active) with a healthy 248 new listings still coming in, so well-priced, well-presented homes that sell inside about four weeks continue to win. Coach sellers to price right and show well; remind buyers that softer rates and price-cutting builders mean they finally have room to negotiate.

Stocks pushed higher — the S&P 500 up 1.65%, the DOW 0.92%, and a standout 3.07% for the NASDAQ. SpaceX (SPCX +19.60%) jumped again Monday after the biggest IPO in history, pushing its value past $2 trillion. CEO Elon Musk says the company could reach $1 trillion in revenue by 2030, up from just over $18 billion last year — though it lost nearly $5 billion last year and analysts can't agree what it's worth.
Dealmaking is back. Fox (FOX -15.07%) is buying streamer Roku (ROKU -0.88%) for about $22 billion — the biggest entertainment deal since the Paramount–Warner Bros. tie-up earlier this year. Investors think Fox overpaid: its shares fell as much as 17% Monday morning. Separately, Salesforce is buying customer-service platform Fin for $3.6 billion.

A quiet day for rates and metals. The 10-year Treasury held at 4.44%, Brent crude eased 2.55% to $81.05, and gold ticked up to $4,363 (+0.26%). Silver gave back a little to about $70.39 (-0.83%) but remains one of 2026's best performers, still up roughly 100% year-to-date.
The headline for housing is sentiment, not prices. Homebuilder confidence — the NAHB Housing Market Index — slipped to 35 in June, its longest slump since 2012. Anything below 50 means more builders feel bad than good, and the gauge has now been stuck under 40 for 14 straight months. High mortgage rates and building costs are scaring off buyers, so about 35% of builders are cutting prices. (The chart below tracks the last nine months.)
On the trade front, President Trump said French wine could face a 100% tariff soon — a move that would roughly double prices on U.S. shelves — as leverage to get France to drop its 3% tax on big U.S. tech firms. Americans buy about $2 billion of French wine a year, near a fifth of France's global wine sales.

Crypto was flat-to-firm: Bitcoin at $66,522 (+0.35%), Ether barely moved at $1,795, and XRP at $1.24. The bigger news is access — Kraken just made "perpetual futures" (a crypto bet that never expires) available to U.S. traders for the first time, after its parent bought CFTC-licensed Bitnomial. Those contracts drew over $60 trillion in trades last year, almost all of it offshore.
Corporate buyers keep stacking coins now that the U.S.–Iran conflict is over: Strategy (MSTR +5.78%) added 1,587 Bitcoin (about $100 million) and Strive (ASST +10.03%) picked up another 73 (about $4.7 million).
