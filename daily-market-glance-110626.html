Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average finished Thursday at 7.17%, down 0.05, and the 15-year at 6.80%, also down 0.05; Friday's reading was not posted as of 9:33 AM PT. We printed 7.22% Thursday morning, and rates improved through the day as money left stocks for Treasury bonds. At 7.17%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,299,000 median with 20% down would pay about $7,033 a month in principal and interest, $312 less than at the 7.61% of a month ago.
The counterweight is Friday's strong jobs report (see Economy). Good news for the economy usually means higher rates, yet the 10-Year was nearly unchanged at 4.85% this morning, about where it closed Thursday.
The board wide table is 2,464, from 2,443 Thursday, with 147 coming soon and 150 brand new. The five city ledger is 628, from 634: Hayward (178) fell three, Milpitas (95) two, Fremont (213) and Newark (81) one each, and Union City (61) added one.
What this means for you: the 0.05 drop is worth about $35 a month on the median Fremont home, so if you are close to locking, ask your lender whether today's jobs news has moved their rate sheet. Buyers counting on company stock for a down payment saw Thursday how fast that number can change; talk with your lender about when to move it to cash. Sellers, 165 homes in our five cities have been listed 60 days or longer.

Levels are Thursday, November 5 at the closing bell. Live at 9:33 AM PT Friday: Dow 0.00%, S&P 500 up 0.74%, Nasdaq up 1.38%.
Thursday was the worst day for stocks since April 2025. The S&P 500 fell 4.36% to 7,353.73, the Nasdaq 5.52% to 24,315.00 and the Dow 3.44%, or 1,833 points, to 51,523.06, more than erasing Wednesday's post election rally. The Nasdaq now sits 11.9% below its October 6 record, a correction. The selling started in chips: the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 12.25%, its worst day since March 2020, on worries that the election results could slow the building of AI data centers.
It hit close to home (chart). Fremont's Lam Research fell 16.4%, and Milpitas neighbors KLA and Sandisk fell 13.3% and 22.3%; AMD dropped 17.3% and Nvidia 9.3%, both based in Santa Clara. Friday is calmer: as of 9:33 AM PT the Nasdaq is up 1.38%, the S&P 500 up 0.74% and the Dow flat, and Lam has won back 8.6%. One bad day does not make a trend, but it did make a lot of Bay Area stock grants feel smaller.

Levels are Thursday, November 5: the 10-Year is Treasury's close, gold and silver are COMEX December settlements, and Brent is the ICE January settlement. Live at 9:33 AM PT Friday: 10-Year 4.85%, gold $4,068.40 (-1.10%), silver $51.96 (-0.80%), Brent $97.78 (-0.52%).
The government's October jobs report beat forecasts. Employers added 165,000 jobs, more than double the monthly average of the past year (about 74,000), and unemployment slipped to 4.1% from 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said Friday. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% for the month and 3.1% over the year.
Layoff announcements cooled too (chart). Employers announced 43,994 job cuts in October, Challenger, Gray & Christmas reported Thursday, down 71% from 153,074 a year earlier and the fewest for an October since 2023. California still led all states with 10,032, down 29% from a year ago, and technology, at 171,501 so far this year, is the industry with the most cuts in 2026.
Treasury has the 10-Year closing Thursday at 4.84%, down 0.06, as money moved from stocks to bonds. Brent settled at $98.29, up 3.88% after Wednesday's 8.67% drop, gold at $4,113.50, up 0.99%, and silver at $52.38, up 0.18%.
