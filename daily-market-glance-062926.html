The 30-year fixed held at 6.53% and the 15-year at 6.12%. With the Fed's inflation gauge still hot, the gentle drift lower we have seen is likely to stay gentle, and the lock-in effect thinning resale supply is not loosening its grip.
Morgan Stanley put a number on the standoff. A homeowner who sold today and bought a similar home could see their monthly payment jump about 200%, roughly $1,300 to $1,400 more a month. That math is exactly why so many owners are staying put.
At the luxury end, sellers are getting creative. With fewer cash buyers around, some are accepting private tech-company stock instead of cash, including a $2.6 million Miami listing taking startup shares. Hard to value and harder to resell, but in a slow market a willing buyer is a willing buyer.
Locally, the East Bay board eased to 2,945 active (down 19 from Friday), with 230 new listings against 190 closings. Oakland again led the board with 42 new on 778 active; Fremont added 28 new on 355 active.

Inflation is still running warm. The Federal Reserve's preferred gauge rose 4.1%, the fastest pace since April 2023, and Richmond Fed President Tom Barkin warned it will be tough to cool prices without keeping rates high. Not the update a mortgage shopper was hoping for.
Aluminum went the other way (see the chart below). Prices just logged a fourth straight weekly drop, off 6.4% on the week, as a possible Middle East ceasefire looks set to restart regional supply. Cheaper aluminum could eventually mean cheaper cans, cars, and construction, though builders will believe it when they see it.
Metals were mixed elsewhere: gold eased 0.40% to $4,080 and silver to about $58.80, while Brent crude firmed 0.53% to $73.37 and the 10-year Treasury yield held at 4.38%.
