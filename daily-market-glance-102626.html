This week's housing news is about fees, not rates. An executive order signed Oct. 22 directs the Federal Housing Finance Agency, which oversees Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, to set up a pilot that cancels a borrower's mortgage insurance once the home's current market value shows enough equity. It also expands the agency's alternative to lender's title insurance on refinances, and tells USDA and VA to cut rates on the home loans they make directly. (Market Briefs called it canceling insurance on certain new loans; the order ties it to equity.)
Here is why the first piece matters at our prices. A buyer putting 10% down on our five city median of $1,079,000 borrows $971,100, a high balance conforming loan here, and reaches 20% equity once the home is worth about $1,213,900, a 12.5% rise before any principal is paid down. Today, getting that insurance dropped on a higher value usually means asking the servicer and paying for an appraisal; the order asks FHFA to cut those costs. Nothing changes until FHFA writes the rules.
Today's chart is a different kind of neighbor problem. S&P Global Ratings counts 146 U.S. data center projects delayed or canceled, about three quarters of them tied to local pushback, and local governments in 40 states have imposed bans. The top complaints are energy costs, water, air pollution and noise, which will sound familiar to anyone who has sat through a planning commission meeting.
Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average stands at 7.33% and the 15-year at 6.98% from Friday, with Monday's reading due later today, and the Fed meets Tuesday and Wednesday. Locally, inventory barely moved: the board wide table slipped to 2,623 from 2,631, with 140 coming soon and 192 brand new. The five city ledger held at 686, as Hayward rose to 201 from 198, Newark slipped to 90 from 91 and Union City to 65 from 67, while Fremont (227) and Milpitas (103) held.
What this means for you: if you are buying with less than 20% down, ask your lender how the mortgage insurance comes off, because under the pilot your home's value, not just your payments, could get you there. If you own and are weighing a refinance, ask whether the title waiver could apply to your loan. If you are selling, today's buyer is still pricing a 7.33% payment, so price for that from day one.
