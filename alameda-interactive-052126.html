Rates Ease 8 BPS To 6.67% After Three-Day Climb; ARMs Surge To 10% Of Mortgages (Highest Since Oct 2025); House Strips Forced-Sale Provision From Housing Bill

Three threads to carry into client conversations today.

First, rates eased back. The 30-year fixed dropped 8 bps to 6.67%, the first downtick after climbing every session this week (Mon 6.68, Tue 6.75, Wed 6.75, Thu 6.67). The 15-year fell 3 bps to 6.22%. The 10-year was flat at 4.57% session-to-session, but it is still 9 bps below Monday’s 4.66% reading, which finally gave mortgage rates room to come down. The MBS-Treasury spread narrative from Tuesday has begun to unwind. Rates were not stuck at 6.75%. The buy decision should not be waiting for cuts; it should be running the math at the rate available today.

Second, the ARM share is the leading-indicator worth flagging. Adjustable rate mortgages made up 10% of all mortgages last week, the highest share since October 2025, per Market Briefs. ARMs offer lower starting rates and lower starting payments, which is why buyers reached for them when fixed hit 6.75%. But payments reset later, often with no cap. If the affordability gap was big enough that an ARM looked attractive at 6.75% fixed, the math probably still works on the fixed at 6.67% today, without the reset risk later.

Third, the housing-policy update. The Senate version of a new housing bill would have forced investors with 350+ units to sell homes they built within seven years. The House just cut that provision, and the bill heads back to the Senate for a final vote. For Bay Area build-to-rent and institutional landlord positions, the legislative intent is still in motion, but the specific seven-year forced-sale mechanic just got pulled.

10-Year Settles Flat At 4.57% (-9 Bps From Monday); WTI Reverses Above $100 (+2.96%); EV Sales Cool After Tax Credit Expires But Trade-Ins Hold

The 10-year settled at 4.57% Wednesday, flat session-to-session but still 9 basis points below Monday’s 4.66% reading. That move is what gave mortgage rates room to ease today (the 30-year dropped 8 bps to 6.67%). The bond market is signaling the macro tape is settling. WTI Crude is moving the other way intraday: $101.17 (+2.96%) after closing under $100 yesterday on Iran-deal optimism. Citi still sees Brent reaching $120 by year-end. Gold at $4,549 (+0.31%). Rice futures hit their highest level since August 2025, +16% in the last month as fertilizer prices linked to Iran tensions climb and El Niño drier conditions hit Southeast Asia.

The fiscal pitch worth carrying: Jeff Bezos called for zero federal income tax on the bottom 50% of U.S. earners. That group paid only 3% of total U.S. income tax in 2023, while the top 1% pays around 40%. Consumer-affordability narratives are starting to shape policy proposals across both sides of the aisle.

US quarterly EV sales fell from a ~440K peak in Q3 2025 to ~210K in Q1 2026 after the federal EV tax credit expired. Source: Cox Automotive sales estimates.

The structural consumer story sits in the chart above. U.S. quarterly EV sales fell from ~440K peak in Q3 2025 to ~210K in Q1 2026, after the federal EV tax credit expired. The data point that goes with it: 72.1% of new EV buyers in April still traded in a gas car, up from 67.1% in January. Even as the headline sales pace cooled, the buyers who DID buy are still moving away from gas, in part because gas prices are up about 44% year-over-year after February’s Iran disruption. The buyer pool is smaller, but the conviction inside that pool is structural, not opportunistic. Carry that lens into the mortgage conversation.

Crypto Holds Bid With Risk-On Tape; Tether Buys SoftBank’s 10,500 BTC Stake In Twenty One Capital; Japan Proposes 55% → 20% Crypto Tax Cut

Crypto held bid Wednesday with the risk-on tape: BTC $77,960 (+0.63%), ETH $2,143 (+0.75%), XRP $1.35 (+1.38%). The headline today is a treasury-side consolidation rather than a price move: Tether bought SoftBank’s full stake in Twenty One Capital (XXI +2.23%), taking control of one of the largest public Bitcoin treasuries, about 10,500 BTC. Twenty One Capital went public in December 2025 with 43,500 BTC and is being positioned as the core of a proposed three-way merger with Strike and Bitcoin miner Elektron Energy.

The regulatory story to track: Japan’s financial regulators are proposing to cut the top crypto tax rate from 55% to a flat 20%, matching the stock-market rate, and would let traders carry losses forward for three years. About 105 tokens, including Bitcoin and Ethereum, would qualify. Adoption is unlikely before 2028, but it is the kind of structural rate-cut that historically pulls a meaningful slug of institutional money into the asset class. The Japan tax cut is the one to bookmark.
