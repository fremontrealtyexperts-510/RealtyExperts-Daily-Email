The 30 Year Fixed Hit A Four Week Low At 6.69% After Wholesale Inflation Came In Flat; A New Rule Lets Banks Keep Your Escrow Interest And States Are Suing; Locally, 722 Homes Are Buyable, Up 15

Rates kept falling. The 30 year fixed eased another 5 basis points to 6.69% and the 15 year slipped 1 to 6.26%. That is the lowest 30 year reading since July 17th, one day shy of four full weeks, and it is the second straight day of declines.
The cause is not a mystery. Mortgage News Daily names two reasons: a favorable producer price reading and lower oil. Both are in the Economy section above, and both point the same direction. Wholesale inflation came in flat when a 0.2% rise was expected, and Brent settled 2.15% lower.
Two items from the newsletter are worth your attention.
• Escrow interest: About 80% of borrowers have an escrow account, the side fund that collects money for taxes and insurance. A new rule from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency lets big banks keep the interest that fund earns instead of passing it to the borrower. Several states are now suing to stop it. Nothing has changed for your clients yet, but this is the kind of item a buyer reads about later and wishes somebody had mentioned at signing.
• Affordable housing bonds: Lenders are bundling affordable housing mortgages into bonds, and issuance already hit $4.5 billion by mid August, topping all of last year. Big yields plus real demand for affordable rentals is drawing capital into a corner of housing that has been starved of it. Money is arriving where the rentals are, even while purchase activity stays slow.
Why a REALTOR® cares: four weeks ago the 30 year was at its recent high and buyers were pausing. It has now come down two days running for reasons anybody can explain in a sentence, which makes this an easy call to make. The buyer who stepped back in mid July has been waiting for exactly this, and the ones who move first are the ones who already ran their numbers.

The story today is the one that moves mortgage rates, so it is worth reading even if wholesale inflation sounds like somebody else's problem.
Producer prices measure what businesses charge each other, before anything reaches a shelf. Forecasters expected a 0.2% rise in July and got nothing at all. Year over year, the headline eased to 4.7% from 5.5% in June, the third straight month of cooling after the April and May peak. Core, which strips out food and energy, came in at 4.2%. See the chart below.
This matters because producer prices arrive ahead of consumer prices. When the cost of doing business stops climbing, the Fed gets room to consider cutting, and mortgage rates follow. That is not theory today, it is the stated reason the 30 year fixed hit a four week low, which you will find in the Real Estate section.
The newsletter reported the 10 year at 4.64% and unchanged. The Treasury's own daily curve has it at 4.63%, down 5 basis points from 4.68%. Small in isolation, but a bond yield falling is the whole mechanism behind a lower mortgage rate, so the direction is the part that matters.
Brent settled at $87.07, down 2.15% on the day. The newsletter printed the level correctly and then called it flat. It was not flat, and cheaper oil is the second reason rates eased this week.
Gold slipped to $4,371 and silver held at $64.74, essentially unchanged at a penny lower. Metals are taking the softer inflation news as a reason to sit still.
Wholesale prices were expected to rise 0.2% in July and came in flat instead. Year over year the headline eased to 4.7% from 5.5% in June, a third straight month of cooling. Core, the amber bars, sits at 4.2%.

Another flat morning. Bitcoin is near $62,996, Ether is around $1,880, and XRP is just over $1.01. Nothing here moved even three quarters of a percent.
The activity is elsewhere. Chime is exploring stablecoin wallets inside its banking app, joining Visa, BlackRock and Coinbase in doing more with digital dollars. Stablecoin payment volume hit $390 billion last year, which is why the payments side keeps growing while the speculative side does not.
Gemini shows the other half of that picture. The Winklevoss exchange posted its fourth straight quarterly loss, down $107 million, with revenue actually up. Assets on the platform shrank 54% to $8.4 billion. Customers are withdrawing faster than the business can grow into it.
The pattern is worth naming, because it shows up in housing too. Infrastructure gets built out and adopted quietly while the speculative layer on top of it deflates. The plumbing and the casino are not the same business, even when they share an address.
