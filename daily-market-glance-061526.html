Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.58% As The Iran Peace Deal Drops Oil; Sellers Lose Their Edge Nationally While The East Bay Board Holds At 2,919 Active On A Heavy 261 New And 212 Closed
The 30-year fixed eased to 6.58% and the 15-year to 6.14%. The weekend's big mover — the Iran peace deal — pushed oil sharply lower, and softer energy prices are exactly the kind of input that can help cool inflation and, over time, take pressure off mortgage rates.
The national tone is shifting toward buyers. Lennar (LEN -4.90%), the country's largest homebuilder, warned of fewer sales next quarter as high rates and shaky confidence keep buyers on the sidelines; it cut its average home price 5% to $371,000 and leaned on incentives. And for the first time since the pandemic, the average U.S. home is selling below its asking price — buyers now have the most leverage in years. The catch is timing: homes that sell in about four weeks still beat the market, while those lingering 18 weeks fall behind.
Locally, the East Bay board held near flat at 2,919 active (up just 4 from Friday), but the listing engine roared back after the weekend: 261 new listings hit the board — up 43 from Friday's 218 — against 212 closings. Oakland led the surge with 78 new on 769 active; Fremont added 24 new on 360 active.

For agents: the headline is leverage shifting to buyers — nationally, homes are now selling below ask for the first time since the pandemic, and the biggest builder is discounting. But locally the board barely moved (2,919) even as 261 new listings poured in, so well-priced, well-presented homes that sell inside ~4 weeks still win. Tell sellers to price right and move fast; tell buyers they finally have room to negotiate — and that the Iran peace deal nudging oil (and maybe rates) lower only helps.

Stocks edged higher into the weekend — the S&P 500 up 0.50%, the DOW 0.70%, and the NASDAQ 0.31%. The star was the debut: Elon Musk's SpaceX (SPCX +19.22%) blasted off in the biggest IPO ever, raising over $75 billion and closing valued above $2.1 trillion — instantly the 7th most valuable public company in the world, ahead of Tesla's $1.5 trillion. Musk is now a trillionaire, and there are already rumors that SpaceX and Tesla could merge. (The first chart below shows where SpaceX now sits among the giants.)
There's a new king of ETFs, too. Vanguard just passed BlackRock (BLK +1.52%) to become the largest ETF provider in the U.S., running about $4.39 trillion to BlackRock's $4.36 trillion — ending a reign BlackRock had held since 2003. Vanguard's VOO also became the first ETF ever to hold $1 trillion on its own.

Done deal. President Trump and Iran confirmed a deal to end the war over the weekend — the Strait of Hormuz is reopening and the U.S. naval blockade is clearing. Oil fell hard on the news: Brent slid 5% to $82.95 and U.S. crude touched $80 for the first time since March. Lower energy is good news for the inflation fight — and, eventually, for mortgage rates.
Precious metals went the other way. Gold rebounded 2.85% to $4,359, recovering much of last week's tumble, while silver kept climbing to about $70.81 (+4.2%) — now up over 100% year-to-date as one of 2026's best-performing assets. The 10-year Treasury eased to 4.44%.
A note on scale: the two charts below put the week's headlines in perspective. After Friday's record IPO, SpaceX vaulted to the 7th most valuable public company on earth — and the bigger picture is striking. NVIDIA alone, near $5 trillion, is worth almost as much as Germany's entire economy ($5.45T), and the three biggest U.S. companies combined rival roughly two-thirds of China's GDP. A handful of American tech giants now stand shoulder-to-shoulder with whole nations.
One cloud: a U.S. appeals court ruled that President Trump's 10% import tariff can stay in place during appeals, so importers keep paying — and those costs can still reach shoppers — while the case likely heads back to the Supreme Court.
After its record IPO, SpaceX (#7, $2.1T) now outranks Tesla among the world's most valuable public companies — in a top 10 dominated by AI and Big Tech, led by NVIDIA near $5 trillion.
