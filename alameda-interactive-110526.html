REAL ESTATE

30-Year Fixed: 7.22% unchanged  |  15-Year Fixed: 6.86% +0.01

Prop. 37 is still projected to pass. The Secretary of State's semi-official count as of 7:20 PM PT Wednesday has it at 61.2% Yes (4,783,474 votes) to 38.8% No (3,036,774), about 268,000 more ballots than Wednesday morning, with Alameda County at 67.5% Yes. Mail ballots still arriving are being counted, and the state certifies the result by Dec. 11.

Rates held. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.22% this morning, unchanged, and the 15-year 6.86%, up 0.01. Rates kept easing after our Wednesday report: the 30-year finished Wednesday at 7.22%, not the 7.25% we printed that morning. At 7.22%, a Fremont buyer at today's $1,299,444 median with 20% down would pay about $7,070 a month in principal and interest, $57 less than at Tuesday's 7.30%.

Zillow felt the rates too. Its residential revenue, mostly fees from real estate agents, fell 4% from a year ago to $419 million, which it blamed on higher than expected mortgage rates slowing sales, while rentals grew 26%. Most of its $158 million loss was a $165 million settlement of a shareholder lawsuit over Zillow Offers, its old home buying business. The stock is down 9.0% this morning.

What it means for you. Stocks are falling and the 10-Year is easing this morning, which tends to reach lender rate sheets within a day. If you are close to locking, ask your lender to watch it with you rather than guess. Sellers, 171 homes in our five cities have been listed 60 days or longer; the market is rewarding the right price, not the hopeful one.

Today's count. Board wide: 2,443 listings across the 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, up from 2,438, with 149 coming soon and 129 brand new. The five city ledger carries 634 live listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, 567 on the market and 67 coming soon, down from 642.

Fremont holds 214 live listings: 163 active, 30 coming soon, 20 brand new and 1 back on the market, with a median on market list price of $1,299,444 at $814 a square foot and a typical listing out 36 days. Hayward carries 181, Milpitas 97, Newark 82 and Union City 60. Across all five cities the median is $1,049,000 at $667 a square foot, 276 homes are priced under a million, and 171 have been sitting 60 days or longer.

STOCKS

S&P 500: 7,688.62 +1.58%  |  Dow: 53,356.12 +3.03%  |  Nasdaq: 25,736.51 +1.44%

Wednesday's rally is coming undone. The numbers above are Wednesday's closes, the morning after Election Day, when the Dow jumped 3.03%. As of 8:55 AM PT Thursday the Nasdaq is down 4.31%, the S&P 500 down 3.18% and the Dow down 2.36%, led by chip and AI stocks: the Philadelphia semiconductor index is down 9.3%, and Qualcomm is down 11.3% after reporting results Wednesday evening.

South Korea's chip boom set a record. Its current account surplus hit $61.5 billion in September, 4.3 times a year earlier, as goods exports reached $130.7 billion, and the first nine months total $340.7 billion against $123.1 billion for all of 2025. Samsung and SK hynix, both with U.S. headquarters in San Jose, make most of those memory chips. Korea's Kospi fell 5.3% Thursday as the selloff spread.

ECONOMY

10-Year Treasury: 4.90% -0.05  |  Gold: $4,073.00 +3.37%  |  Silver: $52.28 +5.08%  |  Brent Crude: $94.62 -8.67%

Private hiring held up. Private employers added 86,000 jobs in October, ADP reported Wednesday, a second solid month, though professional and business services lost 27,000, a third monthly decline in a row. The government's own October report is due Friday.

Closer to home. ADP does not allow charts built from its data, so we drew the local version from public BLS figures. Over the year to September, the San Jose metro added 19,900 private jobs, 11,100 of them in education and health, while the East Bay, home to Fremont, Hayward, Newark and Union City, lost 5,700, led by manufacturing (down 5,100) and construction (down 4,400).

Oil fell hard. Brent settled Wednesday at $94.62, down 8.67% and back under $100, on hopes the Iran war winds down sooner. It is up 2.36% this morning near $96.85. Gold settled up 3.37% and silver 5.08%; the numbers above are Wednesday's settlements and Treasury's close.
