Today's theme is rent versus buy. Zillow's latest report puts the typical U.S. rent at $1,932 a month in September, while a new buyer's payment, with 10% down and taxes and insurance included, is $3,037. That is $1,105 more every month to own, and rental demand is up 19.2% from a year ago as would-be buyers rent instead.
Here the gap is far wider (chart). In Zillow's San Francisco metro, which includes Oakland, Fremont and Hayward, rent is $3,445 against an $8,931 buyer payment, $5,486 apart. In the San Jose metro, which includes Milpitas, it is $3,812 against $11,845, so the payment would cover the rent three times over. Our two metros also lead the country in rental demand growth: San Jose is up 82.5% from a year ago and San Francisco 66.9%.
The local twist is that rents are rising faster than buyer costs. San Francisco metro rent is up 11.8% in a year while the buyer payment rose 7.1%; nationally it is the other way around. On our own MLS, Fremont's median asking price of $1,300,000 works out to about $8,000 a month in principal and interest alone at today's 7.29% with 10% down, before taxes and insurance.
Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average eased to 7.29% today from 7.35%, and the 15-year is 6.95%, after the Fed held its rate Wednesday (more in Economy). The board wide table is 2,558, from 2,554, with 141 coming soon and 139 brand new. The five city ledger is 670, from 674: Milpitas (103), Newark (89) and Union City (60) held, while Fremont eased to 225 and Hayward to 193.
What this means for you: renting wins month one, nationally and even more so here, so buying is a bet on equity and on staying put. With metro rents climbing about 10% a year, a renter weighing a purchase should compare both paths over five to ten years, not one month. Sellers should know their buyer is often a renter doing exactly that math, so a price that pencils against today's rent gets the call.

Levels are Wednesday, October 28 at the closing bell. Live at 11:23 AM PT Thursday: S&P 500 down 0.58%, Dow down 0.55%, Nasdaq down 1.59%.
Alphabet is the story. Its shares are down 13.2% as of 11:23 AM PT, the day after earnings, and the Nasdaq is down 1.59%. Over six months through Wednesday, Microsoft was up 25.1% and Alphabet down 7.3% (chart); today's drop stretches that gap to about 44 points.
Alphabet's top line was strong: revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion and Google Cloud more than doubled, up 103%. What spooked investors was the spending. Capital expenditures hit $51.4 billion in the quarter, more than double a year ago, free cash flow turned negative ($9.1 billion), and earnings per share fell to $2.12 from $2.87, partly on an $8.7 billion loss on stock holdings. Market Briefs said Google's AI sales dipped; the company's release says the opposite. Microsoft's Azure grew 48% on revenue of $92.3 billion, up 19%, and its stock is down 0.35%. Wall Street's message: spend all you like, as long as the cash comes back first.
