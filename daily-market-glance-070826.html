Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.63% As The Housing Market Cools; Asking Prices Post Their Biggest Yearly Drop Since 2017; The East Bay Board Holds At 2,724 Active On 133 New And 211 Closed
The 30-year fixed eased to 6.63% and the 15-year held at 6.17%. A small step down, but rates in this range keep the lock-in effect in place, so most owners stay put and resale supply stays tight.
The bigger story is a market that is finally cooling. About 44% of agents now describe conditions as balanced, and national asking prices have fallen 2.5% from a year ago, the biggest annual drop since 2017. For buyers, that translates into more negotiating room and fewer bidding wars than we have seen in years.
There is a quieter shift on the commercial side too. AI data-center demand is spilling into warehouses, with data-center tenants now taking more than 40% of new industrial leasing in some markets. More competition for space can mean firmer rents for warehouse owners.
Locally, the East Bay board held at 2,724 active (essentially flat from yesterday), with 133 new listings and 211 closings. Oakland again led the board with 31 new on 713 active; Fremont added 14 new on 328 active.

Stocks slipped across the board to start the day. The S&P 500 eased 0.45% to 7,503, the DOW dipped 0.25% to 52,925, and the NASDAQ led the way down, off 1.16% to 25,818, as renewed tension between the U.S. and Iran put investors on edge.
The AI build-out is now a borrowing story. Amazon (AMZN +0.75%) is tapping the bond market for $25 billion to fund its AI infrastructure, calling the technology a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity worth the debt. It joins a parade of big bond sales from SpaceX, Nvidia (NVDA +0.71%), and others.
Two eye-catching moves rounded out the tape. Rivian jumped into a large stock sale, offering 75 million new shares to raise cash, and the dilution knocked its shares down about 18% at one point. And Samsung posted an eye-popping 1,800% jump in operating profit, yet its stock fell 8% because stellar AI numbers are now simply expected.

The 10-year Treasury yield ticked up to 4.45%, climbing back from a June dip near 4.38% but still shy of its spring peak of 4.65% (see today's chart). Inflation and labor data remain the key drivers from here, and the flare-up with Iran only adds to the uncertainty.
Gold has lost its shine. After months of gains, it has slid more than 20% from its January peak and is now officially in a bear market, with investors pulling about $18 billion from gold ETFs. Gold eased another 0.57% to $4,133 and silver dropped 2.43% to $58.53, while Brent crude jumped 3.30% to $76.58 after the U.S. revoked Iran's oil export license.
On the savings front, the new Trump accounts have launched, enrolling more than 6 million children with a $1,000 government seed that converts to an IRA at 18. It lands at a timely moment: Gallup says about half of U.S. households have nothing saved for retirement.

Crypto drifted lower, with Bitcoin off 0.94% to $62,734, Ether down 0.94% to $1,751, and XRP off 1.74% to $1.09. Even so, adoption keeps spreading: Tether put $20 million into Mercado Bitcoin, a top Brazilian exchange, betting that stablecoins can reach the millions of Brazilians who bank around high inflation without a traditional account.
A longer-term worry resurfaced too. Galaxy Digital (GLXY -2.64%) estimates that future quantum computers could put about $470 billion of Bitcoin, roughly a third of all coins in circulation, at risk by cracking the math that secures it. That threat is years away, but it is on the industry's radar.
