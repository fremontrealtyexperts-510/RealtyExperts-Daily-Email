Mortgage rates held near Friday's lower level. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average is 7.34%, up 0.01, with the 15-year at 6.89%, and Freddie Mac's weekly survey last Thursday was 7.28%. On today's $1,248,000 Fremont median with 20% down, that is about $6,872 a month in principal and interest.
Realtor.com's featured report this week maps where young adults live with their parents. In the Northeast, 36.9% of 18 to 34 year olds do, well above the national rate, though it is also the only region that has improved. Our cities run higher still on the Census Bureau's own measure (chart): averaging 2020 to 2024, 48.0% of Union City's 18 to 34 year olds lived in a parent's household, Hayward 40.9%, Newark 38.9%, Fremont 36.4% and Milpitas 33.0%, against 39.3% for California and 32.8% for the nation. A lot of future first time buyers are saving at home.
In realtor.com's latest weekly data, homes for sale are up 8.6% from a year ago, new listings are down 3.5%, and the median list price of $405,000 is down 2.4%, the 44th straight week below last year. Homes are taking 66 days to sell, two days longer than a year ago.
Monday's export shows 2,244 board wide, from 2,262 Saturday, with 126 coming soon and 91 brand new. The five city ledger is 560, from 565 Saturday: Milpitas (85) fell three and Newark (67) two, while Fremont (194), Hayward (161) and Union City (53) held.
What this means for you: parents with grown children at home, this is a good week to run the numbers on a first purchase together. Buyers, listings are sitting longer, so there is room to negotiate.

Nvidia's board brought Jensen Huang back, and the market came back with him. In a filing with the SEC this morning, Nvidia said its board unanimously reinstated Huang as President, CEO and director on Sunday, 11 days after he stepped down. Colette Kress, who led the company in the meantime, stays on as CFO, and the board's independent committee will carry its investigation to a conclusion. Nvidia jumped 31.48% to $165.05 (chart), its biggest one day gain in more than ten years and one of its four best since it went public in 1999. That is roughly $952 billion of market value added in a single day. The stock is still 31.0% below its Oct. 6 record.
Everything that fell last week bounced. The S&P 500 rose 4.73% and the Nasdaq 6.51%, both their best days since April 9, 2025, and the Dow gained 3.01%. Chips led, this time upward: the Philadelphia semiconductor index gained 14.03%, Micron 19.04% and AMD 6.85%, and the VIX fear gauge fell to 26.50. Anthropic went the other way, down 1.43% to $87.64, still above its $83 offering price. The S&P 500 (7.4% below its record) and the Dow (8.7% below) are back out of correction territory, while the Nasdaq, 12.7% below, is still in one. One great day does not erase a rough month.
