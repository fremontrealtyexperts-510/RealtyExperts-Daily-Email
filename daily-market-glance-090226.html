  REAL ESTATE

  
Both mortgage rates set fresh 52 week highs again, for the second day running
30-YEAR FIXED
6.89%
15-YEAR FIXED
6.39%
The 30 year fixed rose two basis points to 6.89% and the 15 year rose one to 6.39%, per Mortgage News Daily. Both printed a new 52 week high for the second straight day. MND's own range now reads 5.99% to 6.89% on the 30 year and 5.55% to 6.39% on the 15 year, and today's number is the top of each.
The cause is not in the mortgage market. It is the Hormuz strikes pushing the ten year Treasury up four basis points to 4.79%, and mortgages are priced off that curve. Yesterday MND put the move down to month end bond mechanics, which can unwind. This one is a geopolitical risk premium, which is a different animal and does not unwind on a calendar.
What it costs, concretely: on a $450,000 purchase with 20% down, the payment is now about $2,369 a month. That is $5 more than yesterday and about $212 more than in February. Small daily steps, and they add up in one direction.
The used car chart in the Economy section is worth two minutes of your day even though it is not a housing number. Barely a third of used cars now sell for under $20,000, against more than half in 2019, and a $10,000 to $15,000 budget that once bought a 4.7 year old car with 58,000 miles now buys an 8.7 year old car with 98,000 miles. That is the same squeeze your buyers are feeling on the housing side, showing up in a different line of their budget. A buyer stretching for a car payment on an older vehicle is a buyer with a thinner debt to income cushion when they sit down with a lender.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Board wide: 2,649 listings across the 15 city board, counting everything active alongside everything in contract, with 202 coming soon and 192 brand new today. That is 15 fewer than yesterday, a normal day's drift after Monday's month end drop of 80.
• Fremont: 234 active across all property types, made up of 32 townhomes, 78 condos and 124 detached homes and duplexes, plus 98 in contract, 40 coming soon and 26 new today.
• Five city live ledger: 695 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, up 3 from yesterday. Fremont 265, Hayward 184, Milpitas 116, Newark 76, Union City 54.
• The read: supply is flat and demand is steady. Fremont shed three active listings and added one to contract. Coming soon rose to 40 in Fremont alone, which is the pipeline for the next two weeks and it is not thinning.
What to tell a client this week: rates are at a one year high because of a war risk premium in the bond market, not because of anything in housing. That premium can come out as fast as it went in. Nobody should time a purchase on that guess, but anyone who locked in the last few weeks did fine, and anyone floating should talk to their lender today rather than Friday.

Ten year yield and Brent are Tuesday's September 1 settlements. Gold and silver are live spot quotes at about 7:46 AM Pacific on September 2, with the change measured from Tuesday's settlement.
The Strait of Hormuz is driving this whole section. The U.S. struck Iranian targets after two oil tankers were attacked in the strait, and the money moved the way you would expect: out of Treasuries and into oil.
Three of the four numbers on this line had to be corrected against the source before publishing.
• Ten year Treasury: the newsletter printed 4.81% and called it a one basis point move. Treasury's own daily yield curve has Tuesday's close at 4.79%, up four basis points from Monday's 4.75%. Their own story says the yield spiked on the strikes, which a one basis point move plainly is not. We publish Treasury's number.
• Brent: the newsletter printed $96.67 and a 2.16% gain. ICE front month futures settled Tuesday at $94.65, up 4.60% from Monday. Their level is too high and their move is less than half the real one, which understates the single biggest price reaction to the strikes.
• Gold: they printed $4,355 and a 0.94% decline as of Tuesday's close, which does not square with where gold actually settled Tuesday. And it is stale in direction. Gold and silver both fell hard Tuesday and both have rebounded roughly 1.9% this morning. Fortune's 9 AM Eastern print had gold at $4,334, up $3 from the same hour Tuesday, and the metal has added about $50 in the hour and a half since. The morning print is true and it understates the session, so the cards carry the live quote instead.
• Silver was left out entirely again, as it usually is. It is on the card.
Gas is over $4 a gallon and the White House met with about a dozen refinery bosses to push for more gasoline and diesel, offering faster permits and more Venezuelan crude. If Brent stays near $95 that is a hard promise to keep.
