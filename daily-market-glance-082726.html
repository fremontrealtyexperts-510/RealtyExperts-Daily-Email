The 30 year fixed is 6.75% and the 15 year is 6.32%, both unchanged for a second day, per Mortgage News Daily. After last week's bad print and its quiet correction, the 15 year has now held steady at 6.31% to 6.32% for four straight sessions. That number is trustworthy again.
Why rates are not moving, in one line: core inflation is 3.3% and the Fed's target is 2%. The Economy section below has the chart. Anyone advising a client to wait for a big drop in mortgage rates should look at it first.
Two housing items from this morning worth a moment:
• New York is building for the wrong buyer: new condos for sale are projected to fall 11% through 2029, but entry level units are set to drop 74%. Builders are chasing luxury. It is a national pattern, not just a New York one, and it is the same squeeze that pushes first time buyers here toward townhomes and condos rather than detached houses.
• Half of planned data centers may never get built: Kimmeridge Energy Management estimates 50% of U.S. projects are delayed or scrapped, with local opposition in Texas, Ohio and Pennsylvania. We showed the data center map two days ago and made the point that those buildings compete with home building for crews, land and power. If half of them stall, some of that pressure comes off.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Board wide: 2,709 listings across the 15 city board, counting everything active alongside everything in contract, with 202 coming soon and 171 brand new today.
• Fremont: 251 active across all property types, made up of 37 townhomes, 87 condos and 127 detached homes, plus 101 in contract, 41 coming soon and 20 new today.
• Five city live ledger: 703 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, up 7 from yesterday.
• The streak ended: Fremont held exactly 102 listings in contract for three straight days and today it is 101. At the same time active supply jumped from 240 to 251, the largest single day gain in weeks, and the coming soon block fell from 46 to 41. Read those together and the picture is clear: staged listings went live. Supply is arriving faster than it is being absorbed.
• What that means for a seller this week: you now have eleven more competitors than you had yesterday, and 20 of Fremont's listings are brand new today. Pricing to the last comp is how a listing sits. Price to the buyer who has more to choose from this morning than they did last night.
• Median asking in Fremont is $1,299,000 at 27 median days on market. Newark is the quickest of our five at 25 days.

A flat session that was really a holding pattern. All three indexes finished within a quarter of a percent of where they started, because the market was waiting on two things: Nvidia's results after the bell and the Fed's Jackson Hole symposium, which opens today.
Nvidia delivered. Revenue came in at $96.2 billion for the quarter, up 106% from a year ago, with data center sales up 117% to $89.0 billion. The company guided to $108 billion for the current quarter. Shares rose after hours even though they are down about 4% over the past five sessions, which tells you the bar is now set by expectations rather than by results.
Salesforce also beat, and paired it with a deal to embed its systems inside Anthropic's Claude. The stock is still down roughly 19% on the year.
Six quarters, from $46.7 billion to a guided $108 billion. The top bar is company guidance, not a reported result.

Ten year yield and Brent are Wednesday's August 26 settlements. Gold and silver are Fortune spot prints at 7:15 AM Eastern today.
Core PCE, the inflation gauge the Fed actually watches, came in at 3.3% for July. That is a tenth above what forecasters expected and it is the number that matters most to anyone waiting on a rate cut.
Read the chart below before you read anyone's opinion about it. Core inflation has spent this entire year in a narrow band above 3%, never once approaching the Fed's 2% target. The Fed meets at Jackson Hole starting today, and this is the reading that makes a near term cut hard to argue for. For housing that translates simply: the case for waiting on dramatically lower mortgage rates just got weaker again.
The ten year rose two basis points to 4.66%. This morning's newsletter printed 4.65% and called the change zero; Treasury's own daily curve has August 25 at 4.64% and August 26 at 4.66%, so the yield went up, not sideways.
Brent settled at $87.84, down 0.84%. The newsletter reported $87.25 and a 0.67% fall, and neither figure matches the front month contract's settlement.
Gold fell $26 to $4,589 and silver eased 21 cents to $68.34. Both of Fortune's day over day comparisons reconcile exactly against what we published yesterday. The newsletter's $4,685 gold print sits about $96 above the morning spot price and has the direction wrong as well.
Core inflation has not been near 2% at any point this year. That gap is why the Fed is not cutting.
Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis, core PCE price index, current vintage.
