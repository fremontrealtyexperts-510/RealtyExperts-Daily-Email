Mortgage Rates Sit Near A One Year High As Purchase Applications Slide 7%; The East Bay Board Climbs To 2,776 Active On 188 New And 193 Closed

The daily 30-year fixed sits at 6.64% and the 15-year at 6.18%. The bigger story is the trend: the weekly average just printed its highest level in nearly a year, and buyers noticed, with purchase applications sliding 7% in a week. Rising fuel costs and Middle East unrest pushed bond yields higher, and mortgage rates went along for the ride. Today's chart shows the full round trip: about 6.77% last August, a 6.02% low in March, and back to 6.64% now.
The market is also splitting in two. Luxury home prices jumped 4.7% to an average $1.37 million while everyone else's rose just 1.5%, powered by cash-rich buyers from booming tech and AI companies who do not much care what mortgage rates are doing. Those trophy purchases keep the market moving, but they pull comps (and prices) up for everyone else.
Locally, the East Bay board climbed to 2,776 active, up from 2,738, on 188 new listings and 193 closings. Inventory rebuilt a touch even with closings nearly matching new supply. Oakland led as usual with 50 new on 723 active, followed by Fremont at 28 and Danville at 24.

Stocks climbed across the board, with the NASDAQ up 0.62% to 26,269, the S&P 500 up 0.38% to 7,572, and the DOW up 0.29% to 52,658. The quiet theme of the day: the AI supply chain keeps collecting tolls.
ASML (ASML +2.23%), the Dutch company that makes the only machines capable of producing the world's most advanced AI chips, raised its full year sales forecast to as much as $51.6 billion. When you run the only toll booth on the road to AI, you can raise the toll. Alibaba (BABA +4.78%) added its own AI headline: its Qwen assistant is coming to iPhones in China, since Apple (AAPL +4.01%) needed a Beijing approved local partner.
Not everything soared. SpaceX (SPCX -0.60%) slipped below its $135 IPO price for the first time as the listing hype cools. And United Airlines (UAL +0.52%) beat expectations but warned of a looming $6 billion fuel bill, which could push airline tickets (already up over 25% this year) even higher.

Stocks, the 10-Year, Brent, and crypto as of the July 15th close; gold and silver are live spot from the morning of July 16th (Market Briefs, Trading Economics, Yahoo Finance).
Retirement math is the story of the day. Americans say they need $1.2 million to retire comfortably, yet most expect to reach their golden years with less than $500,000 saved, since everyday expenses and credit card debt eat paychecks before savings get a turn. A SoFi survey shows exactly when people get going (see today's chart): about half of adults start saving by 35, and 13% never start at all.
The labor market keeps sending mixed signals. The U.S. added just 57,000 jobs last month, well short of the 117,000 Wall Street expected, as AI adoption and the U.S. and Iran conflict slow hiring. Yet a Dallas staffing firm just posted its best June since before the pandemic, a reminder that some regional markets run warmer than the national tape.
In the markets, the 10-Year Treasury eased to 4.55%. Gold slid to about $4,010 this morning, its lowest neighborhood since November, as a fifth straight day of U.S. strikes on Iranian military sites lifted oil and firmed the case for rates staying higher. Silver followed to about $56.68, and Brent crude rose 1.11% to $85.67.
Half of Americans start saving for retirement by 35, and 13% have not started at all.
