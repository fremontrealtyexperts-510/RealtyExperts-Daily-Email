PPI Hits 6%, Warsh Replaces Powell, And Buyers Still Bought At 6.57%

Three threads to anchor client conversations.

First, the counter-narrative buyers need to hear. MBA purchase applications rose 4% week-over-week and 7% year-over-year, even as the 30-year mortgage rate hit its highest point in five weeks. Less Iran optimism and hot April inflation pushed bond yields up; mortgage rates followed. Buyers bought anyway. That is the data point that disarms the “I’m waiting for rates to drop” client. They are not just waiting, they are getting passed by the buyers who are transacting.

Second, the Fed-Chair changeover matters more than the market is pricing. Powell’s last day as Chair is tomorrow. Kevin Warsh, the incoming Chair, was the hawk on the FOMC during 2006-2011, famously dissenting against more aggressive easing post-crisis. He is taking over with PPI at 6%, CPI at 3.8%, and Wall Street giving a 39% chance to a Fed HIKE this year. The “rates fall in 2026” thesis is officially dead. Repeat that to every fence-sitter on your roster.

Third, the Hamptons softening signal. Rental rates there are down 2.5% year-over-year just before the U.S. Open at Shinnecock Hills (150,000 visitors). Last year’s gold rush flooded supply, this year’s market is absorbing the overhang. The lesson for Bay Area listing agents: when a tier sees a sudden supply pulse, prices recalibrate within months. Watch your local inventory closely. Today’s number is up.

PPI Hits 6.0% (Highest Since 2022); Wall Street Now Prices 39% Chance Of Fed HIKE This Year

The big macro story: April PPI just printed and it is worse than April CPI. Producer prices jumped 1.4% month-over-month, the biggest monthly increase in over four years. Annual PPI hit 6.0%, the highest since 2022. The chart above shows the spike. That is the wholesale-prices side of the same Iran-oil and tariff story that pushed CPI to 3.8% on Tuesday. PPI tends to lead CPI, so the next CPI print is unlikely to cool.

The market repricing: Wall Street now sees a 39% chance the Fed HIKES rates this year, not cuts. A week ago that number was near zero. Combined with Warsh replacing Powell as Fed Chair tomorrow, the rate environment for the next six months has been completely re-priced.

The 10-year held at 4.46% (-0.01%), gold cooled to $4,695, and WTI pulled back to $100.36 (-0.65%) but Brent held at $105.67. The energy premium continues to feed the inflation tape. France’s unemployment jumped to 8.1% (highest since 2021), so the global growth picture is softening even as inflation runs. That is the stagflation worry the bond market is starting to price.
