Rates below are Friday's close. Mortgage News Daily does not publish on weekends, so these are what carry into Monday.
The 30-year is 6.89%, one basis point higher on Friday and two basis points under its 52-week high of 6.91%. The 15-year is 6.49%.
Friday's jobs report moved the rate conversation more than it moved rates. Fed funds futures now put 58.4% odds on the Fed raising its target a quarter point at the September 16 meeting, 41.6% on no change and 0.0% on a cut. The day before the report those hike odds were 49.4%. A stronger labor market gives the Fed more room to move, so the odds jumped about nine points in one session.
Three things worth keeping straight:
• The Fed does not set mortgage rates. The 10-year Treasury does most of that work, and a move the market already expects is not the same as a surprise.
• The real test is Friday, September 11, when the August inflation report lands at 5:30 a.m. Pacific. That number, more than the jobs report, is likely to settle the September 16 question.
• Rates near the top of their yearly range are why buyer traffic is deliberate rather than frantic. Correctly priced homes are still moving, and the coming soon count says sellers are positioning for fall.

  ECONOMY

  
Jobs data released Friday, September 4, 2026 by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The inflation figure is the July CPI, the most recent published.
The economy added 162,000 jobs in August against a consensus of about 53,000. It was the best month since March, a sharp step up from July's 21,000, and it brings the 2026 total to 643,000.
Two surveys disagreed this week, which is worth knowing. ADP had private payrolls up just 38,000 on Wednesday, the smallest gain since January. The government's Friday number came in more than four times higher. When the two split this widely, the honest read is that the truth sits somewhere between them and the next couple of reports matter more than usual.
Unemployment held at 4.1% for a second month, down from the 4.5% peak last November.
Average hourly earnings rose 3.09% over the year, $37.75 against $36.62 last August. That is the slowest wage growth since May 2021, and it is running a little behind inflation, which was 3.4% in the July CPI. Context worth keeping: 3.1% is roughly where wage growth sat through 2019. This reads as a return to normal rather than a break.
• Where the hiring was: leisure and hospitality added 62,000, government 35,000, construction 22,000 and manufacturing 16,000. Construction is the line that matters most for housing supply.
• Where it was not: information lost 23,000 and financial activities lost 11,000.
On layoffs, the picture is better than the headline number suggests. Challenger counted 52,881 announced cuts in August, up 58% from July but down 38% from a year ago, and the lowest August total since 2022. Through August the year's cuts are down 41% from 2025, and announced hiring plans are up 37%. Artificial intelligence remains the leading reason cited year to date at 116,175 cuts, about 22% of the total, though in August it fell to fourth at 3,462, its quietest month since December 2025.
A note on the charts. We rebuilt all four straight from the Bureau of Labor Statistics rather than reusing the versions circulating this week, and every figure checked out. One difference: October 2025 has no unemployment reading because the household survey was not collected that month, so our line shows a dashed gap where the widely shared version draws a solid line through it.
