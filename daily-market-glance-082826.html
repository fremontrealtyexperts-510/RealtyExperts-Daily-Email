The 30 year fixed is 6.81% and the 15 year is 6.35%, per Mortgage News Daily. Both moved up today, the 30 year by six basis points and the 15 year by three, after four sessions of sitting still.
This morning's newsletter printed 6.75% and 6.32%. Those were yesterday's numbers, correct when published and stale by the time the email landed. If a client quotes them at you today, they are six basis points light on the 30 year.
The reason for the move is one section down: the ten year yield rose, Brent rose 2%, and the Fed spent the day at Jackson Hole saying inflation is sticky. Mortgage rates follow the ten year, and the ten year is not falling.
THE RENT STORY, AND WHAT IT ACTUALLY MEANS HERE
Apartment List reported that the national median rent rose 0.1% in August to $1,390, the first August increase in four years, with its vacancy index down to 7.1% and falling for the first time since 2021. That is a real turn and the newsletter reported it accurately.
It also describes nobody in our market. The chart below gives the local answer, and one caution belongs with it: Apartment List and Zillow measure different things, so the $1,390 above and the chart below are not the same series and should never be set side by side. The chart is built entirely from Zillow's index, national line included, so the comparison inside it is honest.
• Typical asking rent in Fremont is $3,358, which is 71% above the national figure and up 5.0% over the year.
• Milpitas is the fastest riser at 8.4%, and the priciest at $3,715. Newark is $3,578, Union City $2,941, Hayward $2,635.
• Every one of the five is outrunning the nation's 2.3%. The cheapest of them still costs a third more than the national number.
• What to do with it: when a buyer says they will rent another year and wait for rates, this is the arithmetic. Renting here is not the national $1,390 story. It is $2,600 to $3,700 a month, rising faster than the country, against a 30 year fixed that moved six basis points today.
ALAMEDA COUNTY (FREMONT FOCUS)
• Board wide: 2,741 listings across the 15 city board, counting everything active alongside everything in contract, with 192 coming soon and 191 brand new today. That is 32 more than yesterday.
• Fremont: 250 active across all property types, made up of 35 townhomes, 85 condos and 130 detached homes and duplexes, plus 99 in contract, 41 coming soon and 22 new today.
• Five city live ledger: 716 listings across Fremont, Hayward, Milpitas, Newark and Union City, up 13 from yesterday.
• Watch the pending count, not the active count. Fremont sat at 102 in contract for three straight days, went to 101 yesterday and is 99 today. It has now slipped under 100 for the first time in over a week while active supply held almost exactly flat at 250. Supply is not the thing that changed. Absorption is.
• Yesterday eleven staged listings went live at once and coming soon fell to 41. Today coming soon held at 41 and 22 more listings came out new. The pipeline is refilling as fast as it empties.
• What that means for a seller this week: your competition is steady and your buyer pool is thinning slightly. Those two facts together argue for pricing at the front of the market rather than the middle of it.
• Median asking in Fremont is $1,289,500 at 29 median days on market. Newark is the quickest of our five at 22 days.
The national median is $1,962 on this index. Fremont is $3,358, and every one of our five cities is rising faster than the country.
Source: Zillow Observed Rent Index, July 2026. Not the same series as the Apartment List figure quoted above.

Software came back to life. CrowdStrike jumped 20.5% and Okta jumped 28.6% after both blew past their quarterly numbers, and that carried the NASDAQ to a 1.57% gain while the DOW managed only 0.20%.
The reason those two moves were so large is worth understanding, because it is the same argument running underneath a lot of this market. Investors have spent the year assuming artificial intelligence would eat business software from the inside. Two of the most exposed names just reported that it has not happened yet, and the stocks repriced violently in a single session.
Credit where it is due: all three index numbers in this morning's newsletter reconciled exactly against what we published yesterday. That is the first clean sweep on the stock line in a while.

Ten year yield and Brent are Thursday's August 27 settlements. Gold and silver are Fortune spot prints at 6:30 AM Eastern this morning. Both metals have fallen hard since that print, which the commentary covers.
Read the metals cards above as a 6:30 AM photograph, not as where the metal sits right now. That distinction matters more today than on any morning this month.
Gold printed $4,608 at 6:30 AM Eastern, up 0.41%, and silver printed $70.62, up 3.33%. Both figures are correct and both reconcile exactly against what we published yesterday. Then the US session opened and hard assets were sold. By about 2:50 PM Eastern, gold futures were at $4,495.90 against Thursday's $4,609.70 settlement, roughly 2.5% lower, and silver futures were at $66.86 against Thursday's $69.43, roughly 3.7% lower. Anyone quoting this morning's spot price at dinner tonight will be quoting a number the market left behind before lunch.
Silver is the one to look at, and the chart below is why. Measured from a year ago it is up 77%, which is the number a bull will hand you. Measured from its January 26 peak of $115.08 it is down about 40%, which is the number nobody hands you. Both are true. A metal that can round trip like that inside seven months is not behaving like a safe haven, whatever it is being sold as.
Two corrections to this morning's newsletter. It put the ten year at 4.68% and called the day's change zero; Treasury's own daily curve has August 26 at 4.66% and August 27 at 4.67%, so the yield rose a basis point. And it reported Brent at $89.53, down 0.19%, when the front month contract settled at $89.70 and rose 2.12%. The direction was wrong, not just the level. Oil up 2% on the day is a different story from oil drifting lower, particularly for anyone watching where inflation goes next.
The Fed is at Jackson Hole. Cleveland's Beth Hammack said now is the time to act on rates, Kansas City's Schmid called inflation sticky and stubborn, and Chair Warsh speaks later today. With core inflation still at 3.3% against a 2% target, the honest read is that nobody on that stage is preparing the market for a cut.
Up 77% in a year and down 40% from January. Both numbers describe the same line.
