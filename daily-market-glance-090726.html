Rates frozen on a closed Monday: the 30-year sits at 6.89%, up 0.73 points since late February, and Friday's blowout jobs report is the reason it is not coming down.

Markets are closed for Labor Day, so mortgage rates sit exactly where Friday left them: 6.89% on the 30-year and 6.49% on the 15-year, each one basis point higher on the day. Mortgage News Daily has the 30-year up 0.73 percentage points since late February, and Friday's jobs report is a good part of the reason it has not come back down.
The chain runs through the bond market, not through the Fed's podium. August payrolls came in at 162,000 against a consensus near 53,000, roughly three times what economists penciled in, while unemployment held at 4.1%. A labor market that strong gives the Federal Reserve room to tighten rather than ease, and fed funds futures moved from about a 49% chance of a September hike the day before the report to roughly 58% right after it. Treasury yields followed. The 10-Year closed Friday at 4.78%, and mortgage rates track that yield far more closely than they track anything anyone at the Fed says out loud.
• Worth saying plainly: these are market implied odds from futures pricing, not a forecast and not a Fed announcement. They move every day.

What this means for you: nothing repriced today because nothing traded, but the direction of travel changed on Friday. If you have been waiting for a September rate cut to time a purchase, the futures market just moved the other way, and waiting has a cost. Sellers still hold the advantage on well prepared homes. That said, 148 of the 576 homes on the market have now been waiting two months or more, which is roughly a quarter of the active inventory and exactly where the negotiating room lives.
August hiring came in around three times the forecast, which pushed rate expectations up and took September relief on mortgage rates off the table.

Markets are closed for Labor Day, so every quote above is Friday, September 4, 2026. The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve close, gold and silver are the COMEX front month at Friday's 4:00 PM ET print, and Brent is the ICE front month settlement.
Today is the most expensive Labor Day at the pump this country has ever had. GasBuddy projected a national average of $4.03 a gallon when it published its holiday forecast on September 1. The holiday arrived and the actual print came in higher still: AAA has the national average at $4.15 this morning, about 96 cents above where it stood last Labor Day, and the first time this holiday has ever opened above four dollars a gallon. The old record was $3.83 in 2012, which the chart below marks as a line so you can see exactly what is being broken.
Californians will read that national number with a hollow laugh. The state average is $5.86 today, a full $1.71 above the national figure, and the Bay Area typically prices above the state line. Diesel is worse: it set an all time record on Friday at $5.85 and added another nickel over the weekend, which matters for anyone moving, renovating or hauling anything this fall.
The cause is supply, not demand. Conflict involving Iran has choked traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, drone strikes on Russian refineries have cut refining capacity, and the two together pushed refining margins to records and disconnected pump prices from crude. Brent settled Friday at $96.28. GasBuddy does expect some relief once the cheaper winter blend returns after September 15.
A note on the newsletter's numbers, because several did not survive checking today. Market Briefs printed gold at $4,459 and Brent at $96.81. The COMEX front month contract we track closed Friday at $4,476.60, and Brent's ICE front month settled at $96.28. It also showed the 10-Year up 0.02 points, while Treasury's own daily curve has 4.78% against Thursday's 4.77%, a move of 0.01. Silver was left out of the newsletter entirely once again, so we sourced it ourselves.
The 2026 bar is GasBuddy's September 1 projection. The holiday came in above it, and both clear the 2012 record the headline claims to break.
