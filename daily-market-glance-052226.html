Rates Ease A Second Day To 6.65%; April Contract Signings Up 4.5% (Best Spring In 3 Years); 30-Year Treasury Hits 19-Year High (5.2%)
Three threads to carry into client conversations today.
First, rates eased a second day. The 30-year fixed is at 6.65%, down 2 bps from yesterday's 6.67% and 10 bps below Wednesday's 6.75% peak. The 15-year sits at 6.23%, essentially flat.
The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.54%, down another basis point and now 12 bps below Monday's 4.66%. The two-day rate retreat is the cleanest run of relief we have seen since early May.
But the long end of the curve is moving the other way. The 30-year U.S. Treasury yield hit 5.2% this week, the highest reading since 2007. Mortgages key off the 10-year, so today's relief is real, but the structural pressure on rates is still upward. The window where 6.65% is available is not a permanent floor.
Second, the demand side has woken up. April U.S. contract signings (deals signed but not yet closed) jumped 4.5%, the strongest reading in three years.
The Midwest led, with Kansas City up 20.7% year-to-date. List prices fell 2.4% nationally, but the share of listings with price cuts dropped too. Buyers are absorbing supply rather than waiting for further markdowns.
Translation: the buyer pool nationally is not waiting for cuts. They are running the math at the rate available and signing.
Third, the Dimon and deficit overlay matters. Dimon's call that rates may rise again, combined with a $2 trillion federal deficit and the 30-year Treasury at a 19-year high, says the macro setup for a sustained rate decline is not in place.
The 30-year mortgage spent four sessions this week above 6.7%. It is at 6.65% Friday morning. Buyers who locked Thursday or Friday are paying ~10 bps less than buyers who locked Tuesday.
On a $1M loan, that is roughly $65 a month or $23K over the life of the loan, just for moving the timing two days. That is the texture of why timing-the-rate is harder than locking-and-moving in this tape.

San Ramon's +5 supply add is the largest single-city move and worth flagging. The Tri-Valley had been the leading pullback area Wednesday and Thursday. The Tri-Valley flip is the story today.
For listing agents: the supply rebound is what was supposed to happen Thursday and did not. When the 30-year ticked back to 6.67 yesterday and held to 6.65 today, the marginal seller who was waiting on a rate signal is back in the market.
The pricing tape is supportive. Closed sales are still 239 strong on the seven-day window, and national contract signings just hit a three-year high.
Pricing to today is the move. Sellers waiting another week are betting on either more rate relief (possible) or that demand absorbs slowly enough that holding pays off (unlikely given the contract-signings data). Pricing to last week is the trap.
For buyer agents: 196 new listings is a thicker board than yesterday but still 60 short of Tuesday's 256 peak. The 10-bp two-day drop in the 30-year is real money on a million-dollar loan.
The Dimon-deficit-Treasury setup means the rate available today is not guaranteed to be the rate available next Tuesday.
If you have a client running pre-approval math, this is the friction-light window between the spring rate spike and whatever comes next. Get pre-approval certified at today's rate and pull the trigger when the right house lands.
The national contract-signings data is your evidence that the broader buyer pool is doing exactly that.

Quantum computing stocks rebounded from a 40-50% Q1 drawdown to finish May 2026 at IONQ +32%, QBTS +28%, RGTI +8% YTD. Source: Google Finance.
All three indices closed Thursday in the green: S&P 7,445 (+0.17%), DOW 50,285 (+0.55%), NASDAQ 26,293 (+0.09%).
The headline almost no one saw coming was a federal-policy intervention. The U.S. government is writing a $2 billion check to nine quantum computing companies and taking an equity stake in each. IBM (IBM +12.48%) gets $1B. D-Wave (QBTS +33.37%), Rigetti (RGTI +30.57%), and Infleqtion (INFG +31.48%) each get $100M.
IonQ (IONQ +12.24%) and Quantum Computing (QUBT +19.35%) also powered up on the news. The chart above tells the year-to-date arc: the quantum names spent Q1 in a 40-to-50% drawdown, then snapped back through April and May. After Thursday's announcement, YTD readings are IONQ +32%, QBTS +28%, RGTI +8%.
That is a federal-policy-driven equity bounce, not a fundamentals bounce. But it is also the cleanest signal yet that the government considers quantum a strategic-tech sector worth direct capital allocation.
The other side of the consumer story was Walmart (WMT -7.72%). Walmart missed earnings and cut its full-year outlook. The detail worth carrying: high gas prices pushed the average Walmart fill-up below 10 gallons for the first time since 2022.
Lower-income shoppers are buying less overall as inflation squeezes the bottom of the consumer pyramid. The K-shaped consumer pattern is widening. Premium and policy-driven sectors win; mass-market retail bleeds.

US 10-Year and Gold as of 4:00 PM ET May 21st; WTI Crude reflects today's intraday move via investing.com.
The 10-year ticked down again to 4.54% Thursday, off 12 basis points from Monday's 4.66%. WTI Crude is extending the slide intraday at $95.60 (-0.78%), now nearly $6 below where it sat 48 hours ago and the deepest sub-$100 print since early May. Gold pulled back to $4,531 (-0.24%).
On the surface the disinflation trade is winning: yields lower, oil lower, gold lower.
But the bond market is flashing a contradictory signal underneath. The 30-year U.S. Treasury yield recently hit 5.2%, the highest reading since 2007, a 19-year high. The long end is telling a different story than the belly of the curve.
Jamie Dimon (JPM +0.34%) tied those threads together this week. The JPMorgan CEO said U.S. interest rates may actually start to rise again.
His logic: Americans saved more during the pandemic, but those savings are getting spent down. Heavy federal spending plus the AI buildout could keep growth resilient. More spending is more inflation pressure, and more inflation pressure is potentially more Fed hikes.
Layer onto that the deficit math. The U.S. federal deficit is on track to hit $2 trillion this year, the third-biggest in U.S. history behind only the two pandemic years. There is no pandemic this time. Debt just passed 100% of GDP, and interest payments alone now exceed $1 trillion a year.
For real estate, the practical read is the rate divergence is the story. Mortgages key off the 10-year more than the 30-year Treasury. That is why today's 30-year mortgage at 6.65% (down 2 bps) is consistent with the 10-year easing 12 bps over the week.
But if Dimon is right that the structural pressure is upward, then the buyer pool waiting for cuts is sitting through a window that may not widen. The 10-year at 4.54% on a session where the 30-year Treasury is at a 19-year high is the cleanest illustration of why "wait for cuts" is a riskier strategy than "lock the rate available today."
