Rates barely moved to finish the week. Mortgage News Daily had the 30-year at 7.20% and the 15-year at 6.83% Friday, still a touch under Wednesday's one year high of 7.24%. The 10-Year Treasury closed at 5.01%, back to its own high for the year.
Here is what the Fed's September projections mean for you, in plain terms. The Fed does not set mortgage rates, but it sets the tone. In June the typical Fed official expected the Fed's rate to be 3.8% at the end of this year. Now they expect 4.1%, which is about one more quarter point increase, and they expect it to come down more slowly after that. The reason sits in the same table: they see inflation at 3.7% this year.
For a buyer that means the plan of waiting for rates to drop just got a longer timeline.

The world had 65 active armed conflicts in 2025, the most since record keeping began in 1946, and 8 of them were one state fighting another, up from 2 in 2023. Thirteen counted as wars, the most since 1992.
This is not a foreign news item for a homeowner. It is an energy and shipping story with a price tag. Brent crude settled at $103.87 Friday and has spent this year above $100, which feeds fuel, freight and insurance costs into almost everything else. That is the same inflation the Fed says will run 3.7% this year, and the reason its rate path moved up instead of down. Conflict abroad, then higher costs, then a slower path to cheaper money here.
Gold and silver kept climbing on the same worry, with gold up 1.01% Friday to $4,424.90 and silver up 2.18% to $67.15.
