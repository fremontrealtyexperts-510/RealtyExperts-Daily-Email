Mortgage rates set another high for the year. Mortgage News Daily's 30-year rose to 7.50% on Monday, the highest since April 2024, and the 15-year reached 7.12%, while the 10-Year Treasury closed at 5.24%, its highest close since June 2007. The 30-year started September at 6.89%; on our five city median of $1,090,000 with 20% down, that climb adds about $360 a month.
Rental investors are stepping back. In a ResiClub and LendingOne survey of 216 single-family landlords taken August 17 to September 14, 65% expect the 30-year to average above 6.5% over the next year, up from 11% in late 2025, and 44% say they are unlikely to buy another rental in the next 12 months, the most in the survey's two years.
Insurance is part of it: 84% say rising premiums cut into their cash flow. The newsletter wrote that 59% plan 1% to 3% rent hikes; in the survey, 59% plan an increase of some size and 31% expect 1% to 3%.
Locally, the five city ledger dipped to 703 from 717 on Monday, with Milpitas down six, and the board wide table fell 35 to 2,727. For the record, UBS's annual bubble index, out last week, files San Francisco under low risk; Zurich and Tokyo are the only high risk cities of the 23 it tracks.
What this means for you: with fewer investors planning purchases, buyers who can carry today's payment may find less competition. Sellers should price for 7.5% money; 163 of the ledger's 617 homes on the market have been listed 60 days or longer.
• Board wide: 2,727 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 179 coming soon and 212 brand new.

• Fremont: 254 live, 197 active, 33 coming soon, 24 brand new. The median on market list price is $1,199,990 at $769 a square foot, and the typical active listing has been out 29 days.

Levels are Monday, September 28 at the closing bell. Tuesday is live: at 8:35 AM PT the S&P was 7,668.07 (-0.20%), the Dow 51,224.62 (-0.50%) and the Nasdaq 26,809.51 (-0.04%).
Stocks slipped Monday as the 10-Year climbed to its highest close since 2007, and Tuesday is a little softer so far. The day's two biggest company stories both start in the Bay Area.
Nvidia is the first chart. Its board approved another $150 billion for buybacks on Monday, which the company calls the largest authorization increase in history, lifting what it has left to spend to $235 billion through fiscal 2028. That tops Apple's $110 billion from 2024, the largest single U.S. authorization until now, and it is the Santa Clara company's third increase in 13 months. The stock rose 1.68% Monday to $228.86.
Anthropic is the second. Reuters reported Monday that the San Francisco company's draft IPO prospectus, filed confidentially and not yet public, shows a $42 billion net loss for 2025 on revenue that grew 12-fold to nearly $4.6 billion; about $34 billion of that loss was a non-cash accounting charge tied to its financing, and the operating loss was $8.06 billion, up from $2.98 billion in 2024. Reuters says the offering could value the company at more than $2 trillion, and Anthropic has committed at least $518 billion to computing capacity over about a decade.
Nvidia's $150 billion authorization is the largest single one on record, ahead of Apple's $110 billion in 2024. One bar per company; Apple alone has authorized $90 billion or more seven times.
