The 30 year fixed added 1 basis point to 6.77%, per Mortgage News Daily. Steady, and in line with Freddie Mac's latest survey at 6.65%.
⚠️ Please read this before quoting the 15 year. Mortgage News Daily shows the 15 year fixed at 6.61% today, a jump of 31 basis points in one session and a new 52 week high, while the 30 year moved a single basis point. That would leave only 16 basis points between the two, where the normal gap is closer to 50, and Freddie Mac's latest survey still has the 15 year at 5.95% and drifting down. We publish Mortgage News Daily because it is our source of record, but a move that size with nothing behind it looks like a data problem rather than a market one. Verify the 15 year with your lender before you put it in front of a client, and treat Monday's print as the confirmation.

• Board wide: 2,725 active, BOMK, PCH and new listings across the 15 city board, with 202 coming soon and 206 brand new today.
• Fremont: 250 active across all property types, made up of 35 townhomes, 87 condos and 128 detached homes, plus 96 in contract, 47 coming soon and 20 new today.
• Live ledger: our 5 city count eased to 726 listings. Fremont 287, Hayward 194, Milpitas 111, Newark 77 and Union City 57.
Two national datapoints landed today, and together they explain the standoff.
• Pending home sales fell 2.2% in July nationally, yet Virginia Beach saw demand jump 17.2%. Buyers have leverage in most markets and none at all in a few.
• A typical family now spends 34% of income on a new home mortgage, the highest share on record, with rates near 6.7%.
That second number is the whole story of this year. It is not that people stopped wanting to move. It is that the monthly payment now claims a third of household income, and at that level families rent longer, stay put longer, and remodel instead of trading up. It also means the day rates break lower, the response will be faster than anyone expects, because the wanting never went away.

Numbers as of Friday's close, August 21st. This edition is going out after the bell, so these are today's settled figures rather than yesterday's.
A green finish to the week. The S&P 500 rose 0.43% to 7,674, the Dow added 0.98% to 53,277 and the NASDAQ gained 0.43% to 26,180, recovering part of Thursday's broad decline.
Walmart was the week's cautionary tale. It beat profit expectations and the stock still fell 9.15% on Thursday, because sales grew at their slowest pace in six years and management guided profits lower. It had spent a $2.9 billion tariff refund cutting prices to win shoppers back, and inflation may make those discounts hard to sustain. The stock is down 6.9% for the year.
Two more worth noting. Anthropic is planning what could be the largest public stock sale ever at $86.2 billion, matching SpaceX, despite a $42 billion net loss last year on artificial intelligence infrastructure. And Alibaba's net income fell 75% for the same reason, heavy AI spending. The market is rewarding that spending in some names and punishing it in others, which is usually what happens late in a build out.
Walmart started the year at $111.41 and closed Friday at $103.70, down 6.9% for 2026. The drop at the right edge is the 9.15% fall on August 20 after earnings showed the slowest sales growth in six years.

Ten year yield is the Treasury close for August 21st. Brent is the August 20th settlement, the last one the pricing script treats as settled. Gold and silver are spot quotes on the morning of August 21st.
A correction to this morning's newsletter first. It reported initial jobless claims at around 200,000. The Labor Department's release for the week ending August 15 puts them at 206,000, down 6,000 from a revised 212,000. The direction was right and the level was low. For context, the same week a year ago produced 233,000 claims.
What that number means for us: employers are holding onto the people they have but are not rushing to hire. Layoffs stay rare, which supports household income and keeps people current on mortgages. It also means anyone actively looking for work is having a harder time, and that shows up in relocation timing more than in defaults.
The 10 year rose 5 basis points to 4.74% and the 30 year is back to 5.27%. Gold and silver both ran hard, gold up 2.57% to $4,587 and silver up 4.12% to $69.61, with the Middle East still the driver. Brent settled at $93.78, up 2.36%. The newsletter printed $93.20 and a 1.72% gain, both a little light.
One policy item worth a bookmark: Treasury proposed limiting Trump Accounts, the tax advantaged accounts for Americans 18 and under, to low fee index funds. Small fees compound over decades in the same direction as returns do, just against you.
