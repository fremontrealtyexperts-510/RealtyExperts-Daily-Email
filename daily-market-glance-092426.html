Rates climbed for a second straight day. Mortgage News Daily has the 30-year at 7.37% as of today, up 0.11, and the 15-year at 7.00%, up 0.13. Both are new 52 week highs, topping yesterday's. The newsletter printed 7.26% and 6.87%, which were Wednesday's readings.
The bond market is the reason (chart above). The 10-Year closed Wednesday at 5.11%, up 0.15 in a single session and its highest close since July 2007. It has climbed 114 basis points from February's 3.97% low, and it was at 5.16% at 11:49 AM PT today, so the pressure has not let up.
Here is what that means on a real Fremont home. At today's median on market list price of $1,238,944, with 20% down, the loan is $991,155, which is still high balance conforming here, not jumbo. Principal and interest is $6,842 a month at 7.37%, against $5,936 at 5.99%, the 52 week low. That is $906 more every month for the same house.
• Board wide: 2,740 listings across our 15 city table, counting active and in contract together, with 192 coming soon and 179 brand new.

Buyers: get a written quote this week and compare the 30-year FHA at 6.98% and a 7/6 ARM at 6.80% against the 7.37% fixed, because the gap now buys real payment room. Sellers: the same house now costs a buyer about $900 more a month than it did at the 52 week low, so price to this week's rate, not to spring's.

All four are Wednesday, September 23. The 10-Year is the Treasury daily yield curve close, gold and silver are the COMEX front month settlement against Tuesday's settlement, and Brent is the ICE front month (November contract) settlement.
Beef is the chart today. The Bureau of Labor Statistics has 100% ground beef at a record $6.92 a pound this August, up 55% from $4.47 in August 2021. In the West, the closest region BLS publishes to us, it is $7.44, up 53%. The newsletter said beef prices have roughly doubled in five years; the government's own numbers say closer to half again. The version of this chart going around showed $6.88, which was July; August set the record.
The 10-Year closed at 5.11%, up 0.15 on the day and the highest since July 2007. The newsletter printed 5.12% and called it up 0.01; the Treasury curve says 5.11% and up 0.15. It credits strong economic data, a weakly received government bond sale and Fed talk of more hikes.
Gold settled down 1.33% at $4,318.40 and silver down 2.35% at $64.96 as yields rose. Brent went the other way, settling at $103.08, up 3.86%. The newsletter printed Brent at $91.85; the front month settlement is $103.08, a gap too large to be rounding, so we go with the exchange.
Ground beef hit a record $6.92 a pound nationally this August, up 55% in five years. The West, the closest region BLS publishes to the Bay Area, is at $7.44.
