Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.57% Even As Factory Data Heats Up; East Bay Inventory Slips To 2,909 Active On A Quieter 255-New Tuesday
The 30-year fixed eased two basis points to 6.57%, with the 15-year at 6.10%. It is small relief, and the macro backdrop is pulling the other way: strong factory data and a fresh oil spike (see Economy) keep upward pressure on the inflation story that drives rates. The buyer waiting on the sidelines for a dramatic drop may be waiting a while.
Locally, the East Bay board sits at 2,909 active, down a hair from Monday's 2,915, on 255 new listings and 217 closed sales. That is the Tuesday cool-down after the weekend backlog cleared on Monday: new supply pulled back across most cities while buyers kept right on closing.
Two national notes worth passing along. Berkshire Hathaway's new CEO Greg Abel made his first big move, a $6.8 billion deal for homebuilder Taylor Morrison (350+ communities across 12 states), signaling that Buffett's successor is willing to spend the $397 billion cash pile and that he likes housing. Separately, warehouse landlords are regaining the upper hand as supply tightens; the share of landlord-friendly markets in the Americas could jump from 17% to 46% by 2029.

The 1990s called and they want their stock gains back. Seven dot-com-era names, including Dell (DELL +10.45%), Micron (MU +6.56%), and Intel (INTC -4.67%), have piled on a combined $1.7 trillion in value this year, up an average of 158%, for the simple reason that AI runs on the servers, memory, and networking gear these are among the only companies still making.
The software side caught the updraft too: ServiceNow (NOW +9.21%) and Adobe (ADBE +5.72%) rallied on Nvidia's (NVDA +6.26%) new chip launch, the logic being that cheaper, faster hardware lets more companies afford their AI tools. The catch is that next quarter's earnings actually have to show the AI revenue to justify it.

America's factories are running hot. The ISM manufacturing index hit 54 in May, its best reading since 2022 and a fifth straight month in growth territory, as AI spending drives demand for electrical equipment and new tax rules make it cheaper to build. The one wrinkle: pricier oil and firmer inflation suggest some of that activity is buyers stocking up before costs climb further.
And costs may climb. Iran walked out of talks over Israeli strikes in Lebanon, sending Brent crude up 4.2%, its biggest one-day jump in a month, to $95.32. Tehran is now threatening the Bab el-Mandeb strait on top of Hormuz; close both and tankers add 15 days routing around Africa. The 10-year Treasury held at 4.46% and gold firmed to $4,513.
