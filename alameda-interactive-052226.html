Rates Ease A Second Day To 6.65%; April Contract Signings Up 4.5% (Best Spring In 3 Years); 30-Year Treasury Hits 19-Year High (5.2%)

Three threads to carry into client conversations today.

First, rates eased a second day. The 30-year fixed is at 6.65%, down 2 bps from yesterday’s 6.67% and 10 bps below Wednesday’s 6.75% peak. The 15-year sits at 6.23%, essentially flat.

The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.54%, down another basis point and now 12 bps below Monday’s 4.66%. The two-day rate retreat is the cleanest run of relief we have seen since early May.

But the long end of the curve is moving the other way. The 30-year U.S. Treasury yield hit 5.2% this week, the highest reading since 2007. Mortgages key off the 10-year, so today’s relief is real, but the structural pressure on rates is still upward. The window where 6.65% is available is not a permanent floor.

Second, the demand side has woken up. April U.S. contract signings (deals signed but not yet closed) jumped 4.5%, the strongest reading in three years.

The Midwest led, with Kansas City up 20.7% year-to-date. List prices fell 2.4% nationally, but the share of listings with price cuts dropped too. Buyers are absorbing supply rather than waiting for further markdowns.

Translation: the buyer pool nationally is not waiting for cuts. They are running the math at the rate available and signing.

Third, the Dimon and deficit overlay matters. Dimon’s call that rates may rise again, combined with a $2 trillion federal deficit and the 30-year Treasury at a 19-year high, says the macro setup for a sustained rate decline is not in place.

Buyers who locked Thursday or Friday are paying ~10 bps less than buyers who locked Tuesday. On a $1M loan, that is roughly $65 a month or $23K over the life of the loan, just for moving the timing two days. That is why timing-the-rate is harder than locking-and-moving in this tape.

Indices Green Thursday; US Gov $2B Equity Stake In 9 Quantum Names; Walmart -7.72% On Earnings Miss

All three indices closed Thursday in the green: S&P 7,445 (+0.17%), DOW 50,285 (+0.55%), NASDAQ 26,293 (+0.09%).

The headline almost no one saw coming was a federal-policy intervention. The U.S. government is writing a $2 billion check to nine quantum computing companies and taking an equity stake in each. IBM (IBM +12.48%) gets $1B. D-Wave (QBTS +33.37%), Rigetti (RGTI +30.57%), and Infleqtion (INFG +31.48%) each get $100M.

Quantum computing stocks rebounded from a 40-50% Q1 drawdown to finish May 2026 at IONQ +32%, QBTS +28%, RGTI +8% YTD. Source: Google Finance.

IonQ (IONQ +12.24%) and Quantum Computing (QUBT +19.35%) also powered up on the news. The chart above tells the YTD arc: quantum names spent Q1 in a 40-to-50% drawdown, then snapped back through April and May. After Thursday’s announcement, YTD readings are IONQ +32%, QBTS +28%, RGTI +8%. That is a federal-policy-driven equity bounce, not a fundamentals bounce, but it is the cleanest signal yet that the government considers quantum a strategic-tech sector worth direct capital allocation.

The other side: Walmart (WMT -7.72%) missed earnings and cut its full-year outlook. The detail worth carrying: high gas prices pushed the average Walmart fill-up below 10 gallons for the first time since 2022. Lower-income shoppers are buying less overall. The K-shaped consumer pattern is widening: premium and policy-driven sectors win; mass-market retail bleeds.

10-Year At 4.54% (-12 Bps From Monday); WTI Slides To $95.60; Dimon Warns Rates May Rise; 30-Year Treasury At 5.2% (19-Year High); Deficit On Track For $2T

The 10-year ticked down again to 4.54% Thursday, off 12 basis points from Monday’s 4.66%. WTI Crude is extending the slide intraday at $95.60 (-0.78%), now nearly $6 below where it sat 48 hours ago and the deepest sub-$100 print since early May. Gold pulled back to $4,531 (-0.24%). On the surface the disinflation trade is winning.

But the bond market is flashing a contradictory signal underneath. The 30-year U.S. Treasury yield recently hit 5.2%, a 19-year high (since 2007). The long end is telling a different story than the belly of the curve.

Jamie Dimon (JPM +0.34%) tied it together: the JPMorgan CEO said U.S. interest rates may actually start to rise again. His logic: pandemic savings are getting spent down, heavy federal spending plus the AI buildout could keep growth resilient, more spending = more inflation pressure = more Fed hikes.

Layer onto that the deficit math. The U.S. federal deficit is on track to hit $2 trillion this year, the third-biggest in U.S. history behind only the two pandemic years. Debt just passed 100% of GDP, and interest payments alone now exceed $1 trillion a year.

For real estate: mortgages key off the 10-year, so today’s 30-year mortgage at 6.65% (down 2 bps) is consistent with the 10-year easing 12 bps over the week. But if Dimon is right, the structural pressure on rates is upward. “Wait for cuts” is a riskier strategy than “lock the rate available today.”

Crypto Tight Bid Thursday; SpaceX IPO Filing Reveals 18,712 BTC ($1.45B); Blockchain.com Files To Go Public — First Crypto IPO In Months

Crypto held a tight bid Thursday: BTC $77,615 (+0.09%), ETH $2,135 (+0.18%), XRP $1.37 (+0.17%).

The structural story is the SpaceX IPO filing. SpaceX disclosed it holds 18,712 BTC worth roughly $1.45 billion. That makes SpaceX the seventh-largest known corporate Bitcoin holder, ahead of Coinbase (COIN +1.19%). SpaceX is slated to IPO June 11, 2026, on Nasdaq under ticker SPCX. A $1T+ private company holding a billion-plus in BTC and going public on a fixed near-term date adds a major institutional anchor.

The other datapoint is Blockchain.com filing for an IPO yesterday. Notable timing because Kraken and Ledger paused their listings earlier this year. Blockchain.com’s move is the first major crypto-name IPO filing in months. If Blockchain.com and SpaceX both price clean in June, that opens the gate for the broader crypto-services pipeline.
