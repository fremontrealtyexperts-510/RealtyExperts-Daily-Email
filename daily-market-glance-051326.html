30-Year Hits 6.57% After CPI Hits 3.8%; Fed-Cut Narrative For Summer Is Dead
Three quick threads to anchor client conversations this week.
First, the rate march continues. 30-year mortgages at 6.57% today, 15-year at 6.07%. That is another tick higher after yesterday's 10-bp jump, and now sits +15 bps from Monday morning's 6.42%. The CPI print sealed it. Inflation at 3.8% gives the Fed zero room to cut. Any prospect who told you "I'm waiting for rates to drop before I buy" needs to hear that the trend has reversed. The longer they wait, the more the math gets worse, not better.
Second, the Dubai parallel is worth keeping in mind. One in ten Dubai home sellers has cut their asking price since the Iran war began, with some cuts hitting 50%. Dubai's top-of-market is booming, everyday sellers are slashing. The lesson: in an oil-driven inflation regime, real estate splits by tier faster than usual. Luxury holds. Mid-market gets squeezed. The Bay Area's price-tier distribution should perform similarly. Listings priced just above the qualified-buyer DTI ceiling are going to feel pressure first.
Third, the lower end of the new-car market just disappeared ($20,550 now the cheapest new car, no more sub-$20K options). That is a small data point, but it is the same affordability dynamic playing out in housing's entry-level segment. The buyer who could stretch to a $750K Hayward townhouse two years ago at 4% rates is now looking at the same payment at $625K. They are not buying. Sellers in that tier need to know.

April CPI year-over-year, BLS release May 12, 2026. Headline at 3.8% (hottest since 2023), Core at 2.8%.
The big macro event: April CPI just printed 3.8% year-over-year, the hottest since May 2023, up from 3.3% in March. Core CPI (less food and energy) sits at 2.8%. The chart above shows the dramatic uptick on the right side, a full point higher than where it was hovering just months ago.
The driver is Iran-oil bleeding into pump prices. Gas is up 28.4% since February, and beef and flights also jumped. This is the print that explains everything we've been tracking all week. Mortgage rates marched from 6.42% Monday to 6.57% today (+15 bps in three sessions). The 10-year went from 4.39% to 4.46%. The ECB pivoted from cuts to hikes. The Fed is now boxed in. With CPI re-accelerating, the rate-cut narrative for summer 2026 is officially dead, and any buyer or seller who was waiting for cuts to materialize needs to recalibrate.
WTI pulled back to $100.97 (-1.18%) intraday but Brent kept climbing to $107.49 (+3.15%). The energy premium is not going anywhere yet.
One more signal worth flagging: banks AND major labor unions are both lobbying the Senate to kill the CLARITY Act. Unusual bipartisan opposition. Unions cite pension/401k crypto risk; banks cite stablecoin deposit flight. The bill's path is now less certain than it looked Friday.
