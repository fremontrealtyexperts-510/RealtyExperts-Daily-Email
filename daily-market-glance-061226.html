Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.60% Even As Inflation Runs Hot; Existing-Home Sales Hit A Five-Month High As The East Bay Board Climbs To 2,915 Active On 218 New And 212 Closed
The 30-year fixed eased to 6.60% and the 15-year to 6.15% — a modest step down from yesterday, and a real improvement from 6.84% a year ago. The bigger surprise: even with inflation reaccelerating (May CPI hit 4.2%, a three-year high), existing-home sales just climbed to a five-month high. The read-through is that a stronger job market is giving buyers the nerve to stop waiting on rates.
That theme is showing up locally, too. The East Bay board climbed to 2,915 active, up 31 from Thursday, as 218 new listings outpaced 212 closings — more came on than cleared, which nudged inventory higher. Oakland again led the board (769 active, 65 new), while Fremont logged 15 new on 360 active.

Stocks roared back. The S&P 500 jumped 1.75%, the DOW 1.86%, and the NASDAQ 2.54%, clawing back most of Wednesday's inflation-driven selloff as easing Middle East tensions and IPO fever revived risk appetite. The headline event is today: SpaceX's long-awaited IPO is expected to raise roughly $75 billion at a $135 target price (555 million shares) — a deal big enough that it could make Elon Musk the world's first trillionaire.
Not every name joined the party. Oracle (ORCL -8.53%) had its worst day in over a year — even after beating on both sales and profit — as investors balked at the cash math behind its AI push: the company burned $23.7 billion last year and needs roughly $40 billion more. The worry is that those big AI bets only pay off if they convert into profit. Tesla (TSLA +4.60%) rode the SpaceX halo higher.

📊 Looking Dull. Gold has tumbled to roughly a six-month low — down about 6.3% on the week, to near $4,186 an ounce — even though inflation is running at a three-year high. That is backwards from gold's usual playbook (it tends to rise when inflation is hot), and it comes down to interest rates: after May CPI reaccelerated to 4.2%, traders now bet the Fed could actually HIKE by December, and gold hates higher rates. The chart below traces the slide from January's ~$5,400 peak.
Silver went the other way, spiking toward $68 an ounce (+6.9%) as Middle East supply jitters and bargain-hunting lit a fire under the more industrial metal — a sharp split from gold on the same day.
Brent crude eased to $89.30 (-1.18%) after President Trump said the U.S. is pausing strikes on Iran and expects a deal within days. And in a sign the wealth effect is cooling, U.S. household net worth rose just $100 billion last quarter to a record $183 trillion — the smallest gain in a year, though rising home values and savings bridged some of the gap.
Gold's 2026 round trip: after spiking to about $5,400 in late January, it has ground lower all year and tumbled ~6.3% this week to a roughly six-month low near $4,186 — even with inflation at a three-year high — as traders price in a possible Fed rate hike.

Bitcoin held at $63,401 (+0.26%), with Ether easing to $1,669 (-0.18%) and XRP flat at $1.14. The bigger story is crypto going mainstream: Japan passed a bill to regulate crypto under its stock-market law — lower taxes for holders and a path to crypto ETFs, but paired with insider-trading bans and up to 10 years in prison for unlicensed firms. Japan has about 14 million crypto accounts, mostly everyday earners.
Institutional money kept flowing in. Digital Asset, maker of the Canton Network, raised $355 million led by a16z crypto, with Citadel, HSBC, Apollo, and Abu Dhabi's wealth fund also backing it. Canton lets big banks trade bonds and funds on-chain while keeping the deals private. For Bay Area buyers and sellers the tie-in is indirect, but big swings in crypto and IPO money still shape the risk appetite of a slice of the move-up market.
