Today's Market Briefs argues that AI is pushing good jobs back toward the trades, and it opens with a line worth testing here: for decades, plumbers and electricians could buy a home and raise a family. In our five cities, one median paycheck no longer does it, whether it comes from a job site or a desk.
Our five city median list price is $1,077,000. With 20% down at 7.33%, that is about $7,160 a month with taxes and insurance, and the California Association of REALTORS® affordability method (that payment at 30% of gross income) puts the income needed at about $286,400. In the San Francisco, Oakland and Fremont metro, the median electrician earns $92,830, a carpenter $89,200 and a software developer $186,640, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
Turned around, an electrician's median pay qualifies for about $349,000 of house, where 7 of our 616 on market listings sit, all condos. Two electricians together reach about $698,000, where 126 sit, mostly condos and townhomes, and HOA dues would trim that further.
Mortgage News Daily's 30-year average ended the week at 7.33% and the 15-year at 6.98%; it does not update on weekends. The Fed meets Tuesday and Wednesday, its first meeting since raising its rate in September.
Locally, inventory eased. The board wide table slipped to 2,631 from 2,640, with 139 coming soon and 201 brand new. The five city ledger slipped to 686 from 688: Fremont to 227 from 230, while Milpitas rose to 103 from 102, and Hayward (198), Newark (91) and Union City (67) held steady.
What this means for you: around here, buying is usually a two income project, and even two trade paychecks land in condo and townhome territory at today's rates. If you are buying, consider a townhome or condo as the first step and ask about seller credits, since about one in three local sellers cut their price last month. If you are selling, your likely buyer is a two income household stretching at 7.33%, so price for that payment from day one.

Levels are Friday, October 23: the 10-Year is Treasury's close, gold and silver are COMEX December settlements, and Brent is the ICE December settlement. Markets were closed this weekend.
Today's chart is the government's own ten year jobs forecast. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects 5.9 million more jobs from 2025 to 2035, with the two health care groups adding almost a third of them. Office and administrative support sheds the most, 752,100 jobs or 4.0%, which BLS ties partly to automation tools, including AI.
The skilled trades come out ahead: construction and repair jobs together add 738,400, about as many as office support loses. Computer jobs are still projected to grow 7.3%, about twice the overall pace, so the squeeze lands on routine office work more than on tech.
On the cards, Friday's closes stand into Fed week: Brent up 6.2% for the week, the 10-Year at 5.00%, down 0.09 point for the week, and gold just $7.90 above $4,000.
