Mortgage Rates Ease To 6.55%; The ROAD To Housing Act Stalls At The President's Desk; The East Bay Board Climbs To 2,941 Active On 177 New And 206 Closed
The 30-year fixed eased to 6.55% and the 15-year to 6.15%, a touch lower than last week as Treasury yields settled. That is still well above the rate most homeowners carry, so the lock-in effect that is keeping resale supply tight is not going anywhere.
The housing headline is a reversal. We told you yesterday that Congress passed the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, one of the biggest housing reform bills in years. It reached the President's desk, but he canceled the signing an hour beforehand and says he will not sign until lawmakers pass a separate voter ID bill. For now the bill, which would fund new supply, back manufactured homes, cap big investors, and speed up permits, is in limbo.
A warning sign on the commercial side: a prominent Chicago office tower, One South Wacker, just defaulted on its $343 million loan. The building is 73% occupied and was renovated in 2020, yet it could not refinance, and with citywide office vacancy at 27%, weaker towers may be next. It is a reminder that the pressure in commercial real estate has not let up.
Locally, the East Bay board climbed to 2,941 active (up 46 from yesterday), with 177 new listings against 206 closings. Oakland again led the board with 44 new on 782 active; Fremont added 15 new on 350 active.

For agents: rates dipped slightly but the lock-in effect is still firmly in place, so the opportunity is in the fresh inventory hitting the market (2,941 active, 177 new). Well-priced, well-presented homes still sell inside about four weeks. Coach sellers that they are competing on price and presentation against thin supply, and remind buyers that a softer commodity backdrop and steady yields make this a reasonable window to lock a rate and negotiate.

Stocks were mixed and little changed. The S&P 500 eased 0.10% to 7,358 and the NASDAQ slipped 0.43% to 25,476, while the DOW edged up 0.35% to 51,848 (figures as of the June 24th close).
The day belonged to memory chips. Micron (MU -0.31%) blew past sales expectations and guided higher, saying AI companies are buying memory faster than it can make it. The stock is up more than 200% in 2026. Right behind it, South Korea's SK Hynix filed to go public in the U.S. and plans to raise about $29 billion, which would rank among the largest IPOs ever.
Two more moves stood out. Germany scrapped its biggest warship order since World War II, worth about $14.5 billion, sending European defense maker Rheinmetall lower and steering the work to rival TKMS. On this side of the Atlantic, U.S. defense names like Lockheed Martin (LMT -2.39%) and Northrop Grumman (NOC -1.99%) have been sliding since the U.S. and Iran ended their war.

10-Year and Copper as of June 24th (Market Briefs); gold and silver are live spot, June 25th (Fortune); Brent crude is the June 24th print.
Copper is running out of steam. After a strong run toward $6.65 a pound through late spring, the metal has slid back to about $6.02, up just 0.55% on the year (see the chart below). Three forces are capping it: a strong dollar makes copper pricier for overseas buyers, the Fed's higher for longer stance makes raw materials less appealing, and global supply is at its highest level since 2003.
The precious metals were mixed. Gold rebounded above $4,000 to about $4,005 (+0.43%) as the dollar eased and the latest inflation reading came in roughly as expected, while silver slipped to $57.76 (-1.74%) and is now well off its January highs. Both remain pressured by the same higher for longer Fed bet that lifted the dollar this week.
Oil kept drifting lower. Brent crude eased 1.34% to $72.75 now that the Strait of Hormuz has reopened, but pump prices have not followed. Regular gas is averaging about $3.91 a gallon, down from roughly $4.50 a month ago, and the White House has asked the Justice Department to investigate whether oil companies are keeping prices high even as crude costs fall. The 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.41%.
The takeaway for housing: cooling commodities and a steady 10-year are mildly encouraging, but with the Fed still leaning toward higher for longer, mortgage rates are not getting major relief yet.

Crypto steadied after a rough stretch. Bitcoin firmed 0.30% to $61,183, Ether added 0.66% to $1,630, and XRP slipped 0.30% to $1.07 (figures as of the June 24th close).
The bigger picture is still soft. Bitcoin spent Wednesday dancing around $60,000, its lowest level since October 2024 and the third time it has dipped under that mark this year, capping an eight-month slump. One steadying factor: big holders now own more of the coin than ever, which can make further dips slower.
Regulators are racing to keep up. Hong Kong's crypto ETFs have nearly doubled to about $550 million since launching last year and platform trading is up 125%, so officials are writing four new rulebooks covering how firms trade, hold, advise on, and manage crypto.
