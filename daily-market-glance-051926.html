Rates Push To 6.68% As 10-Year Spikes 23 Bps Overnight; NAHB Builder Index Jumps 3 Points To 37 In Biggest Single-Month Move Since September; New-Construction Pricing Edge Worth $25K
Three threads to carry into client conversations today.
First, rates pushed up again. The 30-year is at 6.68%, up 3 bps from Monday's 6.65%. The 15-year is at 6.12%, up 2 bps. The proximate driver is the 10-year Treasury, which jumped from 4.62% on Sunday to 4.85% this morning, a 23-bp move on the long bond in roughly 24 hours of trading. That is a real repricing, not noise. Five trading days ago the 30-year was at 6.42%. We are now 26 bps higher than that. Anyone telling clients to wait for the Monday print or the Wednesday print is now late on the trade.
Second, the NAHB Housing Market Index just put up its biggest single-month jump since last September. Builder confidence rose to 37 in May, up 3 points from April's 34 (chart above). Anything below 50 still means more builders see conditions as bad than good, so this is not a celebration: it is a builders-are-less-pessimistic story. But the direction matters. The chart shows the index was sitting in the 32-33 zone all summer, then made a step-jump to the high-30s in October when inventory started rebuilding, dipped to 34 in April when the rate-and-tariff news cycle was at its worst, and has now snapped back to 37. Builders are reading the supply backdrop the same way we are: the buyers who are out there are out there, and waiting is not the trade.
Third, the related Realtor.com data point: new-construction homes are now saving buyers over $25,000 over the first 10 years of ownership versus comparable resale stock. Lower maintenance, lower insurance because of newer systems, and lower utility bills because of current energy code. That is a real number to put in front of clients who are anchored on "resale is always cheaper." The math has changed, and the Bay Area new-build pipeline (Fremont's mid-tier townhome product, Dublin's continued build-out, San Ramon's planned communities) is exactly where this argument lands hardest.

For listing agents: the case for moving this week is now mechanical. 30-year is up 26 bps in five trading sessions. The 10-year jumped 23 bps overnight. Builders just printed their biggest sentiment jump since September. The supply story is on your side and the macro story is closing the rate-cut path. Three numbers, one message: list now, price to today, market to closers.

The 10-year is the headline of the macro tape this morning. The yield is sitting at 4.85%, essentially unchanged on the day but up sharply from Sunday's 4.62%. That is a 23-basis-point move in 24 hours of trading, the bond market voting again that inflation persistence and the fading rate-cut path are the dominant narrative right now. Gold ticked up 0.20% to $4,570, WTI Crude is at $103.78 (-0.57% intraday) after Friday's run, and Brent is at $109.72.
The story to watch this week is Meta. The company is laying off about 8,000 workers, roughly 10% of its staff, and scrapping another 6,000 open roles. The reason is unambiguous: Meta plans to spend $145B this year, with most of it going to AI. This is the cleanest signal yet that Big Tech is willing to convert headcount to AI capex even when the immediate productivity story is unproven. For market watchers and clients who hold tech-heavy portfolios, the read is that the AI investment cycle is shifting from "in addition to" the workforce to "in place of" the workforce, and the cost discipline is starting to bite.
For real estate, the macro context that matters is the 10-year touching 4.85%. That is the highest mortgage-relevant Treasury reading since the spring move began. The Mortgage News Daily 30-year quote is following with a 3-bp pop to 6.68%. The rate ceiling is not done lifting.

Crypto continues to drift lower with the broader risk-off tone. BTC is at $76,995 (-0.57%), ETH at $2,126 (-0.16%), and XRP at $1.39 (-0.94%). Bitcoin is now roughly 5% off the Thursday high and trading inside a tight range as the 10-year repricing pulls liquidity out of risk assets.
Two stories worth flagging today. First, Iran just launched Hormuz Safe, a Bitcoin-paid insurance service for ships crossing the Strait of Hormuz, covering checks, holds, and seizures (but not war damage). Tehran is projecting more than $10B of revenue from the program. The U.S., China, and the UN have all said no one should pay to cross Hormuz, so expect this to become a sanctions-policy flash point. For clients who follow geopolitics, this is the first overt sovereign experiment with using Bitcoin to route around dollar payment rails. It is a meaningful structural data point regardless of how the actual cash flows pan out.
Second, Bitcoin Depot (BTM -73.24%), the operator of more than 9,000 Bitcoin ATMs across the U.S., Canada, and Australia, filed for bankruptcy and shut down its entire ATM network yesterday. The CEO blamed tighter regulations. The on-ramp side of retail crypto is consolidating fast, and the public-equity version of that thesis (BTM down 73% in a single session) is a cautionary tale for anyone weighing crypto-adjacent equities.
